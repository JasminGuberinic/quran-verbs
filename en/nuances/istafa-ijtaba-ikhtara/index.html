Nuances

To choose

اصْطَفَى · اجْتَبَى · اخْتَارَ

What they share

All three verbs mean to choose. ar-Rāġib tells them apart by what is taken: iṣṭifāʾ is taking the pure part (ṣafw), iḫtiyār taking the best (ḫayr), and iǧtibāʾ gathering in (like water into a basin).

How to tell them apart

To choose the purest — prophets, Mary, messengers → اصْطَفَى; to single out and shower with unearned favour, and guide → اجْتَبَى; to choose among options — Allah, and also a man → اخْتَارَ.

اصْطَفَى to choose (the purest)

12× in the Quran · Verb page →

To choose the purest, most excellent part. Allah chooses the prophets, their families, Mary, and messengers from angels and from people.

اجْتَبَى to choose and draw near

10× in the Quran · Verb page →

To single out and shower with favour — according to ar-Rāġib, an abundance of blessing the servant did not earn by striving, given to prophets and some close to them in rank. With guidance (هَدَى) for Adam (20:122, with pardon) and Abraham (16:121); Jonah is made one of the righteous (68:50), and Joseph is taught the interpretation of events (12:6).

اخْتَارَ to choose, to select

4× in the Quran · Verb page →

To pick what is better among options. Said of Allah, and also of a man: Moses chose seventy men.
