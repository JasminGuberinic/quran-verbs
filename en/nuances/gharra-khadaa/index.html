Nuances

Deluding and deceiving

غَرَّ · خَدَعَ · خَادَعَ

What they share

All three words speak of a person being led into what is wrong. They differ in the means: a delusion that catches a person off guard and carries him away with something attractive (this world, vain wishes), or a deceit in which someone shows one thing and hides another.

How to tell them apart

Deluding, carrying a person away while he is off guard — mostly this world, wishes or the Deluder → غَرَّ; deceiving, showing one thing and hiding another → خَدَعَ; trying to deceive — the hypocrites towards Allah and the believers → خَادَعَ.

غَرَّ to delude

15× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ‘ġarartuhu’ means catching someone in his ġirra — heedlessness while awake — and getting from him what you want; he explains al-ġarūr as everything that deludes a person: wealth, rank, desire, Satan. In the Quran it is mostly this world, vain wishes and the Deluder that delude; the weight lies on the person's heedlessness, though ar-Rāġib also cites deluding speech (6:112).

خَدَعَ to deceive

2× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, ḫidāʿ is bringing another down from what he is set on by showing him one thing while hiding another. Set beside his ġurūr, the weight here is on the deceiver's hidden aim, while in ġurūr it is on the victim's heedlessness.

خَادَعَ to try to deceive

2× in the Quran

In the Quran only twice, both times of the hypocrites who ‘deceive Allah’. Ar-Rāġib says the Messenger and the believers are meant, and that ‘Allah’ is said to show how ugly their act is and how great the Messenger is. Many mufassirun explain this form here as an attempt.
