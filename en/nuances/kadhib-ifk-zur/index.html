Nuances

Falsehood

كَذِب · إِفْك · زُور

What they share

All three words mean a lie, an untruth; they differ in the image each root carries.

How to tell them apart

A statement contrary to the truth → كَذِب; untruth that turns a thing from its proper face, grave slander → إِفْك; untruth that veers off course, false witness → زُور.

كَذِب lie, falsehood

33× in the Quran · Noun page →

The general word for a lie: a statement not matching how a thing is — the opposite of truthfulness (صِدْق).

إِفْك slander, fabrication

9× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, anything turned from the face it ought to have; a lie is so called because it is turned about. From the same root comes أَنَّى يُؤْفَكُونَ — “how they are turned away” (5:75).

زُور falsehood, false witness

4× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, a lie is called zūr because it inclines away from its course (from a root meaning slant). In the Quran it comes with speech and witness.
