Nuances

Wanting and willing

أَرَادَ · شَاءَ

What they share

Both words mean to want or will. ar-Rāġib reports that most theologians take them as the same, while some tell them apart: Allah's will (مَشِيئَة) means the thing comes to be, whereas His إِرَادَة does not necessarily bring about what is willed.

How to tell them apart

To want, to intend — the leaning and the decision that a thing be done; of Allah also what He wants for people (2:185) → أَرَادَ; to will — Allah's will, and man's, which stands under it (76:30) → شَاءَ.

أَرَادَ to want, to intend

139× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the soul's leaning towards a thing together with the judgement that it should be done; said of Allah it means only the decision, for He is above leaning. It can mean intent (28:83) and what Allah wants of people (2:185), and, since will can follow a compelled force and not only choice, is said even of a wall (18:77).

شَاءَ to will

236× in the Quran · Verb page →

Will. The Quran often uses it of Allah's will and in ‘if Allah wills’; man too ‘wills’, but, by 76:30, only what Allah wills. Some scholars, as ar-Rāġib reports, say that Allah's will (مَشِيئَة) is itself the bringing of a thing into being, so that what He wills, is.
