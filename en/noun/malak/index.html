What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    ٱلْمَلَكُ

    the angel (melek) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الْمَلَكَ

    the angel (meleka) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ الْمَلَكِ

    from the angel (od meleka) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    عَلَى الْمَلَكِ

    on the angel (na meleku) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    اسْمُ الْمَلَكِ

    the name of the angel (ime meleka) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    مَلَكُهُ

    his/its angel (njegov melek) · Bosnian nominative

Practice sentences

  • subject (nominative)

    الْمَلَكُ لَا يَأْكُلُ

    An angel does not eat.

  • object (accusative)

    ذَكَرَ الْمُعَلِّمُ مَلَكًا فِي الدَّرْسِ

    The teacher mentioned an angel in the lesson.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    قَرَأْتُ قِصَّةً عَنِ الْمَلَائِكَةِ

    I read a story about the angels.

  • possessor (genitive)

    اسْمُ الْمَلَكِ جَمِيلٌ

    The angel's name is beautiful.
