Nuances

Disliking and refusing

كَرِهَ · أَبَى

What they share

Both words express a ‘no’ to something. They differ in whether it is a feeling within or a firm refusal.

How to tell them apart

To feel aversion to something — by nature, or by reason and the Law; an inner state → كَرِهَ; to refuse vehemently, not to consent — every ibāʾ is a refusal, but not every refusal is ibāʾ → أَبَى.

كَرِهَ to dislike, to hate

17× in the Quran · Verb page →

ar-Rāġib reports one view that karh and kurh are the same, and another that distinguishes hardship imposed from outside (karh) from the aversion a person feels within (kurh); this second he divides in two: by nature, and by reason or the Law. So, he says, one may say of the same thing that he wants it and dislikes it. The verb karihtu, he says, is used of both.

أَبَى to refuse

13× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ibāʾ is intense holding back: every ibāʾ is a refusal, but not every refusal is ibāʾ. In the Quran people so refuse, consenting to nothing but disbelief, and Allah, as befits Him, ‘refuses’ all but to perfect His light.
