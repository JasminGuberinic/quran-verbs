Nuances

Terror, alarm, fright

رُعْب · فَزَع · رَوْع

What they share

All three words mean a fear that seizes a person. Going by ar-Rāġib's definitions, they differ in the image they carry: fear that fills and cuts off, a shrinking back from the frightening, and fear cast into the inner self.

How to tell them apart

Fear that fills the heart and cuts off, in the Quran mostly cast into the hearts of enemies → رُعْب; a shrinking and recoiling before the frightening, in the Quran the alarm of the Last Day → فَزَع; fear cast into the inner self, which passes (Abraham) → رَوْع.

رُعْب terror

5× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ruʿb is being cut off by a fear one is filled with; so the same root says ‘to fill a basin’ and ‘to cut off’. In the Quran Allah casts it four times into the hearts of those fighting the believers, and the fifth time it would fill whoever looked on the people of the cave.

فَزَع alarm

2× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, fazaʿ is the shrinking and recoil that seizes a person before something frightening, akin to agitation (jazaʿ); so one does not say ‘I took alarm (faziʿtu) at Allah’ as one says ‘I fear (ḫiftu) Allah’. The noun fazaʿ in the Quran is said only of the Last Day.

رَوْع fright

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, the word is tied to rūʿ, the inner mind: rawʿ is what strikes it, the fear cast into it. It comes only once in the Quran: of Abraham, when his fright had passed.
