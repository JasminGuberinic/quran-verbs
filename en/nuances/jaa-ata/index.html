Nuances

To come

جَاءَ · أَتَى

What they share

Both verbs mean ‘to come’ and are often interchangeable; the classical lexicographers nevertheless record a difference of emphasis.

How to tell them apart

Stress on actual arrival → جَاءَ; a coming with ease, or a coming seen as directed toward its goal without stressing arrival → أَتَى. A difference of emphasis, not a strict rule: both are used of events still to come.

جَاءَ to come

278× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the more general word, said with regard to actual arrival (ḥuṣūl).

أَتَى to come

264× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ‘a coming with ease’; it may also be said with regard to the intent to come, even when the arrival does not take place.
