What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الْأَهْوَاءُ

    the desires (prohtjevi) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الْأَهْوَاءَ

    the desires (prohtjeve) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي الْأَهْوَاءِ

    in the desires (u prohtjevima) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ الْأَهْوَاءِ

    from the desires (od prohtjeva) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    اِتِّبَاعُ الْأَهْوَاءِ

    the following of the desires (slijeđenje prohtjeva) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    أَهْوَآءَهُمْ from the Quran

    their desires (njihove prohtjeve) · Bosnian accusative
