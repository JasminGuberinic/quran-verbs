Idle talk and falsehood

لَغْو · بَاطِل

What they share

Both words speak of what has no worth. They differ in what is meant: speech that is not counted because it was said without thought, or what is the opposite of truth and does not hold up when examined — in word or in deed.

How to tell them apart

Speech without thought, not counted — also idle oaths; in the Quran always speech → لَغْو; the opposite of truth, what has no firmness — false gods, wealth taken wrongly → بَاطِل.

لَغْو idle talk

9× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, laġw is speech that is not counted, said without thought, like the chirping of sparrows; and any ugly speech may also be called so. So too is an oath without intent, said out of habit. In the Quran (all 9 places) it is always speech: the believers shun it, and in Paradise it is not heard.

بَاطِل falsehood, the false

26× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, bāṭil is the opposite of truth (ḥaqq): what has no firmness when it is examined. It is said of words and of deeds. In the Quran it is the false gods called on besides Allah, falsehood that vanishes before truth, and wealth taken in a wrongful way.
