Nuances

Intending and resolving

هَمَّ · عَزَمَ

What they share

Both words speak of the will turning towards a deed. They differ in how firm the will is: an intention in the soul, which may remain unrealised, or a heart bound to carrying the deed out.

How to tell them apart

What a person intends within himself, often unrealised → هَمَّ; a heart firmly bound to carrying the matter through → عَزَمَ.

هَمَّ to intend, to be about to

8× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, hamm is what a person intends within himself — and this is the root sense of the word. In the Quran such intentions often remain unrealised: Allah withheld the hands of those who intended to attack, and others intended what they did not attain.

عَزَمَ to resolve, to decide firmly

4× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ʿazm is tying the heart to carrying the matter through. It is no mere thought but a decision followed by the deed: so after the decision the Quran says “then put thy trust in Allah” (3:159).
