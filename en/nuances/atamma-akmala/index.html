Nuances

Completing and perfecting

أَتَمَّ · أَكْمَلَ

What they share

Both words mean that a thing is no longer lacking. According to ar-Rāġib, the measure differs: for the first that the thing needs nothing outside itself, for the second that what it exists for has been attained.

How to tell them apart

To bring to the point where the thing needs nothing outside itself — a number, a term, a rite, a favour → أَتَمَّ; to bring it to where its purpose is attained → أَكْمَلَ (2×; in 5:3 the two verbs stand side by side).

أَتَمَّ to complete

16× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, a thing is complete (tāmm) when it reaches the limit at which it needs nothing outside itself, and lacking when it needs something outside; it is said of what is counted and measured. In the Quran a number of nights, the rites of Hajj, the fast and Allah's favour are completed.

أَكْمَلَ to perfect

2× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, the perfection (kamāl) of a thing is the attaining of what is its purpose. So the “two whole years” of nursing (2:233) mean, he says, the utmost on which the child's welfare depends. The verb occurs only twice in the Quran.

Sources

  • ar-Rāġib al-Iṣfahānī, Mufradāt alfāẓ al-Qurʾān — entries تمام, كمل
