Nuances

Going out and coming forth

خَرَجَ · بَرَزَ

What they share

Both words mean leaving what one was in. Ar-Rāġib even explains kharaja by baraza; the difference lies more in usage: leaving a place or state, or coming out into the open, where one becomes visible.

How to tell them apart

To leave a place or a state — a house, a town, a garment, the general word → خَرَجَ; to come out into the open, step forth before others, become visible → بَرَزَ.

خَرَجَ to go out, to leave

53× in the Quran · Verb page →

Ar-Rāġib defines ḫurūǧ as leaving one's place or state — a house, a town or a garment, whether the state is within a person or in his outward circumstances. The general word: people go out of houses and towns, the dead out of graves, plants out of the earth, drink out of the bees' bellies.

بَرَزَ to come forth (into the open)

5× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, barāz is open ground, and baraza is to come out into the open: to appear by oneself — as a fighter steps out of the ranks — to stand out by merit, or to be left uncovered when what hid one is removed. So on the Day of Judgement all come forth before Allah.
