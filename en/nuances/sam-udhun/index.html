Hearing and the ear

سَمْع · أُذُن

What they share

Both words belong to hearing. One names the power by which one hears and the act of hearing itself, the other the organ, the part of the body in which that power sits.

How to tell them apart

Hearing — the power and the act of hearing; in the Quran always singular → سَمْع; the ear — the organ, a part of the body, mostly plural (آذَان) → أُذُن.

سَمْع hearing

22× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, samʿ is the power in the ear by which sounds are perceived, and the act of hearing is called by the same word; the Quran sometimes means by it the ear, sometimes understanding or obedience. In the Quran it is always singular, even where its owners are many and the eyes and hearts beside it are plural.

أُذُن ear

18× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, udhun is the organ, a part of the body; it is also said of a person who listens a great deal. When the Quran says there is ‘heaviness in their ears’ (waqr), that, in his view, points to their ignorance, not to their not hearing. In the Quran it is mostly plural (ādhān), unlike samʿ.
