Nuances

Garden, orchard, meadow

جَنَّة · حَدَائِق · رَوْضَة

What they share

All three words name a green piece of land with water. They differ in what comes first: trees that cover the ground, land that holds water, or a place where water collects and greenery grows.

How to tell them apart

Land under trees that cover it — a garden, in the Quran mostly Paradise → جَنَّة; a piece of land with water, named after the pupil of the eye → حَدَائِق; a place where water collects, with greenery, in the Quran only of Paradise → رَوْضَة.

جَنَّة garden; Paradise

147× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the root جنن means to cover a thing from the senses, and جَنَّة is any garden whose trees cover the ground. Paradise, he says, is so named either by likeness to a garden on earth — though the gap between them is great — or because its blessings are hidden from us. In the Quran it is mostly Paradise, but earthly gardens too (18:39, 34:16).

حَدَائِق orchards, watered gardens

3× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, حَدِيقَة (plural حَدَائِق) is a piece of land that has water, so named because in shape and in holding water it resembles the pupil of the eye (حَدَقَة). In the Quran it comes only in the plural, three times: twice of what rain brings out of the earth, once of the God-fearing in Paradise (78:32).

رَوْضَة meadow (green, well-watered)

2× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, رَوْض is a place where water collects, and greenery. Of 30:15 he says it is an expression for the beauties and delights of Paradise. In the Quran it comes only twice, both times of Paradise.
