لَوْلَاif not for; why not

75× in the Quran

What it does

Two words of one form. Before a noun it means ‘if not for…’: the noun is nominative, and what did not happen comes in the answer. Before a verb it urges or reproaches: ‘why do you not…?’

Uses

  1. ‘If not for…’
  2. ‘Why do you not…?’: urging

‘If not for…’

condition · corpus: 34×

The noun after it is nominative. According to Ibn Hišām it is a subject whose predicate is left out (‘had the favour not been – i.e. not existed’), not the doer of a left-out verb, nor made nominative by لَوْلَا itself, as some held.

The word after it is nominative.

‘Why do you not…?’: urging

urging · corpus: 41×

Before a present-tense verb it urges; before a past one it reproaches for what was missed.

The verb after it stays indicative.

Sources

  • Ibn Hišām al-Anṣārī, Muġnī al-labīb ʿan kutub al-aʿārīb
