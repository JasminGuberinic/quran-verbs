Couches and canopied couches

سُرُر · أَرَائِك

What they share

Both words are seats of rest, almost always in the Garden. They differ in what they name: the couch itself, to sit and recline on, or a couch with a canopy over it.

How to tell them apart

A couch to sit on — ar-Rāġib links it to gladness (surūr); facing one another, ranged, raised → سُرُر; a couch under a canopy, to recline on and to look from → أَرَائِك.

سُرُر couches

6× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, sarīr is what one sits on, and the name comes from surūr — gladness, since it belonged to people of ease; its plurals are asirra and surur. In the Quran they are the couches of the Garden: facing one another (15:47, 37:44), ranged (52:20), raised (88:13). Once they belong to this world (43:34).

أَرَائِك canopied couches

5× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, arīka is a curtained canopy (ḥaǧala) over a couch (sarīr); it was named either because it was made of arāk wood, or as a place of staying (from aruka bi-l-makān). In the Quran (all 5 places) only in the Garden: they recline on them (18:31, 36:56, 76:13) and look from them (83:23, 83:35).
