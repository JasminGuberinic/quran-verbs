Nuances

Anger, rage and displeasure

غَضَب · غَيْظ · سَخَط

What they share

All three words speak of anger. They differ in strength, in whether they are said of Allah, and in what they stand against.

How to tell them apart

Anger, both human and Allah's → غَضَب; the strongest, seething rage, never said of Allah in the Quran → غَيْظ; displeasure, the opposite of good pleasure (رِضْوَان) → سَخَط.

غَضَب anger

14× in the Quran · Noun page →

The anger of a man (Moses at his people) and Allah's anger at those who earn it. ar-Rāġib explains it, when said of Allah, as punishment; many scholars of Ahl as-Sunna affirm it as an attribute of Allah as befits Him, unlike human anger.

غَيْظ rage, fury

6× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the most intense anger, a heat that boils in the heart. In the Quran it is said of people — disbelievers, and believers who restrain it — and of the Fire; never of Allah (our observation).

سَخَط displeasure, wrath

1× in the Quran

Displeasure, the opposite of being pleased. In the Quran (our observation) this word and its verb stand against Allah's good pleasure (رِضْوَان) or human contentment (رَضُوا).
