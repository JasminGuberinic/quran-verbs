What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    ٱلْقَوْلُ

    the saying (izreka) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    ٱلْقَوْلَ

    the saying (izreku) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ ٱلْقَوْلِ

    from the saying (od izreke) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي ٱلْقَوْلِ

    in the saying (u izreci) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    صَاحِبُ ٱلْقَوْلِ

    the author of the saying (autor izreke) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    قَوْلُهُمْ from the Quran

    their saying (njihova izreka) · Bosnian nominative

Practice sentences

  • subject (nominative)

    الْقَوْلُ وَاضِحٌ

    The saying is clear.

  • object (accusative)

    كَتَبَ الطَّالِبُ قَوْلًا جَمِيلًا

    The student wrote a beautiful saying.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    فَكَّرَ الرَّجُلُ فِي الْقَوْلِ

    The man thought about the saying.

  • possessor (genitive)

    صَاحِبُ الْقَوْلِ مَعْرُوفٌ

    The author of the saying is known.
