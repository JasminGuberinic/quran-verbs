What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الْأَسِيرُ

    the prisoner (zarobljenik) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الْأَسِيرَ

    the prisoner (zarobljenika) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ الْأَسِيرِ

    from the prisoner (od zarobljenika) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي الْأَسِيرِ

    in the prisoner (u zarobljeniku) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    أُمُّ الْأَسِيرِ

    the mother of the prisoner (majka zarobljenika) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    أَسِيرُهُ

    his/its prisoner (njegov zarobljenik) · Bosnian nominative
