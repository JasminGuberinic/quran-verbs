Nuances

To do, to work, to make

فَعَلَ · عَمِلَ · صَنَعَ

What they share

All three verbs mean ‘to do’; ar-Rāġib orders them from the more general to the narrower.

How to tell them apart

Any doing, intended or not → فَعَلَ; a deed done on purpose by a living being → عَمِلَ; skilful making, a deed done well → صَنَعَ.

فَعَلَ to do

88× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, an effect coming from an agent; a general word — whether done well or not, knowingly or not, intended or not. Hence it is ascribed even to animals and things.

عَمِلَ to do, to work

276× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, every doing of a living being done with intent; narrower than fiʿl, and seldom ascribed to animals and things. He notes it is used of good and evil deeds alike — hence عَمَلٌ صَالِحٌ, the righteous deed.

صَنَعَ to make, to craft

15× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ‘doing a deed well’: every ṣunʿ is a fiʿl, but not every fiʿl is ṣunʿ; nor is it ascribed to animals and things as fiʿl is. The Quran nevertheless uses it neutrally too, even of bad deeds (5:62, 29:45).
