Burden and load

وِزْر · ثَقَل

What they share

Both words mean a heavy thing that is carried. According to ar-Rāġib, وِزْر is weight, named after a mountain refuge (وَزَر), and sin is expressed by it, as it is also expressed by heaviness (ثِقْل).

How to tell them apart

Burden — in the Quran mostly the sin a person carries; no one bears another's burden → وِزْر; load — in the Quran mostly a physical load, and once sins (29:13) → ثَقَل / أَثْقَال.

وِزْر burden

12× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, weight — named after a mountain refuge — by which sin is expressed. In Quranic usage mostly the sin a person carries on the Day of Resurrection; bearing another's burden means, he says, bearing as much again, without its being lifted off the other.

ثَقَل load

6× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, heaviness is at root a property of bodies, and is then said of meanings — of a debt or a sin that weighs one down. In the Quran أَثْقَال are mostly loads carried by animals or by the earth, and once sins (29:13).
