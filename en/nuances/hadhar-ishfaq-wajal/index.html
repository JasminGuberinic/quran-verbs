Nuances

Being wary, anxious care, trembling

حَذِرَ · مُشْفِق · وَجِلَ

What they share

All three words are kinds of fear. According to ar-Rāġib, the first is guarding against what is feared, the second care mixed with fear, the third fear felt within.

How to tell them apart

To guard against what is feared, to be on one's guard → حَذِرَ; anxious care — fear mixed with concern for what one holds dear → مُشْفِق; to feel fear within, the heart trembling → وَجِلَ.

حَذِرَ to beware, to be wary

12× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ḥaḏar is guarding against what is feared. The weight is on the action: one takes care, keeps away, prepares — “Take your precautions” (4:71), he says, means weapons and the like.

مُشْفِق anxious, apprehensive

8× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, išfāq is care mixed with fear: one who is anxious loves the one he is anxious for and fears what may befall him. With min (of) the fear is heard more, with fī (among) the care.

وَجِلَ to tremble (with fear)

3× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, waǧal is feeling fear within. In the Quran the hearts of believers tremble when Allah is mentioned (8:2, 22:35; also 23:60), and Abraham tells his guests that he fears them (15:52) and they answer: “Be not afraid!” (15:53).
