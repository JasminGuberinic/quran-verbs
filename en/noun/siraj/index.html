What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    السِّراجُ

    the lamp (svjetiljka) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    السِّراجَ

    the lamp (svjetiljku) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي السِّراجِ

    in the lamp (u svjetiljci) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ السِّراجِ

    from the lamp (iz svjetiljke) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    نُورُ السِّراجِ

    the light of the lamp (svjetlo svjetiljke) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    سِراجُهُ

    his/its lamp (njegova svjetiljka) · Bosnian nominative

Practice sentences

  • subject (nominative)

    أَضَاءَ السِّرَاجُ الْغُرْفَةَ

    The lamp lit up the room.

  • object (accusative)

    أَشْعَلَ الْجَدُّ السِّرَاجَ

    Grandfather lit the lamp.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    وَضَعَ الْأَبُ الزَّيْتَ فِي السِّرَاجِ

    The father put oil in the lamp.

  • possessor (genitive)

    نُورُ السِّرَاجِ ضَعِيفٌ

    The light of the lamp is weak.
