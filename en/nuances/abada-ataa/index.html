Worshipping and obeying

عَبَدَ · أَطَاعَ

What they share

Both words speak of yielding to another. According to ar-Rāġib, worship is the utmost humbling, which only Allah deserves, while obedience (طَاعَة) is mostly said of following a command; in the Quran obedience is owed to the Messenger too.

How to tell them apart

The utmost humbling — worship, which belongs only to Allah → عَبَدَ; to comply, to obey a command (Allah's, the Messenger's, but also others') → أَطَاعَ.

عَبَدَ to worship

122× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, worship is the utmost humbling, stronger than mere servitude, and only the One whose favour is greatest deserves it — Allah. Worship, he says, is of two kinds: by subjection, by which all things serve Allah, and by choice, which is commanded to rational beings.

أَطَاعَ to obey

72× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ṭawʿ is compliance, its opposite compulsion; طَاعَة is the same but said mostly of carrying out a command and keeping to what is prescribed. In the Quran obedience is owed to Allah and the Messenger, and to those in authority, but not to parents when they call to shirk.
