Nuances

To walk, to travel, to hasten

مَشَى · سَارَ · سَعَى

What they share

All three verbs speak of motion. They differ in manner, in the length of the way and in whether the motion carries effort.

How to tell them apart

To walk — motion and the manner of walking → مَشَى; to travel, to go through the land, most often with a call to look and take heed → سَارَ; to hasten, and effort in a matter, good or bad → سَعَى.

مَشَى to walk

21× in the Quran · Verb page →

Ordinary walking from place to place. The Quran often says with it how one walks — proudly, humbly, shyly.

سَارَ to travel, to go

16× in the Quran · Verb page →

To travel, to go on through the land. In the Quran most often in the call “Travel in the land, and see the nature of the consequence…” (6:11) — travel for the lesson it teaches.

سَعَى to hasten; to strive

20× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, a quick walk short of running; it is carried over to earnest effort in a matter — in good (for the hereafter) or in evil (spreading corruption), though most often of praiseworthy deeds.
