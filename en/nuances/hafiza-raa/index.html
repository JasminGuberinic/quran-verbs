Nuances

Guarding and tending

حَفِظَ · رَعَى

What they share

Both words speak of care that something stays safe. They differ in the image they carry: keeping so that it is not lost or forgotten, or tending as a shepherd does — feeding, defending and watching where the thing is going.

How to tell them apart

To keep, retain, guard from loss and forgetting, the general word → حَفِظَ; to tend as a shepherd tends a flock — feeding and defending, and figuratively: to observe something as it deserves → رَعَى.

حَفِظَ to guard, to keep

7× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ḥifẓ is first the power of the soul that holds fast what is understood, and retention in the soul — the opposite of forgetting — and then every kind of care and guarding. In the Quran Allah guards the heaven from the devils, as befits Him, and believers guard their private parts.

رَعَى to tend, to pasture; to observe

2× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, riʿy is at root guarding an animal — by the food that keeps it alive or by driving the enemy from it; then it is said of all guarding and governing. So “they observed it not with right observance” (57:27) means they did not keep it as it should be kept.
