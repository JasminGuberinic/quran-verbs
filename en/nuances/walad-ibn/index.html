Nuances

Child and son

وَلَد · ابْن

What they share

Both words speak of offspring. According to ar-Rāġib, وَلَد is the one born — one or many, small or grown (he quotes Abū l-Ḥasan that it covers son and daughter) — while ابْن is a son, so called because his father ‘built’ him, and is said of anything that comes from something.

How to tell them apart

A child, offspring by birth, son or daughter, one or many → وَلَد; a son; and figuratively the ‘son’ of something — the traveller (ابْن السَّبِيل), the children of Adam → ابْن.

وَلَد child, offspring

56× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the one born — one or many, small or grown; he quotes Abū l-Ḥasan that it covers son and daughter; an adopted child too is called وَلَد. The Quran uses this word, as well as ‘son’ and ‘daughters’, to reject that Allah has offspring.

ابْن son

143× in the Quran · Noun page →

A son. According to ar-Rāġib, from ‘to build’: the father ‘built’ him. So whatever comes from something is called its ‘son’: the traveller is the ‘son of the road’, and a man of learning is the ‘son of knowledge’.
