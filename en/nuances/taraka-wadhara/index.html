Nuances

To leave

تَرَكَ · وَذَرَ

What they share

Both verbs mean ‘to leave’; they differ in form and tone: leaving in general, or leaving what one sets no store by.

How to tell them apart

To leave, abandon — by choice or by force; to leave an inheritance behind → تَرَكَ; to let be, give up, ‘leave them’ — only in present and imperative → وَذَرَ.

تَرَكَ to leave, to abandon

40× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, to reject a thing intentionally and by choice, or by force and necessity. Said too of what the dead leave behind.

وَذَرَ to leave, to let be

45× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, to leave a thing as if casting it off, setting little store by it; its past tense, he says, is not used — in the Quran it occurs only in present and imperative (ذَرْ, ذَرُوا).
