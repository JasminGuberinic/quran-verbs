Nuances

Tarrying and staying a while

لَبِثَ · مَكَثَ

What they share

Both words mean remaining somewhere for a time. They differ in whether one looks at the length of the stay, or at staying put, often while waiting.

How to tell them apart

To stay at a place, keeping to it — in the Quran mostly with the question or measure ‘how long’ → لَبِثَ; to stay put, often while waiting → مَكَثَ.

لَبِثَ to tarry, to remain (for a span)

29× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, staying at a place, keeping to it. In the Quran this verb mostly comes with asking or measuring how long the stay lasted: a day or part of a day, years, a thousand years less fifty.

مَكَثَ to stay, to bide (waiting)

4× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, makṯ is staying put while waiting. Moses tells his family to stay while he goes to the fire; yet in 13:17 what benefits people ‘remains in the earth’, and in 18:3 the believers stay in their reward for ever, with no waiting at all.
