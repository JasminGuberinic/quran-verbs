Nuances

To create and to make

خَلَقَ · جَعَلَ · فَطَرَ

What they share

All three verbs speak of Allah's making and can be rendered ‘to create’; they differ in what is in view.

How to tell them apart

Creating by measure, and originating without a model → خَلَقَ; making a thing into something, placing it, giving it a state → جَعَلَ; bringing into being from the start, giving the creature its original nature → فَطَرَ.

خَلَقَ to create

184× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the root sense is ‘right measuring’; it is used of bringing a thing into being without prior material or model — creation in that sense belongs to Allah alone — and also of making one thing from another.

جَعَلَ to make, to place

340× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, a general word for every kind of doing, wider than فَعَلَ and صَنَعَ; among its uses are making one thing from another and turning a thing from one state into another. Hence it often takes two objects: ‘make X into Y’.

فَطَرَ to originate, to create

8× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the root sense is splitting lengthwise; Allah's faṭr is bringing a thing into being for the first time, in a form ready for what it is made for — hence فِطْرَة, original nature. Tafsir reports that Ibn ʿAbbās did not know what فَاطِر meant until he heard a Bedouin say of a well, ‘I faṭartuhā’, i.e. I first dug it.

Sources

  • ar-Rāġib al-Iṣfahānī, Mufradāt alfāẓ al-Qurʾān — entries خلق, جعل, فطر
  • aṭ-Ṭabarī, Jāmiʿ al-bayān — the report from Ibn ʿAbbās on fāṭir
