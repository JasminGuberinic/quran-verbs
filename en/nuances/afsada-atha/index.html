Nuances

Corrupting and doing wanton evil

أَفْسَدَ · عَثَا

What they share

Both words speak of corruption on the earth. They differ in breadth: one is the general word for any departure from balance and soundness, the other a narrower, sharper term for evil recognised by judgement rather than by the senses.

How to tell them apart

Corrupting, putting out of balance — the general word, the opposite of setting right (iṣlāḥ); in soul, body, things → أَفْسَدَ; doing wanton evil — ar-Rāġib: corruption recognised by judgement; in the Quran only in the ban ‘do not do evil on the earth, making mischief’ → عَثَا.

أَفْسَدَ to corrupt, to make mischief

15× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, fasād is a thing's departure from balance, a little or much, and its opposite is soundness (ṣalāḥ); it is said of the soul, the body and things. It is the general word: in the Quran mostly ‘to make mischief on the earth’, against its opposite ‘to set right’.

عَثَا to do wanton evil

5× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ʿayṯ and ʿuṯiyy are close, but ʿayṯ is said mostly of corruption perceived by the senses, ʿuṯiyy of what is recognised by judgement. In the Quran the verb is all five times in the same prohibition, always with ‘making mischief’ (mufsidīn) — a ban on wanton evil, not merely on damage.
