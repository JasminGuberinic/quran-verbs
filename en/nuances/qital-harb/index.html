Nuances

Fighting and war

قِتَال · حَرْب

What they share

Both words speak of armed conflict. They differ in whether one looks at the fighting itself, between two sides, or at war as a state and an event that flares up and ends.

How to tell them apart

Fighting one another, the act of two sides — what is prescribed, what some shrink from or what does not come → قِتَال; war as a state and an event — a fire lit and put out, a war that ‘lays down its burdens’ → حَرْب.

قِتَال fighting

13× in the Quran · Noun page →

قِتَال is the verbal noun of قَاتَلَ, ‘to fight someone’. According to ar-Rāġib, مُقَاتَلَة is warring (مُحَارَبَة) and seeking to kill. In the Quran fighting is often what is prescribed (2:216, 2:246, 4:77), and what some shrink from.

حَرْب war

4× in the Quran · Noun page →

ar-Rāġib says only that حَرْب is ‘well known’, and from the same root gives حَرَب, plunder in war. In the Quran (four times) حَرْب is war as a state and an event: a fire people light and Allah puts out (5:64), a war that ‘lays down its burdens’ (47:4), and the war threatened against those who persist in usury (2:279).
