What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الْبُنَيُّ

    the son (sin) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الْبُنَيَّ

    the son (sina) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ الْبُنَيِّ

    from the son (od sina) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    بِالْبُنَيِّ

    with the son (sa sinom) · Bosnian instrumental

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    اسْمُ الْبُنَيِّ

    the name of the son (ime sina) · Bosnian genitive

Practice sentences

  • subject (nominative)

    نَامَ بُنَيُّ الْجَارِ

    The neighbor's little son fell asleep.

  • object (accusative)

    رَأَيْتُ بُنَيَّ الْجَارِ

    I saw the neighbor's little son.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    لَعِبْتُ مَعَ بُنَيِّ الْجَارِ

    I played with the neighbor's little son.

  • possessor (genitive)

    لُعْبَةُ بُنَيِّ الْجَارِ جَمِيلَةٌ

    The neighbor's little son's toy is beautiful.
