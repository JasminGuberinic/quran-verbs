What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الْفَقِيرُ

    the poor person (siromah) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    ٱلْفَقِيرَ

    the poor person (siromaha) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي الْفَقِيرِ

    in the poor person (u siromahu) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ الْفَقِيرِ

    from the poor person (od siromaha) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    بَيْتُ الْفَقِيرِ

    the house of the poor person (kuća siromaha) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    فَقِيرُهُ

    his/its poor person (njegov siromah) · Bosnian nominative
