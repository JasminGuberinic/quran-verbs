Nuances

Evil and ill

شَرّ · سُوء

What they share

Both words mean what is bad. According to ar-Rāġib, شَرّ is what everyone shuns, as good (خَيْر) is what everyone desires; سُوء is whatever grieves a person, and a foul deed.

How to tell them apart

Evil, the counterpart of good (خَيْر): what everyone shuns → شَرّ; what grieves — in this world and the next, in soul, body and property — and a foul deed → سُوء.

شَرّ evil

30× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, what everyone shuns, as good is what everyone desires. In the Quran it often stands with good (خَيْر) as its pair: as a deed, as a trial, and as what befalls a person.

سُوء ill, what grieves; a foul deed

50× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, whatever grieves a person — of worldly and otherworldly matters, states of soul and body, and the loss of wealth, standing or someone dear; and also a foul deed. In the Quran it often qualifies another word: ‘the worst torment’ (سُوءَ الْعَذَابِ), ‘the evil reckoning’.
