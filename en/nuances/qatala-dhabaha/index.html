Nuances

Killing and slaughtering

قَتَلَ · ذَبَحَ

What they share

Both words speak of taking life. According to ar-Rāġib, قَتْل is the most general word for it, while ذَبْح is cutting the throat.

How to tell them apart

To kill — in any way, as someone's act → قَتَلَ; to slaughter, to cut the throat (most often of an animal) → ذَبَحَ.

قَتَلَ to kill

83× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, removing the soul from the body, like death; it is called قَتْل when seen as someone's act, and death when seen as the loss of life. It is the most general word for killing.

ذَبَحَ to slaughter

5× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, cutting an animal's throat. In the Quran: the cow Allah ordered slaughtered; Abraham's dream of sacrificing his son — whom Allah ransomed with a great sacrifice (37:107); and Pharaoh's men “slaying your sons” (2:49) — يُذَبِّحُونَ, which ar-Rāġib takes as slaughter repeated, one after another.
