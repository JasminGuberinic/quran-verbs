What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الطِّينُ

    the clay (glina) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الطِّينَ

    the clay (glinu) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ ٱلطِّينِ

    from the clay (od gline) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    عَلَى ٱلطِّينِ

    on the clay (na glini) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    لَوْنُ ٱلطِّينِ

    the color of the clay (boja gline) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    طِينُهُ

    his/its clay (njegova glina) · Bosnian nominative

Practice sentences

    The clay is flexible.

    حَمَلَ الْعَامِلُ الطِّينَ

    The worker carried the clay.

    The children played with the clay.

  • possessor (genitive)

    نَوْعُ الطِّينِ جَيِّدٌ

    The type of the clay is good.
