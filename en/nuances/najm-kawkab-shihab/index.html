Star, shining star and flame

نَجْم · كَوْكَب · شِهَاب

What they share

All three words speak of light in the sky or from a fire. They differ in what they stress: the star that rises and by which a course is set, a heavenly body as it appears and shines, or a blazing flame that flashes.

How to tell them apart

The rising star, a guide on the way → نَجْم; a star as it appears and shines, the sky's ornament → كَوْكَب; a blazing flame — from a fire, or the one that pursues in the sky → شِهَاب.

نَجْم star

13× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the root sense of نَجْم is the star that rises; the same word is also the verbal noun ‘rising’, so it is said of plants that sprout and of a thought that occurs. In the Quran the stars are a guide (6:97, 16:16), subjected to Allah's command (16:12), and on the Last Day they are dimmed (81:2).

كَوْكَب shining star

5× in the Quran · Noun page →

Ar-Rāġib treats it under the entry كب: kawākib are the stars that appear, and they are not called so until they show. In the Quran كَوْكَب is what is seen and shines: the ornament of the lowest heaven (37:6), the glass that gleams ‘like a shining star’ (24:35), and on the Last Day the shining stars are scattered (82:2).

شِهَاب flame

5× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, شِهَاب is the bright flame from a kindled fire, and also what appears in the air (37:10). The Quran has both: Moses promises his family a flame from the fire he has seen (27:7), and in the sky a flame pursues the one who listens by stealth (15:18, 37:10, 72:9).
