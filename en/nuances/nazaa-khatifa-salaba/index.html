Nuances

Drawing out, snatching, robbing

نَزَعَ · خَطِفَ · سَلَبَ

What they share

All three words mean taking a thing from where it was. They differ in manner: drawing out, snatching in haste, or taking by force from another.

How to tell them apart

To pull a thing from its place — a hand, or what is in the heart; the general word → نَزَعَ; to snatch quickly, in an instant → خَطِفَ; to take from another by force → سَلَبَ.

نَزَعَ to draw out, to remove

10× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, nazʿ is pulling a thing from its place, and it is said of what is not a body too: drawing enmity or love out of the heart. It can also mean taking away, as kingship. In the Quran Moses draws out his hand, and Allah draws malice out of the hearts of the people of Paradise.

خَطِفَ to snatch

3× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, ḫaṭf is snatching away with speed. In the Quran lightning almost snatches sight, a devil snatches what it overhears, and, in a simile, one who has fallen from the sky is snatched by birds.

سَلَبَ to rob, to take by force

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, salb is taking a thing from another by force. In the Quran it comes only once, in a parable of the helplessness of those invoked besides Allah: they cannot recover even what a fly robs from them.
