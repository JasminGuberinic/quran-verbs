Nuances

Stinginess and avarice

بَخِلَ · بُخْل · شُحّ

What they share

Both words mean holding back one's goods. According to ar-Rāġib, بُخْل is withholding what should not be withheld, while شُحّ is stinginess joined with greed, as a settled habit.

How to tell them apart

To be stingy: to withhold what should be given (the verb) → بَخِلَ; the act of stinginess, in the Quran also when urged on others → بُخْل; stinginess with greed, grown into the soul's habit → شُحّ.

بَخِلَ to be stingy, to withhold

10× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, to withhold possessions where they should not be withheld; its opposite is generosity. In the Quran it is mostly said of holding back what Allah has given of His bounty.

بُخْل stinginess

2× in the Quran · Noun page →

The noun, only twice in the Quran, both times in the same phrase, said of people who are mean themselves and urge others to meanness too. According to ar-Rāġib, stinginess with others' possessions is worse than with one's own, and this is the very ayah he gives for it.

شُحّ avarice

5× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, stinginess joined with greed (حِرْص), in what has become habit. In Quranic usage the noun is all three times the soul's avarice: souls are prone to it, and successful is the one kept from it.
