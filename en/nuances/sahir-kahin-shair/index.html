Magician, soothsayer and poet

سَاحِر · كَاهِن · شَاعِر

What they share

All three words are charges the deniers laid against the Prophet, and the Quran rejects them: the soothsayer and the poet (52:29, 69:41–42), while “a wizard shall not be successful” (20:69).

How to tell them apart

One who deceives the eye with illusion or seeks the devils' help → سَاحِر; a soothsayer who claims hidden knowledge by guessing → كَاهِن; a poet — and for some scholars, in that charge: a teller of fictions → شَاعِر.

سَاحِر magician, sorcerer

22× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, sorcery is illusion without reality, or seeking the devils' help; the belief that it changes the forms of things has no reality. The deniers called both Moses and the Prophet by this name; the Quran says the magician will never succeed.

كَاهِن soothsayer

2× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, one who tells hidden past matters by a kind of guessing (the عَرَّاف tells future ones); both crafts rest on guessing that sometimes hits and sometimes misses. Twice in the Quran, both times a rejected charge.

شَاعِر poet

5× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, شِعْر at root means fine knowledge, then measured, rhymed speech. Many mufassirun take the charge ‘poet’ as calling the Quran verse; some scholars say they meant to call him a liar, since poetry is the home of invention (26:224).
