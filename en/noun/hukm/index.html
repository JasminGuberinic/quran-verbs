What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    ٱلْحُكْمُ

    the judgment (presuda) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    ٱلْحُكْمَ

    the judgment (presudu) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي الْحُكْمِ

    in the judgment (u presudi) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ الْحُكْمِ

    from the judgment (od presude) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    نَصُّ الْحُكْمِ

    the text of the judgment (tekst presude) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    حُكْمُهُ

    his/its judgment (njegova presuda) · Bosnian nominative
