What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    ٱلظُّلُمَٰتُ

    the darknesses (tama) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ

    the darknesses (tamu) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ

    in the darknesses (u tami) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ

    from the darknesses (iz tame) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    سَوَادُ ٱلظُّلُمَٰتِ

    the blackness of the darknesses (crnilo tame) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    ظُلُمَٰتُهُ

    his/its darknesses (njegova tama) · Bosnian nominative
