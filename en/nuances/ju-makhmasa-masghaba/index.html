Nuances

Hunger, emaciating hunger, exhausting hunger

جُوع · مَخْمَصَة · مَسْغَبَة

What they share

All three words mean hunger. They differ in what the hunger brings with it: the bare pain of an empty stomach, a body that wastes from hunger, or hunger joined to fatigue.

How to tell them apart

The pain that seizes a living being when its stomach is empty of food, the general word → جُوع; hunger that makes the belly shrink → مَخْمَصَة; hunger joined to fatigue → مَسْغَبَة.

جُوع hunger

4× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, hunger is the pain that strikes a living being when its stomach is empty. In Quranic usage it is the general word: hunger is a trial, a punishment of an ungrateful town, and what Allah fed Quraysh against.

مَخْمَصَة emaciating hunger

2× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, maḫmaṣa is a hunger that makes the belly shrink. It comes twice in the Quran: as the hard necessity that permits eating what is otherwise forbidden (5:3), and among the hardships endured in Allah's cause (9:120).

مَسْغَبَة exhausting hunger

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, saġab is hunger together with fatigue (and some say: thirst together with fatigue). The word comes only once in the Quran, describing a day on which feeding orphans and the poor is the steep ascent of good.
