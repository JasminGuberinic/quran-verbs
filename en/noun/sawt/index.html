What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الصَّوْتُ

    the voice (glas) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الصَّوْتَ

    the voice (glas) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    بِالصَّوْتِ

    with the voice (glasom) · Bosnian instrumental

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ الصَّوْتِ

    from the voice (iz glasa) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    قُوَّةُ الصَّوْتِ

    the strength of the voice (snaga glasa) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    أَصْوَٰتَكُمْ from the Quran

    your voices (vaše glasove) · Bosnian accusative

Practice sentences

  • subject (nominative)

    صَوْتُ الْمُغَنِّي جَمِيلٌ

    The singer's voice is beautiful.

  • object (accusative)

    سَمِعَتِ الْأُمُّ الصَّوْتَ

    The mother heard the voice.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    تَكَلَّمَ الْمُعَلِّمُ بِصَوْتٍ هَادِئٍ

    The teacher spoke in a calm voice.

  • possessor (genitive)

    قُوَّةُ الصَّوْتِ مُنَاسِبَةٌ

    The strength of the voice is suitable.
