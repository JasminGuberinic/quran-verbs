Nuances

Denying: belying, gainsaying, disowning

كَذَّبَ · جَحَدَ · أَنْكَرَ

What they share

All three words mean refusing something as true. They differ in what is refused and what is in the heart of the one refusing: calling the speaker a liar, denying what the heart is sure of, or not acknowledging something as known.

How to tell them apart

Calling the one who speaks the truth a liar — messengers, signs, the truth → كَذَّبَ; knowingly denying what the heart affirms → جَحَدَ; not acknowledging, rejecting as unknown — with the heart or with the tongue alone → أَنْكَرَ.

كَذَّبَ to belie, to call a lie

176× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, takḏīb is ascribing a lie to someone, whether he speaks the truth or not; but in the Quran, he says, it is the belying of one who speaks the truth: messengers, Allah's signs, the truth.

جَحَدَ to deny knowingly, to gainsay

12× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ǧuḥūd is denying what the heart affirms, or affirming what the heart denies. In the Quran (all 12 places) the verb takes the preposition bi-, and its object is Allah's signs, except in 16:71, where it is His favour.

أَنْكَرَ to disown, to refuse to acknowledge

3× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, inkār is the opposite of recognising (ʿirfān): something comes to the heart that it cannot picture. It is also said of disowning with the tongue, and the tongue sometimes disowns a thing whose image is present in the heart — and then it lies; so he understands 16:83.
