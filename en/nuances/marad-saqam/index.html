Nuances

Illness

مَرَض · مَرِيض · سَقِيم

What they share

All three words speak of illness. According to ar-Rāġib, سَقَم is illness of the body alone, while مَرَض may be in the body or in the soul — as in “In their hearts is a disease” (2:10).

How to tell them apart

Disease — as a noun, in the Quran always ‘in the hearts’: doubt, hypocrisy, desire → مَرَض; a sick person, ill in body (the allowances of the law) → مَرِيض; ill in body alone → سَقِيم.

مَرَض disease

13× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, leaving the balance proper to man — in the body or in the soul (ignorance, cowardice, avarice, hypocrisy). The noun مَرَض is in all 12 of its Quranic places a disease of the heart.

مَرِيض sick, ill

10× in the Quran · Noun page →

A sick person in the bodily sense. The Quran names him mostly in its allowances: the sick may break the fast, there is no blame on him if he stays behind.

سَقِيم ill, sick

2× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, illness of the body alone. Twice in the Quran: Abraham's words “Lo! I feel sick!” (37:89), which ar-Rāġib reads as an allusion (تَعْرِيض), as a past or coming illness, or as the slight ailment no one is ever wholly free of; and Jonah cast on the shore while ill (37:145).
