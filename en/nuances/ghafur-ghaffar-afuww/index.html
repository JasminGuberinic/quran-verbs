Nuances

the Forgiving, the Oft-Forgiving, the Pardoner

غَفُور · غَفَّار · عَفُوّ

What they share

All three names speak of Allah's forgiving, as befits Him. They differ in the image: forgiveness as covering and shielding from punishment (two forms of one root), or pardon — effacing the sin, turning from it so that it does not remain to be punished.

How to tell them apart

The Forgiving — ar-Rāġib: forgiveness is shielding the servant so that punishment does not touch him; very often with ‘the Merciful’ → غَفُور; the Oft-Forgiving — 5 times, with ‘the Mighty’ or with repentance and asking forgiveness → غَفَّار; the Pardoner — ar-Rāġib: passing over the sin; in four of five ayat beside ‘Ghafūr’ → عَفُوّ.

غَفُور the Forgiving

91× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, the root sense is ‘to clothe a thing in what guards it from dirt’, and Allah's forgiveness (maġfira) is that He guards the servant so that punishment does not touch him — as befits Him. Al-Ġafūr is in the Quran always Allah's name and very often stands beside ‘the Merciful’.

غَفَّار the Oft-Forgiving

5× in the Quran

A form that stresses abundance and repetition. It is in the Quran five times: three times beside ‘the Mighty’, and twice with repentance (20:82) and asking forgiveness (71:10). On 71:10 ar-Rāġib says that forgiveness was not to be asked with the tongue only, but with the tongue and with deeds — as befits Him.

عَفُوّ the Pardoner

5× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, ʿafw is ‘aiming to remove the sin, turning away from it’ — passing over the sin; in the prayer ‘al-ʿafw wa-l-ʿāfiya’ it is the leaving of punishment. As Allah's name it occurs five times, four times beside ‘Ghafūr’ and once beside ‘All-Powerful’ (4:149) — as befits Him.
