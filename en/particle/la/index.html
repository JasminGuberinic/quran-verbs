Particles · negation · prohibition · negation of the kind

لَاnot; do not; there is no

1742× in the Quran

What it does

The most frequent negation in the Quran. Before a present-tense verb it negates without changing it (the indicative stays). As a prohibition (‘do not’) it puts the verb in the jussive. As the lā that denies a whole kind it works like إِنَّ: the noun after it takes a fatḥa without nunation.

Uses

  1. ‘not’: negating a verb
  2. ‘do not’: prohibition
  3. ‘there is no … at all’: denying the kind
  4. Like لَيْسَ: ‘there is no’

‘not’: negating a verb

negation · corpus: 1345×

It negates a present or future verb; the verb stays indicative.

The verb after it stays indicative.

‘do not’: prohibition

prohibition · corpus: 332×

A prohibition (lā an-nāhiya): the present-tense verb comes in the jussive.

The verb after it is jussive.

‘there is no … at all’: denying the kind

negation of the kind · corpus: 57×

It denies a whole kind (lā an-nāfiya li-l-jins) and works like إِنَّ: the noun after it takes a fatḥa without nunation (لَا رَيْبَ). According to Ibn Hišām a simple noun there is fixed (built) on the fatḥa, the ending it would have as an accusative; the corpus tags it accusative and puts لَا in the family of إِنَّ.

Like لَيْسَ: ‘there is no’

negation · corpus: 8×

The corpus files so a لَا that works like لَيْسَ: the noun after it stays nominative, with tanwīn. Others read it as a لَا that changes nothing, the noun being the subject; in either reading the noun is nominative. Compare the لَا of the kind (لَا رَيْبَ), after which the noun has a fatḥa.

The word after it is nominative.
