Particles · defective verb (of the كَانَ family)

لَيْسَis not

89× in the Quran

What it does

A defective verb of the كَانَ family, not a particle: it denies that something is so, first of all in the present, and other times when the context says so (Ibn Hišām). Its noun is nominative and its predicate accusative; the predicate often takes بِـ (أَلَيْسَ ٱللَّهُ بِأَحْكَمِ ٱلْحَٰكِمِينَ). It is a verb because it changes for person – لَسْتُ، لَسْتَ، لَيْسُوا۟، لَسْتُنَّ – and with these forms Ibn Hišām rejects the view of Ibn as-Sarrāj and al-Fārisī that it is a particle; but it has no other tense and no imperative.

Uses

  1. ‘Is not’: the noun nominative, the predicate accusative
  2. ‘There is not (for someone)…’
  3. أَلَيْسَ: ‘is not…?’

‘Is not’: the noun nominative, the predicate accusative

defective verb (of the كَانَ family)

Like كَانَ, لَيْسَ stands before a nominal sentence: the subject becomes its noun (nominative), the predicate its predicate (accusative). When the noun is a pronoun, it is the ending on the verb (لَسْتَ – ‘you are not’).

‘There is not (for someone)…’

defective verb (of the كَانَ family)

When the noun is indefinite and the predicate is a phrase with a preposition (لَهُۥ، لِلْإِنسَٰنِ), the predicate comes first and the noun after it: ‘there is not for him…’. With إِلَّا the negation becomes a restriction: ‘man has only…’.

أَلَيْسَ: ‘is not…?’

defective verb (of the كَانَ family)

The question hamza before لَيْسَ asks for confirmation; the answer that affirms is بَلَى (‘yes, indeed’), not نَعَمْ. The predicate here often takes بِـ.
