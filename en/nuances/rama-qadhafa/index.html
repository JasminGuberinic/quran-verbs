Nuances

Throwing and hurling

رَمَى · قَذَفَ

What they share

Both verbs mean to throw. According to ar-Rāġib, رَمْي is said of objects such as an arrow or a stone, while قَذْف is throwing far; both are also used figuratively for insult.

How to tell them apart

To throw something at someone — a stone, an arrow; also to accuse someone falsely (24:4) → رَمَى; to hurl, to throw far or with force — into the river, terror into hearts, truth upon falsehood → قَذَفَ.

رَمَى to throw, to shoot

9× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, said of objects such as an arrow or a stone; in speech it is a figurative term for insult. In Quranic usage it is this verb, not قَذَفَ, that names the false accusation of chaste women (24:4).

قَذَفَ to hurl, to cast

9× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, throwing far; hence a distant place is called قَذَف. In the Quran Moses' mother casts him into the river, Allah casts terror into the hearts of the enemy, and hurls the truth against falsehood.
