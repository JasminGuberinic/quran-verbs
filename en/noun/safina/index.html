What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    ٱلسَّفِينَةُ

    the ship (lađa) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    السَّفِينَةَ

    the ship (lađu) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي ٱلسَّفِينَةِ

    in the ship (na lađi) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ ٱلسَّفِينَةِ

    from the ship (iz lađe) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    صَاحِبُ ٱلسَّفِينَةِ

    the owner of the ship (vlasnik lađe) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    سَفِينَتُهُ

    his/its ship (njegova lađa) · Bosnian nominative

Practice sentences

  • subject (nominative)

    أَبْحَرَتِ السَّفِينَةُ

    The ship set sail.

  • object (accusative)

    بَنَى النَّجَّارُ سَفِينَةً

    The carpenter built a ship.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    نَزَلَ الرُّكَّابُ مِنَ السَّفِينَةِ

    The passengers got off the ship.

  • possessor (genitive)

    صَاحِبُ السَّفِينَةِ غَنِيٌّ

    The owner of the ship is rich.
