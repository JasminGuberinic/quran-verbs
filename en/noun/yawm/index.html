What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    ٱلْيَوْمُ

    the day (dan) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    ٱلْيَوْمَ

    the day (dan) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    إِلَى ٱلْيَوْمِ

    to the day (do dana) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي ٱلْيَوْمِ

    in the day (u danu) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    آخِرُ ٱلْيَوْمِ

    the end of the day (kraj dana) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    يَوْمِكُمْ from the Quran

    of your day (vašeg dana) · Bosnian genitive

    The day is beautiful.

  • object (accusative)

    قَضَيْنَا الْيَوْمَ فِي الْبَيْتِ

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    خَرَجْنَا فِي يَوْمٍ مُشْمِسٍ

    We went out on a sunny day.

  • possessor (genitive)

    رَجَعْتُ فِي آخِرِ الْيَوْمِ

    I returned at the end of the day.
