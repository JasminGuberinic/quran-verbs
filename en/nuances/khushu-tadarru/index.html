Nuances

Humility and humble entreaty

خَاشِع · تَضَرُّع · خَاضِع

What they share

All three words speak of lowliness. ar-Rāġib notes that ḫušūʿ is mostly said of what shows in the body and ḍarāʿa of what is in the heart: ‘when the heart is humbled, the limbs become humble’.

How to tell them apart

Humble — lowliness that shows in the face, the gaze, the voice → خَاشِع; humble entreaty, pleading from the heart → تَضَرُّع; submissive, bowing before one greater → خَاضِع.

خَاشِع humble, humbly submissive

14× in the Quran

Humble; a lowliness that is seen — in prayer, on faces and in eyes on the Last Day, even on the earth and a mountain. It may come from awe, or from abasement.

تَضَرُّع humble entreaty

3× in the Quran

Humble, earnest entreaty, most often in supplication. According to ar-Rāġib, ḍarāʿa is mostly said of lowliness in the heart, and taḍarruʿ is showing it.

خَاضِع submissive, bowed

1× in the Quran

Submissive, bending before what is greater. Once in the Quran (our observation), of necks (or, for some mufassirun, leaders) that would bow before a sign from heaven.
