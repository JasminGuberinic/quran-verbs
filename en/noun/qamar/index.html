What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    ٱلْقَمَرُ

    the moon (mjesec) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    ٱلْقَمَرَ

    the moon (mjesec) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي ٱلْقَمَرِ

    in the moon (na mjesecu) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ ٱلْقَمَرِ

    from the moon (od mjeseca) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    نُورُ ٱلْقَمَرِ

    the light of the moon (svjetlost mjeseca) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    قَمَرُهُ

    his/its moon (njegov mjesec) · Bosnian nominative

Practice sentences

  • subject (nominative)

    ظَهَرَ الْقَمَرُ

    The moon appeared.

  • object (accusative)

    رَأَيْنَا الْقَمَرَ

    We saw the moon.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    نَظَرَ الطِّفْلُ إِلَى الْقَمَرِ

    The child looked at the moon.

  • possessor (genitive)

    نُورُ الْقَمَرِ جَمِيلٌ

    The light of the moon is beautiful.
