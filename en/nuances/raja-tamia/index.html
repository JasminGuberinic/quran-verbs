Nuances

To hope and to long for

رَجَا · طَمِعَ

What they share

Both verbs speak of expecting some good. According to ar-Rāġib, rajāʾ is an expectation of what brings joy, while ṭamaʿ is the soul's inclination towards a thing out of desire for it.

How to tell them apart

To hope, with grounds, most often for the meeting with Allah and the hereafter → رَجَا; to long for, to covet — praiseworthy when it is longing for Allah's forgiveness, blameworthy when groundless or from a sick heart → طَمِعَ.

رَجَا to hope

22× in the Quran · Verb page →

Hope that has grounds and expects joy. In the Quran most often hope for the meeting with Allah, for the Last Day and Allah's mercy; “those who expect not the meeting with Us” (10:7) are those who deny it.

طَمِعَ to long for, to covet

8× in the Quran · Verb page →

The soul's reaching for something. Turned to Allah's forgiveness, the Quran praises it; when groundless or from a sick heart, it blames it.
