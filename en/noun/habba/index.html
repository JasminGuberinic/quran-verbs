What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الْحَبَّةُ

    the grain (zrno) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الْحَبَّةَ

    the grain (zrno) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي الْحَبَّةِ

    in the grain (u zrnu) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ الْحَبَّةِ

    from the grain (iz zrna) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    حَجْمُ الْحَبَّةِ

    the size of the grain (veličina zrna) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    حَبَّتُهُ

    his/its grain (njegovo zrno) · Bosnian nominative

Practice sentences

    سَقَطَتْ حَبَّةٌ عَلَى الْأَرْضِ

    A grain fell on the ground.

  • object (accusative)

    أَكَلَ الطَّائِرُ الْحَبَّةَ

    The bird ate the grain.

    The tree started from a small grain.

  • possessor (genitive)

    حَجْمُ الْحَبَّةِ صَغِيرٌ

    The size of the grain is small.
