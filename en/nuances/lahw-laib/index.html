Diversion and play

لَهْو · لَعِب

What they share

Both words describe what is not earnest, and the Quran often joins them when speaking of this world. According to ar-Rāġib, لَهْو is what occupies a person and distracts him from what concerns him, while لَعِب is an act done without a sound aim.

How to tell them apart

What occupies and distracts from what matters, amusement → لَهْو; play, an act with no earnest aim → لَعِب.

لَهْو diversion, amusement

10× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, what occupies a person and distracts him from what matters to him; any enjoyment is also called so. In the Quran it also stands alone — for what draws people away from prayer (62:11).

لَعِب play

8× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the root is flowing saliva (لُعَاب), and play is an act aimed at no sound purpose. In the Quran the noun لَعِب never stands alone (the verb does, e.g. 12:12): it comes with لَهْو or with mockery (هُزُو), when disbelievers take religion and prayer as play.
