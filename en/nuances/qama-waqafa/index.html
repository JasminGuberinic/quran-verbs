Standing up and being made to stand

قَامَ · وَقَفَ

What they share

Both words speak of standing. They differ in who stands and why: rising and standing by one's own movement or strength (and so standing to a task, staying steadfast), or being halted and set before someone.

How to tell them apart

Rising, standing, standing upright — of people and of the Hour; in ar-Rāġib by choice, by subjection, and in the sense of ‘taking care of something’ → قَامَ; halting someone and setting him in place — in the Quran all three times transitive, of the disbelievers on the Day of Judgement → وَقَفَ.

قَامَ to stand, to rise

33× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, qiyām is of several kinds: standing by subjection (a plant that stands) or by choice (a man in prayer), keeping and caring for something (‘qāʾim’ over a thing = the one who guards it) and resolve (‘when you rise for prayer’). In the Quran people rise, but the Hour also ‘rises’ (the Qiyāma); once it means stopping in one's place, in the dark (2:20).

وَقَفَ to halt, to make stand

3× in the Quran

In ar-Rāġib ‘waqaftu l-qawma’ means ‘I halted the people’, and mawqif is the place where a man stands. In the Quran the verb is all three times transitive: the disbelievers do not stand by themselves, they are halted and set in place — before the Fire, before their Lord — to be questioned.
