Nuances

Fasting and abstaining

صِيَام · صَوْم · صَامَ

What they share

All are words of the root ṣ-w-m: both nouns are verbal nouns of صَامَ. According to ar-Rāġib, ṣawm at root means holding back from something — food, speech or movement; in the Sharia it is the fast.

How to tell them apart

The prescribed fast, in the Quran always within the rulings → صِيَام; its one place, Mary's vow, which tafsir mostly explains as silence → صَوْم; to fast (the verb) → صَامَ.

صِيَام fasting

9× in the Quran · Noun page →

The prescribed fast: the fast of Ramadan and the fast of expiation in the rulings. In the Quran (our observation) always in this legal sense.

صَوْم abstaining; fast

1× in the Quran

Only once in the Quran: Mary's vow. It is followed at once by “and may not speak this day to any mortal” (19:26), so tafsir mostly explains it as silence; some add that fasting in their law included silence.

صَامَ to fast

2× in the Quran

The verb both nouns come from. Only twice in the Quran, both times in the ayat on fasting (2:184–185).
