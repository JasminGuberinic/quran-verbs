Nuances

Replacing, altering, distorting

بَدَّلَ · غَيَّرَ · حَرَّفَ

What they share

All three words mean making something not stay as it was. They differ in what happens: the thing is replaced by another, its state is changed, or a word is bent away from its place.

How to tell them apart

To put one thing in another's place, to replace → بَدَّلَ; to alter a thing's state or form → غَيَّرَ; to distort a word, to tilt it from its place → حَرَّفَ.

بَدَّلَ to replace, to exchange

23× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, tabdīl is putting one thing in another's place; at times it is said of change in general, even with no replacement. So a word that was told is replaced by another, and on the Last Day the earth is changed from its state.

غَيَّرَ to alter (a state)

4× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, taġyīr is said two ways: changing a thing's form, not its essence, and replacing it with another — and it is under the second that he sets 8:53. In the Quran it is often a change of state: Allah does not alter the favour He gave a people until they alter what is in themselves — as befits Him.

حَرَّفَ to distort (words)

4× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ḥarf is the edge of a thing, and taḥrīf is tilting, as when a pen is cut slantwise; to distort speech is to set it on an edge of possibility, so that it can be taken two ways. In the Quran it is always said of some of the people of the Book; whether the wording or the meaning is meant, the mufassirun discuss.
