Nuances

Wholesome and lawful

حَلَال · طَيِّب

What they share

Both words describe in the Quran what may be taken, most often food, and they often stand together (‘lawful and good’). They differ in whether one looks at the permission, or at the goodness and cleanness of the thing itself.

How to tell them apart

What is permitted, ‘untied’ from prohibition → حَلَال; what is good and clean, pleasing to the senses and the soul, said of people and words too → طَيِّب.

حَلَال lawful

6× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, the root is untying a knot; from it is borrowed ‘to become lawful’. In the Quran حَلَال stands beside طَيِّب in four of its six places; in the other two it blames those who declare of their own accord what is lawful and what forbidden.

طَيِّب good, wholesome

46× in the Quran · Adjective page →

According to ar-Rāġib, طَيِّب is at root what pleases the senses and the soul; food is ṭayyib in the Law when it is taken as permitted, in the permitted amount and from the permitted place. It is said of land, words and people too — clean of what is foul.
