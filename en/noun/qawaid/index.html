What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    ٱلْقَوَٰعِدُ

    the foundations (temelji) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    ٱلْقَوَاعِدَ

    the foundations (temelje) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ ٱلْقَوَاعِدِ

    from the foundations (iz temelja) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي ٱلْقَوَاعِدِ

    in the foundations (na temeljima) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    بِنَاءُ ٱلْقَوَاعِدِ

    the building of the foundations (gradnja temelja) · Bosnian genitive

Practice sentences

  • subject (nominative)

    كَانَتْ قَوَاعِدُ الْمَبْنَى قَوِيَّةً

    The foundations of the building were strong.

  • object (accusative)

    فَحَصَ الْمُهَنْدِسُ قَوَاعِدَ الْمَبْنَى

    The engineer examined the foundations of the building.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    يَقُومُ الْمَبْنَى عَلَى قَوَاعِدَ قَوِيَّةٍ

    The building stands on strong foundations.

  • possessor (genitive)

    بِنَاءُ الْقَوَاعِدِ صَعْبٌ

    The building of the foundations is difficult.
