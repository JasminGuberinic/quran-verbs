Nuances

Indecency and wrongdoing

فَاحِشَة · مُنكَر

What they share

Both words speak of foul deeds. According to ar-Rāġib, فَاحِشَة is what is grave in its ugliness, while مُنكَر is anything sound reason judges ugly, or the Sharia where reason hesitates.

How to tell them apart

A deed or word of grave ugliness, often fornication and lewdness → فَاحِشَة; whatever reason or the Sharia rejects, the opposite of the right (مَعْرُوف) → مُنكَر.

فَاحِشَة immorality, indecency

17× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, a deed or word whose ugliness is grave; a ‘clear فَاحِشَة’ he calls a euphemism for fornication. In the Quran it is used of fornication, of the deed of Lot's people, and of any grave foul deed.

مُنكَر wrongdoing, what is wrong

18× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, every deed sound reason judges ugly, or about which reason hesitates and the Sharia then judges ugly. Most often beside مَعْرُوف: enjoining the right and forbidding the wrong.
