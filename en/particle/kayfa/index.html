كَيْفَhow?

83× in the Quran

What it does

A question word asking about a state or manner. It is a noun: Ibn Hišām shows this by a preposition that can come before it (عَلَى كَيْفَ تَبِيعُ), by a noun that can stand in for it (كَيْفَ أَنتَ، أَصَحِيحٌ أَمْ سَقِيمٌ) and by its being a predicate (كَيْفَ أَنتَ – ‘how are you?’), so it is not a particle; and since it stands right beside a verb, it is not a verb either. It changes no case or mood in the word after it.

Uses

  1. Asking ‘how?’
  2. Inside a sentence: ‘see how…’
  3. ‘However…’: كَيْفَ يَشَآءُ

Asking ‘how?’

question word · corpus: 30×

It asks about a state or manner. Often the question seeks no information: Ibn Hišām says 2:28 is put as an expression of wonder.

Inside a sentence: ‘see how…’

question word

It mostly stands after a verb of looking, seeing or knowing (ٱنظُرْ، أَلَمْ تَرَ) and with the sentence after it says what is to be seen. Ibn Hišām explains 88:17: the verb of looking is ‘suspended’ there (muʿallaq) – the question after it is not its object – and كَيْفَ with its sentence stands in (badal) for ٱلْإِبِلِ: what is to be regarded is the camels, that is, how they were made. The corpus files this use with no question tag.

‘However…’: كَيْفَ يَشَآءُ

condition

Ibn Hišām also lists a conditional كَيْفَ, with two verbs of the same sense; as examples he takes 5:64, 3:6 and 30:48, where the answer is left out because what comes before says it: “He bestoweth as He will” (5:64). The Basrans (except Quṭrub) do not allow the jussive after it, the Kufans and Quṭrub allow it; in the Quran the verb after it is indicative.

The verb after it stays indicative.
