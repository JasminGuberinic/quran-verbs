Nuances

Human being

إِنْسَان · بَشَر · رَجُل · اِمْرُؤ · نَاس

What they share

All five words speak of a human being or of people, but each looks from a different angle: human nature, the visible bodily creature, the male, the single individual, or people as a community.

How to tell them apart

Human nature → إِنْسَان; the visible, bodily creature → بَشَر; the male (as against a woman or an angel) → رَجُل; the single person, as the Quran uses it (“every man…”, 52:21) → اِمْرُؤ; people as a community → نَاس.

إِنْسَان human being, man

71× in the Quran · Noun page →

Man as a species, with his nature and his weaknesses. Ar-Rāġib links the word to uns — familiarity and company, since man cannot survive without others — and also mentions the view that it comes from nisyān (forgetting). The Quran mostly uses it when it says what man is like.

بَشَر human being, mortal

37× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the word comes from bashara (skin): man as he appears outwardly, a creature of body and skin. The Quran uses it where man's bodily creation is meant, or where a messenger is said to be a man like others. The same form serves for one and for many.

رَجُل man (male)

57× in the Quran · Noun page →

A male person. Ar-Rāġib says the word belongs to the male among people; in the Quran it stands against woman, and also against angel. In the plural (رِجَال) it often praises men of firm character.

اِمْرُؤ a person, an individual

11× in the Quran · Noun page →

A person, a human being (a man; the feminine is imraʾa). In the Quran it appears mainly where the individual is in view: personal accountability and rulings about one person.

نَاس people, mankind

241× in the Quran · Noun page →

People as a group, mankind as a whole; the word of address to all people (“O mankind!”, 2:21). Ar-Rāġib notes that it sometimes means only people of worth, as in 2:13.
