Particles · question word · equalization · urging

أَis it…?, do…?

513× in the Quran

What it does

The question hamza opens a question and changes no case or mood. According to Ibn Hišām it differs from هَلْ in that it can also ask ‘this or that’ (with أَمْ), stands before a negation (أَلَمْ، أَفَلَا) and before a conjunction: أَفَلَا، أَوَلَمْ.

Uses

  1. Asking ‘is it…?’
  2. A question that affirms or rebukes
  3. ‘Whether… or…: all one’
  4. أَلَا: offering and urging, ‘will you not…?’

Asking ‘is it…?’

question word

It asks whether something is so; when it asks about a person or thing, that word comes right after the hamza (ءَأَنتَ فَعَلْتَ – “Is it thou who hast done this”, 21:62).

A question that affirms or rebukes

question word

A question often does not ask for information (and in Allah's speech it never seeks what He does not know): it leads the listener to admit (taqrīr – أَلَمْ ‘have We not?’), or it rebukes and denies (inkār, tawbīḫ). The hamza stands before وَ and فَ: أَوَلَمْ، أَفَلَا. According to Sībawayh and most grammarians (al-jumhūr) the conjunction belongs to the sentence after it, and the hamza is put before it because a question goes first; az-Zamaḫšarī held that a sentence is left out between them (Ibn Hišām gives both views).

‘Whether… or…: all one’

equalization · corpus: 6×

After سَوَآءٌ the hamza no longer asks: with أَمْ it makes two choices equal (hamzat at-taswiya), and the clause is understood as a verbal noun: “Whether thou warn them or thou warn them not it is all one for them” (2:6), i.e. your warning and your not warning are alike to them.

أَلَا: offering and urging, ‘will you not…?’

urging

Ibn Hišām counts the أَلَا of offering (ʿarḍ, a gentle request) and urging (taḥḍīḍ, an insistent one) as one word, the fifth meaning of أَلَا, and says it comes only before a verbal sentence; he cites 24:22 and 9:13. The corpus files it as the question hamza and لَا, so it is here.

  • Ibn Hišām al-Anṣārī, Muġnī al-labīb ʿan kutub al-aʿārīb
