Going away and passing on

ذَهَبَ · مَضَى

What they share

Both words speak of going. Ar-Rāġib even explains one by the other; the difference lies more in usage: going away from something and vanishing, or passing through and carrying on to the end.

How to tell them apart

To go away from someone or something, to vanish; with بِـ ‘to take away’ → ذَهَبَ; to pass through and go on, and of peoples and customs — to be past → مَضَى.

ذَهَبَ to go (away), to vanish

35× in the Quran · Verb page →

Ar-Rāġib explains ḏahāb by the word muḍiyy (passing) and says it is used both of things and of meanings. In the Quran a prophet goes to Pharaoh, and fear, light and evil go away; with بِـ (ذَهَبَ بِـ) it means ‘to take away’.

مَضَى to pass on, to go on

4× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, muḍiyy is nafāḏ — passing through and going on; it is said of things and of events. In the Quran the example and the way of earlier peoples have passed, and Lot and his family are told to go on.
