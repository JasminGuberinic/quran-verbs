Praise and thanks

حَمْد · شَكَرَ

What they share

Both words acknowledge good; according to ar-Rāġib, they differ in range.

How to tell them apart

Praise for excellence and perfection, even when no favour was done to us → حَمْد; thanks for a favour received → شَكَرَ. Every thanks is praise, but not every praise is thanks.

حَمْد praise

43× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, praising for excellence; narrower than madḥ (commendation) and wider than šukr, since it is not tied to a favour received.

شَكَرَ to thank, to be grateful

46× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, šukr is picturing a favour to oneself and showing it. Where Allah is called شَكُور (64:17), ar-Rāġib explains it as His favour to His servants and His rewarding of their worship — and شَاكِر (2:158) is understood the same way. Its opposite, he notes, is kufr — forgetting and concealing the favour.
