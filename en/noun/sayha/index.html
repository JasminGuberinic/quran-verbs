What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    ٱلصَّيْحَةُ

    the shout (krik) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    ٱلصَّيْحَةَ

    the shout (krik) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي الصَّيْحَةِ

    in the shout (u kriku) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ الصَّيْحَةِ

    from the shout (od krika) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    قُوَّةُ الصَّيْحَةِ

    the strength of the shout (jačina krika) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    صَيْحَتُهُ

    his/its shout (njegov krik) · Bosnian nominative

Practice sentences

  • subject (nominative)

    الصَّيْحَةُ أَيْقَظَتِ الطِّفْلَ

    The shout awakened the child.

  • object (accusative)

    سَمِعَ الْجِيرَانُ الصَّيْحَةَ

    The neighbors heard the shout.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    خَافَ الطِّفْلُ مِنَ الصَّيْحَةِ

    The child was afraid of the shout.

  • possessor (genitive)

    قُوَّةُ الصَّيْحَةِ كَبِيرَةٌ

    The strength of the shout is great.
