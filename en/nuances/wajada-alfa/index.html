Nuances

Finding and coming upon

وَجَدَ · أَلْفَى

What they share

Both words mean to find. ar-Rāġib explains alfā simply by wajada; the difference is in how wide the word is.

How to tell them apart

To find — by the senses, the mind or a feeling, even to have something within reach; the widest word → وَجَدَ; to come upon someone in the state they were already in — in the Quran fathers on their way and a husband at the door → أَلْفَى.

وَجَدَ to find

106× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, finding is of several kinds: by one of the five senses; by appetite (finding satiety) and by anger (grief, displeasure); and by the mind, as knowing Allah. When ascribed to Allah it means pure knowledge, as befits Him, for He is above limbs and instruments. The word also means having something within reach.

أَلْفَى to come upon

3× in the Quran

ar-Rāġib explains alfā with one word: ‘I found’. In the Quran (3×) it always means coming upon someone in the state they were already in: twice it is about fathers, once Joseph and the woman meet her husband standing by the door. The same saying about the fathers also comes with wajada (5:104, 31:21), so the difference is small.
