What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الدُّخَانُ

    the smoke (dim) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الدُّخَانَ

    the smoke (dim) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    بِالدُّخَانِ

    with the smoke (dimom) · Bosnian instrumental

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي الدُّخَانِ

    in the smoke (u dimu) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    رَائِحَةُ الدُّخَانِ

    the odor of the smoke (miris dima) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    دُخَانُهُ

    his/its smoke (njegov dim) · Bosnian nominative
