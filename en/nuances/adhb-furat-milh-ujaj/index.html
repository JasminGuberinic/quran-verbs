Nuances

Fresh and sweet, salt and bitter water

عَذْب · فُرَات · مِلْح · أُجَاج

What they share

Four words describe the taste of water, in two pairs: fresh and sweet water against salt and bitter. Where they stand together in the Quran, many explain the second word of each pair as strengthening the first.

How to tell them apart

Fresh, good and cool water → عَذْب; sweet water, alone or beside ʿadhb → فُرَات; salt water → مِلْح; very salt, bitter, ‘blazing’ like fire → أُجَاج.

عَذْب fresh, palatable

2× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, ʿadhb is good, cool water — water that is pleasant. In the Quran it is only in the two ayat of the two seas, both times beside furāt.

فُرَات sweet

3× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, furāt is sweet water; it is said of one water and of many. Beside ʿadhb it is often explained as strengthening its sense, and alone in 77:27 it describes the water Allah gives people to drink.

مِلْح salt

2× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, milḥ is water whose taste has changed in the known way; the Arabs rarely say ‘māliḥ’. In the Quran it is only beside ujāj, in the two ayat of the two seas.

أُجَاج bitter

3× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, ujāj is very salty and ‘hot’ water, from ajīj an-nār — the blaze of fire. It stands alone in 56:70: the water people drink Allah could, if He willed, make bitter.
