Going beyond bounds and squandering

أَسْرَفَ · بَذَّرَ

What they share

Both words blame a lack of measure, most often with wealth. They differ in whether one looks at crossing the limit in any act, or at scattering wealth so that it is lost.

How to tell them apart

Crossing the limit in anything — spending, eating, retaliation, sin → أَسْرَفَ; scattering wealth like seed strewn about, so that it is wasted → بَذَّرَ.

أَسْرَفَ to exceed the bounds, to be prodigal

6× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, isrāf is exceeding the limit in any act a person does, though it is best known in spending; it may concern the amount, and it may concern the manner. In the Quran one so exceeds in spending, in eating and drinking, and in retaliation for one slain.

بَذَّرَ to squander

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, tabḏīr is scattering; at root it is casting seed, and it is borrowed for anyone who wastes his wealth. The verb is in the Quran only once, and the next ayah calls the squanderers (مُبَذِّرِين) brothers of the devils (17:27).
