Nuances

River, spring, wellspring

نَهَر · عَيْن · يَنْبُوع

What they share

All three words speak of water that runs on the earth or comes out of it. They differ in the place: the channel the water runs in, the spring where the water shows, or the source it bursts out of.

How to tell them apart

A channel of running water, a river — on earth and in the Garden → نَهَر; a spring, water rising to the surface — named after the eye → عَيْن; a wellspring water bursts out of, only twice → يَنْبُوع.

نَهَر river

54× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, nahr is the course of overflowing water, and Allah made it a likeness of His bounty that pours forth upon people in the Garden. In the Quran these are mostly the rivers of the Garden ‘flowing beneath’, but also rivers on earth (2:249, 43:51).

عَيْن spring

57× in the Quran · Noun page →

The same word means ‘eye’; according to ar-Rāġib, a source of water is called ʿayn by likeness to the eye, for the water in it, and ‘māʾ maʿīn’ is water the eye can see. It is the place where water comes to the surface, not the channel it runs in.

يَنْبُوع wellspring

2× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, nabʿ is water coming out of a spring, and yanbūʿ the spring water comes out of; the word brings out the water's coming out. In the Quran it is only twice: rainwater Allah leads through the earth as wellsprings (39:21), and the disbelievers' demand that a wellspring burst from the earth for them (17:90).
