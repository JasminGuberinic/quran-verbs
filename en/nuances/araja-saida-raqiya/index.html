Nuances

Ascending, rising, climbing

عَرَجَ · صَعِدَ · رَقِيَ

What they share

All three words mean moving upwards. They differ in the way and in what rises: going up by ascents, going to a high place, or climbing as on steps and a ladder.

How to tell them apart

Going in ascent, as by stairways (مَعَارِج) — the angels, the Spirit, ‘the matter’ to Allah → عَرَجَ; going to a high place, and figuratively what reaches Allah from the servant → صَعِدَ; climbing steps and a ladder → رَقِيَ.

عَرَجَ to ascend

5× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ʿurūǧ is going in ascent; مَعَارِج are the ascents, the ways up. In the Quran the angels and the Spirit ascend, ‘the matter’ ascends to Allah, and whatever rises into the sky — as befits Him.

صَعِدَ to rise, to go up

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, ṣuʿūd is going into a high place. He says it is used figuratively for what reaches Allah from the servant, just as nuzūl is used for what reaches the servant from Allah.

رَقِيَ to climb

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, climbing on steps and a ladder. In the Quran the verb in this form occurs once, in 17:93, where the deniers demand that the Prophet climb into the sky (38:10 has the related irtaqā, “let them ascend”).
