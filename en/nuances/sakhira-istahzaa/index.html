Nuances

Ridiculing and scoffing

سَخِرَ · اِسْتَهْزَأَ

What they share

Both words mean mocking and belittling. They differ in what is mocked and in the image the word carries: ridiculing people, or making a jest of what deserves to be taken seriously.

How to tell them apart

Ridiculing someone — with min, and when the object is named it is always people → سَخِرَ; making a jest — with bi-, of people, of Allah's signs, of the warning; ar-Rāġib: seeking out a hidden jest → اِسْتَهْزَأَ.

سَخِرَ to ridicule, to deride

11× in the Quran · Verb page →

Ar-Rāġib mentions it within his entry on subjection (taṣḫīr): ‘saḫirtu minhu’ means to ridicule someone. In the Quran (7 ayat) the verb takes min in six of them, and when it is said whom they ridicule, it is always people: Noah, the believers, messengers; in 37:12 it stands alone (‘you marvel, and they ridicule’). When it is said of Allah (9:79), ar-Rāġib reads it as he reads istihzāʾ: He requites them for their ridicule — as befits Him.

اِسْتَهْزَأَ to scoff, to make a jest of

21× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, hazʾ is joking in secret, and istihzāʾ is seeking out such a jest. He says istihzāʾ cannot truly come from Allah, as play cannot; 2:15 means that He requites them with the requital of scoffing — as befits Him. In the Quran it takes bi-, and its object is messengers, Allah's signs, even ‘Allah and His signs and His messenger’ (9:65).
