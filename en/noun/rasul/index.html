What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    ٱلرَّسُولُ

    the messenger (poslanik) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    ٱلرَّسُولَ

    the messenger (poslanika) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ ٱلرَّسُولِ

    from the messenger (od poslanika) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    عَلَى ٱلرَّسُولِ

    on the messenger (na poslaniku) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    كَلَامُ ٱلرَّسُولِ

    the speech of the messenger (govor poslanika) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    رُسُلُهُم from the Quran

    their messengers (njihovi poslanici) · Bosnian nominative

Practice sentences

    جَاءَ رَسُولٌ مِنَ الْمَلِكِ

    A messenger came from the king.

  • object (accusative)

    أَرْسَلَ الْمَلِكُ رَسُولًا

    The king sent a messenger.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    سَلَّمْتُ الرِّسَالَةَ إِلَى الرَّسُولِ

    I handed the letter to the messenger.

  • possessor (genitive)

    رَأَيْتُ حِصَانَ رَسُولِ الْمَلِكِ

    I saw the horse of the king's messenger.
