Nuances

Lamp and lantern

سِرَاج · مِصْبَاح

What they share

Both words mean a kindled source of light. ar-Rāġib says the sirāj is also called a misbāḥ; the Quran still uses them for different things.

How to tell them apart

A lamp burning with wick and oil, then anything that shines; in the Quran the sun and the Prophet → سِرَاج; the lantern in the niche of the parable of light, and the stars adorning the nearest heaven → مِصْبَاح.

سِرَاج lamp

4× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, sirāj is what shines by wick and oil, and it is said of anything that gives light; he cites 71:16 and 78:13 of the sun. In the Quran the sun is so called, and the Prophet too, as an illuminating lamp — a light that reaches others.

مِصْبَاح lantern

4× in the Quran · Noun page →

ar-Rāġib lists the word under ṣubḥ (morning), says the sirāj is also called misbāḥ, and that maṣābīḥ are the prominent stars. In the Quran it is the lantern in the niche of the parable of Allah's light, as befits Him, and the stars that adorn the nearest heaven.
