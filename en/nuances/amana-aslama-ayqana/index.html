Nuances

To believe, to submit, to be certain

آمَنَ · أَسْلَمَ · أَيْقَنَ

What they share

All three verbs describe the believer's stance towards the truth; they differ in whether the heart, submission or firmness of conviction is in view.

How to tell them apart

Belief in the heart, affirming the truth → آمَنَ; submitting to Allah — at the least outward acceptance of the religion, at the fullest complete inward and outward surrender → أَسْلَمَ; firm conviction free of doubt → أَيْقَنَ.

آمَنَ to believe

537× in the Quran · Verb page →

To believe and affirm the truth in the heart, with trust; from the same root as safety (أَمْن). The most frequent of the three.

أَسْلَمَ to submit, to accept Islam

22× in the Quran · Verb page →

To submit to Allah and accept His religion. According to ar-Rāġib, islām is of two kinds: below īmān — the tongue's acknowledgement alone (49:14) — and above it, when heart, deed and complete submission join the tongue (Ibrāhīm).

أَيْقَنَ to be certain

12× in the Quran · Verb page →

To be firmly convinced, without any doubt. According to ar-Rāġib, yaqīn is a degree of knowledge above maʿrifa: stillness of understanding with firm judgement. Most often said of conviction in the hereafter and in the signs.
