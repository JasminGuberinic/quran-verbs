Nuances

Soul and spirit

نَفْس · رُوح

What they share

Both words speak of that by which a person is alive. According to ar-Rāġib, nafs is sometimes said for rūḥ, and rūḥ is also used as a name for the nafs; yet the Quran uses them very differently.

How to tell them apart

The soul, the person's own self — it dies, desires and answers for its deeds → نَفْس; the spirit that Allah breathes in, sends with revelation, or the angels so named → رُوح.

نَفْس soul, self

295× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, nafs is also said for the rūḥ, as in 6:93: “Deliver up your souls.” In the Quran the nafs is mostly the person's own self: the soul that tastes death, that desires and must be held back, and that answers for what it has earned. When it is said of Allah (“Allah biddeth you beware of Himself”, 3:28), ar-Rāġib says it means His very Being, as befits Him.

رُوح spirit

21× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the rūḥ is that by which life, movement, gaining benefit and warding off harm come about; when Allah calls it “My Spirit” (15:29), it is ascription of possession, singling it out for honour. The noblest angels are also called rūḥ: Jibrīl is “the True Spirit” (26:193). In the Quran the rūḥ is always ascribed to Allah (“of My Spirit”, 15:29; “by command of my Lord”, 17:85) and is not spoken of as the self that answers for deeds.
