Nuances

One and the One

وَاحِد · أَحَد

What they share

Both words mean ‘one’ and both are said of Allah. They differ in use: one is the ordinary number and adjective said of anything that is one, the other — when it stands alone as a description — is kept for Allah alone, and otherwise means ‘anyone’ or ‘one of’.

How to tell them apart

One — the number and adjective for anything single (one gate, one food), and of Allah: God is one → وَاحِد; the One — as a description standing alone, only of Allah (112:1); otherwise ‘anyone’ in a negation and ‘one of’ in a construct → أَحَد.

وَاحِد one

30× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, ‘one’ in the true sense is what has no parts, and then the word is said of everything that exists — one kind, one body, one sun. When Allah is described as wāḥid, it means that neither division nor multiplicity applies to Him — as befits Him. In the Quran it also stands with ordinary things: one gate, one food.

أَحَد the One; anyone; one of

74× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, aḥad is used in two ways. In a negation it covers all rational beings, one and more: ‘there is no one’. In an affirmation, in a construct, it has the sense of ‘the first’ (12:41), or stands with the tens — and as a description standing alone it is said only of Allah (112:1), as befits Him. So in the Quran it is most often ‘anyone, no one’.
