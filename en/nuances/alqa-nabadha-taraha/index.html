Nuances

Throwing, flinging away, casting off

أَلْقَى · نَبَذَ · طَرَحَ

What they share

All three words mean letting something go from the hand by throwing it. They differ in what goes with the throwing: plain throwing (also ‘casting’ a word, a greeting or affection to someone), throwing away a thing held of little account, or throwing that puts it far off.

How to tell them apart

Throwing, casting down; also casting a word, a greeting or affection to someone → أَلْقَى; flinging away as worthless — ‘behind their backs’ → نَبَذَ; casting off far away — only 12:9 → طَرَحَ.

أَلْقَى to throw, to cast

71× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ilqāʾ is at root throwing a thing where you meet it, that is, see it, and it then became a name for any throwing. One also ‘casts’ to someone a word, a greeting or affection, and ‘alqā s-samʿ’ (50:37) is listening closely. The word itself says nothing of the worth of what is thrown — in that it differs from nabdh.

نَبَذَ to fling away

10× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, nabdh is throwing a thing because it is held of little account — as one throws away a worn-out sandal. So the Quran uses it of those who ‘flung behind their backs’ the Book or a covenant. Of 8:58 he himself says the use is like ilqāʾ: ‘cast the peace to them’ — the pact is tossed to them, not made firm.

طَرَحَ to cast off, far away

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, ṭarḥ is throwing a thing and putting it far off; aṭ-ṭarūḥ is a far place. The verb is in the Quran only once, in the counsel of Joseph's brothers: to kill him or to cast him off into some land, to put him far from their father.
