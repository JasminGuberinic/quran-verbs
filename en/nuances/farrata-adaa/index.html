Nuances

Neglecting and letting go to waste

فَرَّطَ · أَضَاعَ

What they share

Both words speak of something of worth being lost through neglect. They differ in the angle: falling short in what one should have done, or letting what one has go to waste.

How to tell them apart

Falling short, not doing what is due — often in regret or reproach (6:31, 39:56, 12:80) → فَرَّطَ; letting something go to waste — in the Quran mostly in ‘Allah does not let the reward… be lost’ → أَضَاعَ.

فَرَّطَ to neglect, to fall short

5× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the root is ‘going ahead’: ifrāṭ is overdoing it and tafrīṭ falling short. So farraṭa is ‘not doing what was due’. In the Quran it is often heard in regret or reproach (6:31, 39:56, 12:80), but also in the denial that anything is neglected: the angels ‘do not neglect’ (6:61), and nothing is left out of the Book (6:38).

أَضَاعَ to let go to waste, to let be lost

10× in the Quran · Verb page →

Aḍāʿa is letting something that exists go to waste. In the Quran nine of the ten places are a promise: Allah, as befits Him, does not let faith, work or reward be lost; only in 19:59 do people let something go to waste — the prayer. Ar-Rāġib cites the promise ayat (3:195, 18:30, 2:143).
