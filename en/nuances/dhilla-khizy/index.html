Nuances

Abasement and disgrace

ذِلَّة · ذُلّ · خِزْي

What they share

All three words speak of a person brought low. According to ar-Rāġib, both lowliness (ذُلّ) and shame (خِزْي) may come from the person himself or from another: for lowliness, from oneself it is praised, imposed by another it is blamed.

How to tell them apart

Abasement imposed, a punishment → ذِلَّة; lowliness — praised when one's own (towards parents), blamed when imposed (42:45) → ذُلّ; disgrace, being shamed before others → خِزْي.

ذِلَّة humiliation, abasement

7× in the Quran · Noun page →

Abasement that is imposed — in the Quran a punishment: “humiliation and wretchedness were stamped upon them” (2:61), “ignominy overtaketh them” (10:27).

ذُلّ humility; lowliness

3× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, lowliness is praised when a person takes it on himself — as towards parents: “lower unto them the wing of submission” (17:24), be before them as one subdued, he says. Allah is far above any weakness: He needs no protector out of lowliness (17:111).

خِزْي disgrace

11× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, a brokenness that comes over a person — from himself (excessive shame) or from another (being treated with contempt). In the Quran it is mostly disgrace as punishment, in this world and the next.
