Particles · vocative

يَاO!

361× in the Quran

What it does

The word of calling: يَٰمُوسَىٰ ‘O Moses’. The form of the one called depends on the kind of noun: a proper name, or one particular person called, takes a ḍamma, a phrase in a genitive construction the accusative. A noun with ٱلْ is called through أَيُّهَا. A call often comes without يَا: رَبَّنَا ‘Our Lord’.

Uses

  1. Calling by name: ‘O Mary!’
  2. Calling a phrase: ‘O children of Adam!’
  3. يَٰٓأَيُّهَا: ‘O you who…’
  4. يَا before لَيْتَ: ‘Oh, I wish…’

Calling by name: ‘O Mary!’

vocative

A name, or one particular person called, takes a ḍamma without tanwīn: يَٰمَرْيَمُ. The grammarians say it is ‘built on the ḍamma in the place of an accusative’; the corpus tags it nominative.

Calling a phrase: ‘O children of Adam!’

vocative

When the one called is the first term of a genitive construction, it is accusative: يَٰبَنِىٓ ءَادَمَ، يَٰٓأَهْلَ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ.

The word after it is accusative.

يَٰٓأَيُّهَا: ‘O you who…’

vocative

A noun with ٱلْ, apart from the name Allah, is not called directly: أَيُّهَا (أَيَّتُهَا for the feminine) stands between it and يَا, and the noun itself is nominative. The most frequent call of the Quran.

يَا before لَيْتَ: ‘Oh, I wish…’

vocative

يَا also stands before a word that is not a noun (يَٰلَيْتَنِى). Ibn Hišām reports two views: either the one called is understood there, or يَا only draws attention (tanbīh).
