Nuances

Splitting apart and differing

تَفَرَّقَ · اخْتَلَفَ

What they share

Both words describe a community ceasing to be one. They differ in what comes apart: people and groups that separate from one another, or words and paths that go different ways.

How to tell them apart

Splitting apart — into groups or, of spouses, parting → تَفَرَّقَ; differing, each taking another way in word or view — Allah judges it → اخْتَلَفَ.

تَفَرَّقَ to split apart, to separate

8× in the Quran · Verb page →

The root sense, according to ar-Rāġib, is separation (infiṣāl): a firqa is a group set apart, and tafrīq is said of breaking up a community's unity and word. Tafarraqa is one whole coming apart into pieces: the Quran uses it to forbid a community's break-up (3:103), and also of spouses who part (4:130).

اخْتَلَفَ to differ, to disagree

35× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ikhtilāf is each one taking a way other than the other's, in state or in word; since difference in word can lead to quarrel, the word was borrowed for dispute. He cites 3:105, where both verbs stand together. In the Quran people mostly differ over the Book, the truth and religion, and Allah judges in what they differed — as befits Him.
