Nuances

Palace and lofty edifice

قَصْر · صَرْح

What they share

Both words name a grand building. They differ in what stands out: a building joined part to part, or an edifice high and adorned.

How to tell them apart

A palace, a building joined part to part, like those people build or leave in ruins → قَصْر; a high, adorned house — Solomon's hall of glass, the edifice Pharaoh asks for → صَرْح.

قَصْر palace, castle

4× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the verb قَصَرَ also means ‘to join part to part’, and from it قَصْر takes its name; the plural is قُصُور. In the Quran they are mostly the palaces of people of this world: Thamud's in the plains (7:74), and the high-built palaces of destroyed towns (22:45); once it is a simile for the sparks thrown up by the Fire (77:32).

صَرْح lofty edifice

4× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, صَرْح is a high, adorned house, so named as being ‘pure’, free of admixture. In the Quran it is Solomon's hall made smooth with glass (27:44), and the edifice Pharaoh asks Haman to build, hoping to look up at the god of Moses (28:38) and to reach the ways (40:36).
