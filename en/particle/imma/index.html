إِمَّاeither… or…

23× in the Quran

What it does

A word of choice between two things, almost always repeated: إِمَّا… وَإِمَّا…. It changes no case or mood: the word after it has the case it has in the sentence. Ibn Hišām lists five meanings (doubt, vagueness, choice, permission, setting out) – the same as أَوْ, except that with إِمَّا the speech is built on that meaning from the start, while with أَوْ it arises on the way. The conditional إِمَّا (فَإِمَّا يَأْتِيَنَّكُم) is another word: the conditional إِنْ with an added مَا.

Uses

  1. Choice: ‘either this or that’
  2. Vagueness: which of the two is not said
  3. Setting out: ‘whether… or…’
  4. Condition: إِنْ + مَا, ‘if…’

Choice: ‘either this or that’

alternative (either… or)

It lets the hearer choose one of two (taḫyīr). After إِمَّا there is often أَن with a verb.

Vagueness: which of the two is not said

alternative (either… or)

The speaker knows but does not say which of the two it will be (ibhām). Ibn Hišām says 9:106 is vagueness, and that Ibn aš-Šaǧarī was wrong to count that ayah as choice.

Setting out: ‘whether… or…’

setting out

It sets out what was said as two cases (tafṣīl). Ibn Hišām reads the words after إِمَّا in 76:3 as states (ḥāl), hence accusative.

Condition: إِنْ + مَا, ‘if…’

condition · corpus: 9×

This is not the إِمَّا of choice but the conditional إِنْ with an added مَا (Ibn Hišām). In all nine ayat where the corpus files the conditional إِمَّا as one word, the verb after it has the emphatic ـنَّ (يَأْتِيَنَّكُم), so its mood does not show on the ending.
