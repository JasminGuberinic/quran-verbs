What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الزَّرْعُ

    the crops (usjev) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    ٱلزَّرْعَ

    the crops (usjev) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي الزَّرْعِ

    in the crops (na usjevu) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ الزَّرْعِ

    from the crops (iz usjeva) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    مَوْسِمُ الزَّرْعِ

    the season of the crops (sezona usjeva) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    زَرْعُهُ

    his/its crops (njegov usjev) · Bosnian nominative

Practice sentences

  • subject (nominative)

    الزَّرْعُ يَنْمُو سَرِيعًا

    The crops grow quickly.

  • object (accusative)

    حَصَدَ الْفَلَّاحُ الزَّرْعَ

    The farmer harvested the crops.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    فَرِحَ الْفَلَّاحُ بِالزَّرْعِ

    The farmer was glad about the crops.

  • possessor (genitive)

    لَوْنُ الزَّرْعِ أَخْضَرُ

    The color of the crops is green.
