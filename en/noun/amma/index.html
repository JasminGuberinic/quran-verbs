What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الْعَمَّةُ

    the paternal aunt (tetka) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الْعَمَّةَ

    the paternal aunt (tetku) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي الْعَمَّةِ

    in the paternal aunt (u tetki) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ الْعَمَّةِ

    from the paternal aunt (od tetke) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    بَيْتُ الْعَمَّةِ

    the house of the paternal aunt (kuća tetke) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    عَمَّتُهُ

    his/its paternal aunt (njegova tetka) · Bosnian nominative
