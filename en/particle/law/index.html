Particles · condition · conjunction (verbal noun)

لَوْif (it were), had…; even if; (wish) that

201× in the Quran

What it does

The condition of what did not happen or is not expected: “If We willed, We verily could make it chaff” (56:65). It does not change the verb's mood. Its answer often has لَـ (لَجَعَلْنَٰهُ). After وَدَّ ‘wishes’ it means ‘that’ and introduces a wish.

Uses

  1. ‘If it were…, it would…’
  2. وَلَوْ: ‘even if’
  3. After ‘wish’: ‘that…’

‘If it were…, it would…’

condition

Grammarians call it the word ‘of a thing not happening because another did not happen’; Ibn Hišām, with the careful grammarians (al-muḥaqqiqūn), puts it more precisely: لَوْ by itself says only that the condition did not happen; it does not say the answer did not happen, for that may hold by another cause.

وَلَوْ: ‘even if’

condition

With وَ before it, لَوْ means ‘even if’: what is said holds in that case too.

After ‘wish’: ‘that…’

conjunction (verbal noun) · corpus: 12×

After وَدَّ and يَوَدُّ, لَوْ introduces what is wished for and does not change the verb's mood. Ibn Hišām reports that some grammarians, al-Farrāʾ and Ibn Mālik among them, take it as a verbal-noun subordinator like أَنْ; others read it as a condition whose answer is left out.

The verb after it stays indicative.
