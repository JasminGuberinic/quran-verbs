Nuances

Delivering and rescuing

نَجَّى · أَنْجَى · أَنْقَذَ

What they share

All three words mean bringing someone out of danger. نَجَّى and أَنْجَى are two forms of one root, whose basic sense, according to ar-Rāġib, is separating from something; أَنْقَذَ stresses pulling someone out of a predicament.

How to tell them apart

Delivering, separating from danger — ar-Rāġib lists them together, and the Quran uses both forms of the same events → نَجَّى, أَنْجَى; freeing from a predicament → أَنْقَذَ.

نَجَّى to deliver (Form II)

37× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the root naǧāʾ originally means separating from something; he lists ‘anǧaytuhu’ and ‘naǧǧaytuhu’ together, without telling them apart. Our observation: every imperative prayer ‘deliver me / us’ in the Quran takes this form (10:86, 26:118, 26:169, 28:21, 66:11); the conditional vows ‘if You / He deliver us’ (10:22, 6:63) use أَنْجَى.

أَنْجَى to deliver (Form IV)

23× in the Quran · Verb page →

The same root; ar-Rāġib lists it beside نَجَّى without drawing any difference. In the Quran both forms are said of the same event: the deliverance from Pharaoh's folk is نَجَّيْنَاكُمْ in 2:49 and أَنْجَيْنَاكُمْ in 7:141; 6:63 has both in one ayah.

أَنْقَذَ to rescue from a predicament

4× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, inqāḏ is freeing from a predicament (warṭa). In the Quran only 4 times: once of Allah's rescue (3:103), otherwise in negative and rhetorical sentences — no one can rescue the one Allah wills to punish.
