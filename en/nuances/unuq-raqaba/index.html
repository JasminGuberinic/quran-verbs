Nuances

Neck and nape

عُنُق · رَقَبَة

What they share

Both words mean the neck. They differ in what is said with them: the neck as a part of the body — the place of fetters and of what is ‘fastened’ to a person — or the neck standing for the whole person, even a slave to be set free.

How to tell them apart

The neck as a part of the body — fetters, the augury fastened to the neck, the hand chained to the neck → عُنُق; the nape for the whole person, mostly a slave: ‘freeing a nape’ → رَقَبَة.

عُنُق neck

9× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, عُنُق is the bodily limb, plural aʿnāq; ‘above the necks’ (8:12) means the heads, and he reports that the nobles of a people are called aʿnāq, taking 26:4 so. In the Quran (all 9 places) it is mostly the bodily neck: the place of fetters (13:5, 36:8, 40:71), the augury fastened to the neck (17:13), the hand chained to the neck (17:29).

رَقَبَة nape; a slave

9× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, رَقَبَة is the name of the known limb; then it expresses the whole person, and in usage it became a name for slaves — as ‘head’ and ‘back’ are said for mounts. In the Quran, in every place but 47:4, it is a person to be set free: as expiation (4:92, 5:89, 58:3) or as a good deed (90:13).
