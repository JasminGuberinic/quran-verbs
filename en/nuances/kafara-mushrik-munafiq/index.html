Nuances

Disbelief, idolatry and hypocrisy

كَفَرَ · مُشْرِك · مُنَافِق

What they share

All three words describe a stance opposed to faith; they differ in what is at its heart: denial, ascribing partners to Allah, or hiding disbelief behind a public faith.

How to tell them apart

Covering over and denying the truth or a favour — the widest word → كَفَرَ; ascribing a partner to Allah → مُشْرِك; faith shown in public, disbelief kept hidden → مُنَافِق.

كَفَرَ to disbelieve, to be ungrateful

289× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the root sense is ‘to cover’: the disbeliever covers the truth, the ingrate covers a favour. In Quranic usage the widest of the three — it takes in the idolaters and those of the People of the Scripture who disbelieve, and it also means ingratitude.

مُشْرِك polytheist, one who associates

44× in the Quran · Noun page →

One who ascribes a partner to Allah in divinity or worship. The Quran often names the polytheists separately, beside the People of the Scripture, as a group of their own.

مُنَافِق hypocrite

27× in the Quran · Noun page →

One who shows faith in public and hides disbelief. According to ar-Rāġib the word comes from nafaq, a tunnel that runs right through, like the jerboa's bolt-hole: the hypocrite enters the religion by one door and leaves by another.
