What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    ٱلْوَزْنُ

    the weight (težina) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    ٱلْوَزْنَ

    the weight (težinu) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي الْوَزْنِ

    in the weight (u težini) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ الْوَزْنِ

    from the weight (od težine) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    زِيَادَةُ الْوَزْنِ

    the increase of the weight (povećanje težine) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    وَزْنُهُ

    his/its weight (njegova težina) · Bosnian nominative

Practice sentences

  • subject (nominative)

    زَادَ وَزْنُ الطِّفْلِ

    The child's weight increased.

  • object (accusative)

    قَاسَ الطَّبِيبُ وَزْنَ الْمَرِيضِ

    The doctor measured the patient's weight.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    سَأَلَتِ الْأُمُّ عَنِ الْوَزْنِ

    The mother asked about the weight.

  • possessor (genitive)

    زِيَادَةُ الْوَزْنِ مُضِرَّةٌ

    The increase of weight is harmful.
