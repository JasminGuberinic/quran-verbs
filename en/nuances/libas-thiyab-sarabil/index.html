Nuances

Clothing, garments, shirts

لِبَاس · ثِيَاب · سَرَابِيل

What they share

All three words speak of what a person wears. They differ in breadth: whatever covers and protects, the pieces of clothing that are worn, or one kind of garment — the shirt.

How to tell them apart

Whatever is worn and whatever covers and protects, figuratively too — night, God-fearing, hunger and fear → لِبَاس; pieces of clothing that are put on, laid aside and wrapped round → ثِيَاب; a shirt of any kind, guarding against heat or in battle → سَرَابِيل.

لِبَاس clothing; covering

10× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, لِبَاس is what is worn, and the root means to cover. He says لِبَاس became a name for whatever covers a person from what is shameful — so spouses are ‘clothing’ for each other (2:187) — and God-fearing is called clothing by likeness, as hunger and fear are pictured as clothing (16:112). In the Quran the night too is ‘clothing’ (78:10).

ثِيَاب garments, pieces of clothing

8× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the root ثوب means a thing's return to its first state or to the state meant for it, and ثَوْب is so named because the yarn returns to the state planned for it; the plurals are أَثْوَاب and ثِيَاب. In the Quran they are pieces of clothing: put on (18:31), laid aside (24:60) and wrapped round oneself (71:7); for 74:4, ‘and purify your garments’, ar-Rāġib gives two readings: the clothes themselves, or ‘garments’ as a figure for the self.

سَرَابِيل shirts, coats

3× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, سِرْبَال is a shirt (قَمِيص) of whatever kind. In the Quran it comes only in the plural, in two places: shirts that guard against heat and those that guard in battle (16:81), and the shirts of pitch of the guilty (14:50).
