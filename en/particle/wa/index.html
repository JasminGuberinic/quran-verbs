Particles · conjunction · resuming conjunction · circumstantial wāw · oath · accompaniment

وَand; while; by…!

9594× in the Quran

What it does

The most frequent word of the Quran. It is written joined to the word after it (وَقَالُوا). As a conjunction it only links and changes no case: the word after it takes the case of the word it is joined to. As an oath (وَٱلْعَصْرِ) it is a preposition and takes the genitive.

Uses

  1. ‘and’: joining
  2. Starting a new sentence
  3. ‘while’: the circumstance (wāw al-ḥāl)
  4. ‘By…!’: the oath
  5. أَوَ: after the question hamza
  6. ‘Together with’: the wāw of accompaniment
  7. With a verb: the subjunctive

‘and’: joining

conjunction · corpus: 8177×

It joins words and sentences. According to Ibn Hišām, وَ by itself does not say which came first: it only joins (for order the Quran uses فَ and ثُمَّ). The word after it shares the case of the word it is joined to.

Starting a new sentence

resuming conjunction · corpus: 1034×

The corpus marks this way a وَ that does not join to the sentence before but opens a new one (al-istiʾnāf). In English it is often ‘and’ at the start of a sentence, or left out.

‘while’: the circumstance (wāw al-ḥāl)

circumstantial wāw · corpus: 293×

It introduces a clause describing the state in which the action happens: ‘while’, ‘and (they)…’. A pronoun or قَدْ most often follows it.

‘By…!’: the oath

oath · corpus: 28×

At the start of a surah or an ayah وَ is an oath and a preposition: the noun after it is genitive. Allah swears by what He created to draw attention to what follows.

The noun after it is genitive.

أَوَ: after the question hamza

conjunction · corpus: 59×

The corpus files so (SUP) a وَ that most often stands after the question hamza: أَوَلَمْ، أَوَلَوْ. According to Sībawayh and most grammarians it is the conjunction of the sentence that follows, and the hamza is put before it because a question goes first (see أَ).

‘Together with’: the wāw of accompaniment

accompaniment

The noun after this وَ is accusative because it means ‘together with’ (al-mafʿūl maʿahu). In 10:71 the corpus reads it so; others read it as the object of an understood verb (‘and call’).

The word after it is accusative.

With a verb: the subjunctive

accompaniment

When this وَ ties a verb to the one before in the sense ‘while at the same time…’, the verb is subjunctive. The Kufans call it the wāw of ‘turning away’ (wāw aṣ-ṣarf) and say it makes the verb subjunctive itself; Ibn Hišām rejects this: it is the conjunction, joining the verb (with an understood أَنْ) to a verbal noun understood before it.

The verb after it is subjunctive.
