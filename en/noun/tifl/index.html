What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الطِّفْلُ

    the child (dijete) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الطِّفْلَ

    the child (dijete) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي ٱلطِّفْلِ

    in the child (u djetetu) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ ٱلطِّفْلِ

    from the child (od djeteta) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    لُعْبَةُ ٱلطِّفْلِ

    the toy of the child (igračka djeteta) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    طِفْلُهُ

    his/its child (njegovo dijete) · Bosnian nominative

Practice sentences

  • subject (nominative)

    نَامَ الطِّفْلُ مُبَكِّرًا

    The child slept early.

  • object (accusative)

    حَمَلَتِ الْأُمُّ الطِّفْلَ

    The mother carried the child.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    قَرَأَ الْجَدُّ قِصَّةً لِلْأَطْفَالِ

    The grandfather read a story to the children.

  • possessor (genitive)

    سَمِعْنَا أَصْوَاتَ الْأَطْفَالِ

    We heard the voices of the children.
