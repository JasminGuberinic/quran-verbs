Striving for and seeking

اِبْتَغَى · طَلَبَ

What they share

Both words mean seeking what one does not have. They differ in the effort: plain seeking to find something, or seeking with toil and earnestness — and the root of the second word also carries the idea of going beyond a measure.

How to tell them apart

Seeking, going after something — the verb only in 7:54, of night and day → طَلَبَ; striving for, seeking with effort — Allah's bounty and the way to Him, but also sedition → اِبْتَغَى.

اِبْتَغَى to strive for, to seek earnestly

34× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the root baghy is seeking beyond the right measure, and ibtighāʾ is specifically ‘striving in seeking’: when what is sought is praiseworthy, the seeking is praiseworthy. In the Quran believers strive for Allah's bounty and for the way of approach to Him, while others strive for sedition or a religion other than Islam.

طَلَبَ to seek, to pursue

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, ṭalab is searching for whether a thing is there, a concrete thing or a meaning. The verb is in the Quran only once: of night and day, the one seeking the other swiftly (7:54). The word has neither the effort nor the measure that ibtighāʾ carries — it is plain seeking.
