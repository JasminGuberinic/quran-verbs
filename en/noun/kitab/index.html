What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    ٱلْكِتَٰبُ

    the book (knjiga) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    ٱلْكِتَٰبَ

    the book (knjigu) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي ٱلْكِتَٰبِ

    in the book (u knjizi) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ

    from the book (iz knjige) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    اسْمُ ٱلْكِتَٰبِ

    the name of the book (naziv knjige) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    كِتَٰبَهُۥ from the Quran

    his/its book (njegovu knjigu) · Bosnian accusative

    قَرَأَتِ الْبِنْتُ الْكِتَابَ

    The girl read the book.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    وَجَدْتُ الصُّورَةَ فِي الْكِتَابِ

    I found the picture in the book.

  • possessor (genitive)

    غِلَافُ الْكِتَابِ أَحْمَرُ

    The cover of the book is red.
