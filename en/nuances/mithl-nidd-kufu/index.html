Nuances

Like, rival and equal

مِثْل · أَنْدَاد · كُفُو

What they share

All three words speak of one thing being equal to another. They differ in what the equality lies in: in any respect at all, in the very substance, or in rank and measure. The Quran uses all three also to deny that anything is equal to Allah.

How to tell them apart

Likeness in any respect — the most general word; ‘nothing is like Him’ → مِثْل; a rival sharing the substance — ‘do not set up rivals to Allah’ → أَنْدَاد; equal in rank and measure → كُفُو.

مِثْل like, the like of

75× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, miṯl is the most general word for likeness: it covers likeness in substance, in quality, in quantity and in measure. So when Allah willed to deny every likeness to Him, He used just this word: ‘nothing is as His likeness’ (42:11).

أَنْدَاد rivals

6× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, a nidd is what shares the very substance of a thing; it is a kind of likeness, so ‘every nidd is a miṯl, but not every miṯl is a nidd’. In the Quran (all 6 places) they are always rivals that people set up to Allah — as if what belongs to Him alone belonged to them.

كُفُو equal, comparable

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, kufʾ is equality in rank and measure: one is said to be another's equal in marriage or in fighting. In the Quran it comes only once, in Sura al-Iḫlāṣ: no one is equal to Allah.
