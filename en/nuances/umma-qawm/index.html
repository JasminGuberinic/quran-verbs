Community and people

أُمَّة · قَوْم

What they share

Both words mean a group of people. According to ar-Rāġib, أُمَّة is any community held together by something — a religion, a time or a place — while قَوْم is at root a group of men, though in most of the Quran it includes women too.

How to tell them apart

A community joined by something shared — faith, way, time (even animal kinds) → أُمَّة; a people, the folk of one tribe; a prophet's people (“O my people!”, 7:59) → قَوْم.

أُمَّة community, nation

64× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, any group joined by one thing — a religion, a time or a place — by choice or by Allah's ordering; so even animals are “peoples like unto you” (6:38). Of Abraham the Quran says he was an أُمَّة: alone, ar-Rāġib says, he stood in place of a whole community in worship.

قَوْم people

383× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, at root a group of men without women (so 49:11 names both), but in most of the Quran it means men and women alike. The usual word for the people a prophet was sent to.
