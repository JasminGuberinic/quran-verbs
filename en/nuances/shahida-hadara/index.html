Witnessing and being present

شَهِدَ · حَضَرَ

What they share

Both words mean being present. According to ar-Rāġib, شُهُود and شَهَادَة are presence together with seeing — by eye or by insight — though he says presence alone can also be so called: شُهُود suits bare presence better, شَهَادَة presence with seeing. He explains حُضُور with the word شَهَادَة, ‘being present at a place or with someone’. So testimony (شَهَادَة) is speech from knowledge gained by seeing.

How to tell them apart

To be present and see; to testify to what one knows → شَهِدَ; to be present, to come upon (death, people) → حَضَرَ.

شَهِدَ to witness, to testify

44× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, presence together with seeing — by eye or by insight; hence also testimony, speech from knowledge gained by seeing. He adds that the noun شُهُود suits bare presence better. A witness, he says, must say ‘I testify’, not ‘I know’. In the Quran it is mostly testifying, sometimes bare presence.

حَضَرَ to be present, to come upon

7× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the root is settled life (the opposite of the desert), then being at a place or with someone. He defines it with the word شَهَادَة itself, meaning bare presence, adding nothing of seeing or knowing in that definition. In Quranic usage, in four of its seven places, it is death that ‘comes upon’ a person.
