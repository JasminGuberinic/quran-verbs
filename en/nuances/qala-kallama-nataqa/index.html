Nuances

To speak

قَالَ · كَلَّمَ · نَطَقَ

What they share

All three verbs concern the spoken word; they differ in what is at the centre: the content, the one addressed, or the act of speech itself.

How to tell them apart

What was said → قَالَ; the one spoken to, conversation → كَلَّمَ; the power and act of articulate speech, often negated (‘they do not speak’) → نَطَقَ.

قَالَ to say

1618× in the Quran · Verb page →

The most general word for an utterance; what was said is at its centre. According to ar-Rāġib, it is also said of what is conceived in the soul before it is spoken.

كَلَّمَ to speak to

20× in the Quran · Verb page →

To speak to someone, to address him: the verb names the one addressed. From the same root come كَلَام, speech, and كَلِمَة, word.

نَطَقَ to speak, to utter

9× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, in common usage: articulate sounds the tongue utters and the ears take in. In the Quran it often comes negated — idols that do not speak, people denied speech — so the very power of speech is in view.

Sources

  • ar-Rāġib al-Iṣfahānī, Mufradāt alfāẓ al-Qurʾān — entries قول, كلم, نطق
