Nuances

Showing, bringing to light, declaring

أَبْدَى · أَظْهَرَ · أَعْلَنَ

What they share

All three words mean that something hidden becomes seen or known. They differ in what is disclosed and in what each is set against.

How to tell them apart

To show what was in the soul or covered → أَبْدَى; to bring out into view, in the Quran mostly to make prevail or to let be known → أَظْهَرَ; to do openly, as against in secret → أَعْلَنَ.

أَبْدَى to show, to disclose

15× in the Quran · Verb page →

From badā, to appear plainly, as ar-Rāġib describes it. In Quranic usage it mostly stands against hiding (أَخْفَى, كَتَمَ): to show what is in the soul or what one gives openly, and also what is covered, such as adornment or the private parts.

أَظْهَرَ to bring to light, to make prevail

7× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ẓahara is at root being on the surface of the earth, so not hidden, as against baṭana, being in its belly; then anything prominent to sight or to insight. In the Quran أَظْهَرَ mostly means to make prevail (9:33) or to let be known (66:3, 72:26).

أَعْلَنَ to declare, to do openly

12× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, openness (عَلَانِيَة) is the opposite of secrecy (سِرّ), and is said mostly of meanings rather than of concrete things. In the Quran all twelve times it stands beside its opposite: what is kept secret.
