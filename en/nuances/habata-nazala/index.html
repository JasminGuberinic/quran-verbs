Nuances

Going down and coming down

هَبَطَ · نَزَلَ

What they share

Both words mean moving from above to below. They differ in how that movement is seen: as a fall and a descent that is imposed, or as coming down from a height.

How to tell them apart

Coming down from a height, the general word; in the Quran for the Quran, the truth and what comes down from the sky → نَزَلَ; a descent like a stone's fall, which ar-Rāġib sees as a going down ‘by compulsion’ → هَبَطَ.

هَبَطَ to go down, to descend (as a fall)

8× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, hubūṭ is a descent ‘by compulsion’, as a stone falls. When said of a person, he sees belittlement in it, unlike inzāl (from the root of نَزَلَ), which Allah uses for what He honours — the angels, the Quran, rain. In the Quran it is almost always the command ‘Go down!’; yet Noah goes down “with peace from Us and blessings” (11:48), so this is no rule without exception.

نَزَلَ to come down, to descend

6× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, nuzūl is at root a coming down from a height. In the Quran (Form I) the Quran and the truth come down so, as does what comes down from the sky and the punishment that comes down upon a people.
