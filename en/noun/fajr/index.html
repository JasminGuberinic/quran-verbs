What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الْفَجْرُ

    the dawn (zora) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الْفَجْرَ

    the dawn (zoru) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ ٱلْفَجْرِ

    from the dawn (od zore) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي ٱلْفَجْرِ

    in the dawn (u zori) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    ضَوْءُ ٱلْفَجْرِ

    the light of the dawn (svjetlo zore) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    فَجْرُهُ

    his/its dawn (njegova zora) · Bosnian nominative

Practice sentences

  • subject (nominative)

    جَاءَ الْفَجْرُ سَرِيعًا

    Dawn came quickly.

  • object (accusative)

    انْتَظَرَ الْمُسَافِرُ الْفَجْرَ

    The traveler waited for dawn.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    يَسْتَيْقِظُ الْفَلَّاحُ مَعَ الْفَجْرِ

    The farmer wakes up with dawn.

  • possessor (genitive)

    ضَوْءُ الْفَجْرِ جَمِيلٌ

    The light of dawn is beautiful.
