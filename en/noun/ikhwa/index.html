What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الْإِخْوَةُ

    the brothers (braća) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الْإِخْوَةَ

    the brothers (braću) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    عَلَى الْإِخْوَةِ

    on the brothers (na braći) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي الْإِخْوَةِ

    in the brothers (u braći) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    بَيْتُ الْإِخْوَةِ

    the house of the brothers (kuća braće) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    إِخْوَتُهُ

    his/its brothers (njegova braća) · Bosnian nominative
