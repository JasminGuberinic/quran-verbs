Nuances

Desire and passions

هَوًى · شَهْوَة

What they share

Both words mean the soul's pull toward something. According to ar-Rāġib, hawā is the soul's leaning toward shahwa, and shahwa is the soul's very yearning for what it wants.

How to tell them apart

The soul's leaning that is followed instead of guidance, in the Quran always blamed → هَوًى; the soul's yearning for what it wants, and the thing wanted — genuine (hunger) or false → شَهْوَة.

هَوًى desire, inclination

28× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, hawā is the soul's leaning toward shahwa; some say it is so named because it makes its follower fall into every calamity. The Quran uses the plural ‘their desires’, he says, to show that each has his own desire, and that it has no end. In the Quran desire is always blamed.

شَهْوَة passion, appetite

5× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, shahwa is the soul's yearning for what it wants; in this world it is of two kinds: genuine, without which the body suffers, like the wish for food in hunger, and false, which it can do without. The thing wanted is also called shahwa. The Quran uses the verb of this root for what souls desire in Paradise too (41:31).
