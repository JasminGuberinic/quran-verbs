Male · female

A collective noun names a group as a whole: it has no singular, dual or plural of its own. Verbs, adjectives and pronouns agree with it as stated above. For one of them Arabic has words of their own:

What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الْخَيْلُ

    the horses (konji) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    ٱلْخَيْلَ

    the horses (konje) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    بِٱلْخَيْلِ

    with the horses (s konjima) · Bosnian instrumental

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ ٱلْخَيْلِ

    from the horses (od konja) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    إِسْطَبْلُ ٱلْخَيْلِ

    the stable of the horses (štala konja) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    خَيْلُهُ

    his/its horses (njegovi konji) · Bosnian nominative
