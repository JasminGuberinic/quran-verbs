What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتُ

    the good deeds (dobročinstva) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ

    the good deeds (dobročinstva) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ

    from the good deeds (od dobročinstava) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ

    in the good deeds (u dobročinstvima) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    أَجْرُ ٱلصَّٰلِحَٰتِ

    the reward of the good deeds (nagrada dobročinstava) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    صَالِحَاتُهُ

    his/its good deeds (njegova dobročinstva) · Bosnian nominative

Practice sentences

    فَعَلَ الطِّفْلُ صَالِحَاتٍ كَثِيرَةً

    The mother was glad of the good deeds.

    أَثَرُ الصَّالِحَاتِ جَمِيلٌ

    The trace of good deeds is beautiful.
