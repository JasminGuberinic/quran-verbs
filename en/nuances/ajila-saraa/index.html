Haste and hastening

عَجِلَ · اِسْتَعْجَلَ · سَارَعَ

What they share

All three words speak of speed. According to ar-Rāġib, عَجَلَة is seeking a thing before its time — it springs from desire, so it is blamed in most of the Quran — while سُرْعَة is simply the opposite of slowness.

How to tell them apart

To hurry, before the time → عَجِلَ; to ask for a thing to come sooner (the deniers demanding the punishment) → اِسْتَعْجَلَ; to hasten, to vie in speed — praised or blamed by its goal → سَارَعَ.

عَجِلَ to hasten, to be in haste

5× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, seeking a thing before its time; it comes from desire, so in most of the Quran it is blamed. Moses' haste to Allah (20:84) was, he says, haste, but what moved it was praiseworthy — seeking Allah's pleasure.

اِسْتَعْجَلَ to ask to hasten, to be impatient for

19× in the Quran · Verb page →

To ask for a thing to come before its time. In the Quran most often the deniers mockingly demanding that the punishment come at once.

سَارَعَ to hasten, to race

9× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, سُرْعَة is the opposite of slowness, said of bodies and of acts. The Quran praises hastening to forgiveness and good deeds and blames hastening into disbelief and sin — the goal gives it its worth.
