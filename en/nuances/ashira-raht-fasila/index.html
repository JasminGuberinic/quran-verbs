Nuances

Kindred, family, nearest kin

عَشِيرَة · رَهْط · فَصِيلَة

What they share

All three words mean a group of a man's own people on whom he relies. They differ in size and in what the word brings out.

How to tell them apart

A man's relatives by whom he grows in number, as if with them he were a ‘complete number’ (from the word for ten) → عَشِيرَة; a smaller band, under ten men (some say up to forty) → رَهْط; the kin ‘separated off’ from him, from the word for separating → فَصِيلَة.

عَشِيرَة kindred

3× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ʿašīra are a man's relatives by whom he grows in number, as if they gave him the ‘complete number’ — since ten is the complete number. In the Quran kindred stands beside fathers, sons, brothers and wives, as what a believer must not put before Allah.

رَهْط family (a small band)

3× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, rahṭ is a band of fewer than ten men (some say: up to forty). In the Quran Shuʿayb's people say they do not stone him only because of his family — a small band that protects him.

فَصِيلَة nearest kin

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, a man's faṣīla is his kindred ‘separated off from him’ — the word is from faṣl, separating. It comes only once in the Quran, describing the Day of Judgement (70:11–14): the wrongdoer would gladly ransom himself even with his nearest kin who used to shelter him.
