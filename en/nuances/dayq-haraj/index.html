Nuances

Constriction and hardship

ضَاقَ · حَرَج

What they share

Both words speak of tightness. According to ar-Rāġib, ضِيق is the opposite of spaciousness and expresses grief, poverty or stinginess, while حَرَج comes from a thick, close-packed cluster of things, and so means both constriction and sin.

How to tell them apart

To be straitened — the breast, the soul, the earth; helplessness and grief before a thing → ضَاقَ; a hardship laid on someone, and also sin (‘there is no ḥaraj upon…’) → حَرَج.

ضَاقَ to be straitened, to constrict

7× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the opposite of spaciousness; said of the breast, the soul and the earth it expresses grief, and ‘ضَاقَ بِهِمْ ذَرْعًا’ means he was at a loss with them. In Quranic usage the verb is said only of the breast, the soul, the earth and of Lot before his guests — never of a rule or of sin.

حَرَج hardship, constraint; blame

15× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the root is a thick, close-packed cluster of things, so the tightness among them is called حَرَج, and so is sin. Hence the Quran says there is no hardship in the religion (22:78) and “no blame” upon the sick and the blind (24:61).
