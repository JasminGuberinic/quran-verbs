What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الرُّهْبَانُ

    the monks (monasi) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الرُّهْبَانَ

    the monks (monahe) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي ٱلرُّهْبَانِ

    in the monks (u monasima) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ ٱلرُّهْبَانِ

    from the monks (od monaha) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    دَيْرُ ٱلرُّهْبَانِ

    the monastery of the monks (samostan monaha) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    رُهْبَانُهُ

    his/its monks (njegovi monasi) · Bosnian nominative
