Nuances

Sign, proof, clear evidence, argument

آيَة · بُرْهَان · بَيِّنَة · حُجَّة

What they share

All four words mean what points to the truth. According to ar-Rāġib, they differ in how they point: the seen that leads to the unseen, the firmest proof, a clear indication by reason or the senses, and a proof that shows the straight way and settles which of two contraries is sound.

How to tell them apart

A visible sign inseparably joined to something unseen (and a verse of the Quran) → آيَة; the firmest proof, one that always leads to truth → بُرْهَان; a clear indication, by reason or by the senses (also witnesses) → بَيِّنَة; an argument showing the right way in a dispute — so called even when refuted → حُجَّة.

آيَة sign; verse

382× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, a clear sign: something seen that is inseparably joined to something unseen, so that whoever grasps the first knows the second — as a made thing tells of its Maker. Every passage of the Quran that indicates a ruling or is set off by a break is also called a verse (āya).

بُرْهَان proof

8× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, burhān is the making-plain of a proof and the firmest of all proofs — the one that always leads to truth. Four times in the Quran those who make a claim without evidence are told to bring their proof.

بَيِّنَة clear evidence

71× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, a clear indication, whether by reason or by the senses; so two witnesses are also called bayyina. In the Quran the she-camel sent to Thamūd is such evidence (7:73), seen with the eyes.

حُجَّة argument

7× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the argument that points the right way and requires that of two contrary claims one is sound — and what people argue with may also be called ḥujja. The Quran also calls the disbelievers' refuted plea an argument (42:16).
