Secret and whispered counsel

سِرّ · نَجْوَى

What they share

Both words speak of hidden speech. They differ in where the speech is: a secret a person keeps within or does in hiding, or talk held apart, among a few, so that others do not hear it.

How to tell them apart

What is hidden in the soul, the opposite of the open — also giving wealth in secret → سِرّ; talk held apart between two or more → نَجْوَى.

سِرّ secret

11× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, sirr is speech hidden in the soul, and isrār is the opposite of the open (iʿlān). In the Quran it is what Allah knows, and what is more hidden still — as befits Him — and giving wealth in secret and openly.

نَجْوَى whispered counsel, secret conference

11× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, munāǧāt is talk held apart; its origin, he says, is to be alone with someone on a raised place (naǧwa), and he also gives other explanations. Naǧwā is in origin a verbal noun, and it is also said of the people who confer (17:47). In the Quran it is always talk among people.
