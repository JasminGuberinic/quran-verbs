What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الْغُرُوبُ

    the setting (zalazak) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الْغُرُوبَ

    the setting (zalazak) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي ٱلْغُرُوبِ

    in the setting (na zalasku) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ ٱلْغُرُوبِ

    from the setting (od zalaska) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    وَقْتُ ٱلْغُرُوبِ

    the time of the setting (vrijeme zalaska) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    غُرُوبِهَا from the Quran

    of her/its setting (njenog zalaska) · Bosnian genitive

Practice sentences

  • subject (nominative)

    اقْتَرَبَ غُرُوبُ الشَّمْسِ

    The setting of the sun approached.

  • object (accusative)

    شَاهَدَ السُّيَّاحُ غُرُوبَ الشَّمْسِ

    The tourists watched the setting of the sun.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    عَادَ الصَّيَّادُ مَعَ الْغُرُوبِ

    The fisherman returned with the sunset.

  • possessor (genitive)

    وَقْتُ الْغُرُوبِ قَرِيبٌ

    The time of the setting is near.
