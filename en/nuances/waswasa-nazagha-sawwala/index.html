Nuances

Whispering, stirring up and making evil seem fair

وَسْوَسَ · نَزَغَ · سَوَّلَ

What they share

All three words speak of how a bad thought or a bad deed reaches a person. They differ in the path: a quiet whisper in the breast, a meddling among people to spoil what is between them, or a soul that paints the ugly as beautiful.

How to tell them apart

Whispering a bad thought quietly — Satan or a person's own soul → وَسْوَسَ; meddling in an affair to spoil it, sowing discord between people → نَزَغَ; making what the soul craves look fair, painting the ugly as beautiful — mostly the soul → سَوَّلَ.

وَسْوَسَ to whisper (an evil suggestion)

4× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, waswasa is the bad thought that passes through a person; the word comes from the sound of jewellery and a quiet whisper. In the Quran it is not only Satan who whispers: in 50:16 a man's own soul whispers to him.

نَزَغَ to stir up strife, to sow discord

4× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, nazġ is entering into an affair in order to spoil it. In the Quran the subject is always Satan; twice he is said to stir ‘between’ people — spoiling what lies between them.

سَوَّلَ to make seem fair, to entice

4× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, taswīl is the soul's making attractive what it is eager for and showing the ugly in it as beautiful. In the Quran the subject is the soul three times (12:18, 12:83, 20:96) and Satan once (47:25).
