Nuances

Reprieve, giving rein, granting respite

أَنْظَرَ · أَمْلَى · مَهَّلَ

What they share

All three words mean giving someone time before what awaits him. They differ in what is stressed: putting off the term, lengthening the time, or unhurried gentleness.

How to tell them apart

To defer, to put off a term — in the Quran mostly negated: ‘they will not be reprieved’ → أَنْظَرَ; to give rein, to let time run long (Allah with the deniers, as befits Him) → أَمْلَى; to give time slowly, gently, without haste → مَهَّلَ.

أَنْظَرَ to reprieve, to defer

12× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, inẓār is deferring, putting off till later. In the Quran it is mostly negated: when the punishment or the set term comes, the deniers are not reprieved — as ar-Rāġib points to 7:34, the term cannot be moved by an hour.

أَمْلَى to give rein, to grant a long respite

9× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, imlāʾ is extending, letting time run long; he explains 7:183 as ‘I give them respite’. In the Quran Allah so gives the deniers rein, as befits Him — and says it is not good for them (3:178).

مَهَّلَ to grant a gentle respite

2× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, mahl is slowness and calm, and ‘mahlan’ means ‘gently, slowly’. In the Quran it is twice a command: to give the deniers a little time, without haste.
