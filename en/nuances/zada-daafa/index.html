Nuances

Increasing and multiplying

زَادَ · ضَاعَفَ

What they share

Both words mean that there comes to be more of something. They differ in the measure: any addition at all, or adding the same amount again, once or many times.

How to tell them apart

To add something to what a thing already is, for good or ill → زَادَ; to add to a thing its equal, to double and multiply — in the Quran of reward and punishment → ضَاعَفَ.

زَادَ to increase, to add

49× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ziyāda is that something else is joined to what a thing already is; it may be praiseworthy or blameworthy. Allah increases in guidance those who are guided, as befits Him; and the reminders increase some only in aversion, a blameworthy increase. Explaining a blameworthy increase (2:10), ar-Rāġib says: whoever takes up a thing, good or bad, grows stronger in it.

ضَاعَفَ to multiply, to double

9× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ḍiʿf is the joining of two equal amounts, a word of number; ḍāʿafa is adding to a thing its like or more. Some say ḍāʿafa is stronger than ḍaʿʿafa, so that multiplying a good deed would mean ten-fold (6:160). In the Quran Allah so multiplies a good deed, as befits Him, and for some the punishment is doubled.
