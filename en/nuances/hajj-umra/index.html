Hajj and umrah

حَجّ · عُمْرَة

What they share

Both words name the journey to Allah's House for its rites. According to ar-Rāġib, ḥajj at root means setting out to visit, and ʿumra a visit that keeps affection alive; the Sharia tied both to the Kaaba.

How to tell them apart

Hajj, in the well-known months, a duty on whoever is able → حَجّ; umrah, a visit to the Kaaba not tied to those months → عُمْرَة.

حَجّ pilgrimage, hajj

10× in the Quran · Noun page →

The pilgrimage to the Kaaba at its set time, with ʿArafa and its other rites. The Quran makes it a duty on whoever is able to reach the House.

عُمْرَة umrah, the lesser pilgrimage

2× in the Quran · Noun page →

A visit to the Kaaba with the rites of iḥrām, ṭawāf and saʿy, not bound to the months of hajj. The noun ʿumra occurs only in 2:196 (the verb iʿtamara also in 2:158), beside hajj; whether it is obligatory, scholars differ.

Sources

  • ar-Rāġib al-Iṣfahānī, Mufradāt alfāẓ al-Qurʾān — entries حج, عمر
  • Fiqh on 2:196 and 3:97 (whether umrah is obligatory)
