Nuances

Obeying and following

أَطَاعَ · اِتَّبَعَ

What they share

Both words mean going after another. They differ in whether a command is carried out, or someone's trace and way is followed.

How to tell them apart

To carry out what is commanded, to yield to an order → أَطَاعَ; to go along a trace, to follow a way, a person, or what gives no command — desire, the fathers' custom → اِتَّبَعَ.

أَطَاعَ to obey

72× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ṭawʿ is compliance, the opposite of compulsion; ṭāʿa is the same, but is mostly said of carrying out what is commanded. So أَطَاعَ in the Quran goes with those who command: Allah, the Messenger, those in authority — or those who must not be obeyed.

اِتَّبَعَ to follow

136× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ittibāʿ is going along someone's trace; sometimes this is carrying out a command, but not always. So in the Quran one also follows what gives no command: desire, Satan's footsteps, what the fathers were found upon — but also the messengers and what was sent down.
