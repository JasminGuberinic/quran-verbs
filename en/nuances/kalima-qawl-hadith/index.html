Nuances

Word, saying and account

كَلِمَة · قَوْل · حَدِيث

What they share

All three words name what is spoken or passed on. They differ in scope and in what they stress: a single word or a pronouncement that carries weight, what is said (even what is only in the soul), or an account that reaches a person by hearing or revelation.

How to tell them apart

A word, a pronouncement, even Allah's decree (‘the word of your Lord’) → كَلِمَة; what is said in general, also what is in the soul, and what is ascribed to someone → قَوْل; an account, tidings that come by hearing or revelation — and the Quran calls itself so → حَدِيث.

كَلِمَة word

46× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, for many theologians ‘kalima’ is said of each kind of word — noun, verb, particle. In Quranic usage it is often a single pronouncement that carries weight. ‘The word of your Lord’ (6:115) he reads as Allah's decree: every decree is called ‘kalima’, whether word or deed — as befits Him.

قَوْل saying, speech

92× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, qawl is first what is uttered, a single word or a sentence; but it is also said of what is in the soul before it is uttered, of a belief and of inspiration. Speech can be ascribed to the one who conveys it: in 69:40 it is the speech of the messenger who delivers it from the One Who sent him.

حَدِيث account, tidings

28× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, every speech that reaches a person by hearing or revelation, awake or asleep, is called ḥadīth — an account; and Allah named His Book so. In the Quran it is often the Quran itself, and in the formula ‘Has the account of … come to you?’ a story of past or coming events.
