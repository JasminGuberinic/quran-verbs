Adornment and embellishment

زِينَة · زُخْرُف

What they share

Both words mean what makes a thing pleasing to the eye. According to ar-Rāġib, زُخْرُف is adornment that has been embellished — hence gold too is called زُخْرُف — while زِينَة is the wider word: adornment of the soul, of the body and of possessions.

How to tell them apart

Adornment in general — knowledge and faith, strength and stature, wealth and standing; lawful or tempting, by how it is used → زِينَة; embellishment, polished glitter — gold, embellished words, the earth decked out before its ruin → زُخْرُف.

زِينَة adornment

19× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, true adornment is that which does not disgrace a person in this world or the next. Adornment is of three kinds: of the soul (knowledge, right belief), of the body (strength, stature) and external (wealth, standing). The Quran names it both as a lawful gift (7:32) and as the adornment of worldly life that passes (18:46, 28:79).

زُخْرُف embellishment; gold

4× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, adornment that has been embellished; hence gold is called زُخْرُف, and ‘embellished speech’ (زُخْرُفَ الْقَوْلِ) is speech dressed up. In Quranic usage the word is never praised: deceptive words, the earth decked out before its ruin, a house of gold the disbelievers demand, passing enjoyment.
