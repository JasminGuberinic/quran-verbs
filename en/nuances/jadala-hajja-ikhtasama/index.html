Nuances

To dispute and to quarrel

جَادَلَ · حَاجَّ · اخْتَصَمَ

What they share

All three verbs speak of a dispute between two sides. They differ in what the dispute is waged with and where it is held.

How to tell them apart

To argue, to contend — blamed when it is for winning, commanded when it is “in the better way” (16:125) → جَادَلَ; to argue with proofs, to bring arguments, most often about Allah and religion → حَاجَّ; to quarrel as opposing parties, often before Allah on the Last Day → اخْتَصَمَ.

جَادَلَ to argue, to dispute

25× in the Quran · Verb page →

To argue, to try to get the better of another in speech. Most often blamed — argument without knowledge or proof; but the Quran commands argument “in the better way” (16:125).

حَاجَّ to argue with proofs

12× in the Quran · Verb page →

To argue by bringing (or claiming to bring) proofs (ḥuǧǧa); in the Quran most often about Allah, the religion and Abraham — often without knowledge.

اخْتَصَمَ to quarrel, to dispute

8× in the Quran · Verb page →

To quarrel as two opposing parties; in the Quran often a quarrel before Allah on the Last Day.
