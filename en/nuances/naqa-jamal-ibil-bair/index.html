She-camel, camel, herd of camels

نَاقَة · جَمَل · إِبِل · بَعِير

What they share

All four words mean the camel. They differ by sex, age and number.

How to tell them apart

The female; in the Quran always Ṣāliḥ's she-camel, Allah's sign → نَاقَة; a camel once it has reached full age → جَمَل; camels as a group, with no singular from the same word → إِبِل; one camel, male or female, as insān is said of both sexes → بَعِير.

نَاقَة she-camel

7× in the Quran · Noun page →

The female camel. In the Quran (7×) it is always the same one — the she-camel sent to Thamūd as a sign through Ṣāliḥ; the Quran calls her “the camel of Allah” (11:64).

جَمَل camel (full-grown)

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, jamal is a camel once it has reached full age. In the Quran it comes only once, in an image of the impossible: a large animal and the eye of a needle.

إِبِل camels (as a herd)

2× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ibil means many camels and has no singular from the same word. The Quran names it among the livestock Allah created and as a sign to look at.

بَعِير camel (one, male or female)

2× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, baʿīr is a camel, said of male and female alike, just as insān is said of man and woman. In the Quran (2×) it comes in the story of Joseph's brothers, as the measure of a load.
