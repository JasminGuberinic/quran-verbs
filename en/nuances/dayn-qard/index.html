Debt and loan

دَيْن · قَرْض

What they share

Both words speak of wealth given on the duty of return. They differ in their Quranic use: an obligation resting on a debtor among people, or what is given to be returned — in the Quran always ‘a goodly loan’ given to Allah.

How to tell them apart

Debt — an obligation among people, in trade and inheritance → دَيْن; loan — what is given to be returned; in all six places ‘a goodly loan’ to Allah → قَرْض.

دَيْن debt

5× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ‘dintu’ means ‘I took a debt’ (he also reports it for lending), and tadāyun and mudāyana are giving debt (2:282). In the Quran dayn is always a real debt among people: it is written down before witnesses, and it is paid from an estate before the inheritance is shared. The word dīn (religion) is of the same root but another word.

قَرْض loan

6× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, qarḍ is at root a kind of cutting, and then wealth given to someone on condition that its equivalent be returned. In the Quran all six places are ‘a goodly loan’ (qarḍan ḥasanan) given to Allah: many mufassirun explain it as spending in Allah's way, and Allah multiplies it — as befits Him, Who needs nothing from anyone.
