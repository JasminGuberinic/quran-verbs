What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الْجارُ

    the neighbor (susjed) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الْجارَ

    the neighbor (susjeda) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي ٱلْجَارِ

    in the neighbor (u susjedu) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ ٱلْجَارِ

    from the neighbor (od susjeda) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    بَيْتُ ٱلْجَارِ

    the house of the neighbor (kuća susjeda) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    جَارُهُ

    his/its neighbor (njegov susjed) · Bosnian nominative
