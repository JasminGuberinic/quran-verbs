Nuances

Refuge: seeking refuge, holding fast, a haven

عَاذَ · اِعْتَصَمَ · مَلْجَأ

What they share

All three words speak of seeking protection. They differ in the image: fleeing under someone's protection from what one fears, holding fast to what saves, or a place one flees into.

How to tell them apart

Seeking refuge with someone from something — with bi- (with whom) and min / an (from what) → عَاذَ; taking hold and holding fast — to Allah and His rope → اِعْتَصَمَ; a haven, a place one flees into — in the Quran only three times: none except with Allah Himself (9:118), or none to be found (9:57, 42:47) → مَلْجَأ.

عَاذَ to seek refuge

10× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ʿawḏ is taking refuge with another and clinging to him. With the verb stand the one sought (bi-) and the thing feared (min, or ‘lest …’). In the Quran it is almost always refuge with Allah; the one exception is 72:6, where men sought protection with jinn — and it only increased them in revolt.

اِعْتَصَمَ to hold fast

5× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ʿaṣm is holding (imsāk), and iʿtiṣām is holding on to something, clinging. It is not fleeing from a danger but a lasting hold on what keeps one safe. In the Quran (all 5 places) it takes bi-: holding fast to Allah or to the rope of Allah.

مَلْجَأ a haven, a place to flee to

3× in the Quran · Noun page →

A place one flees into and shelters in. Ar-Rāġib has no entry of its own for this root, but he explains ʿawḏ itself with its verbal noun (iltiǧāʾ — taking shelter). In the Quran the word is only three times: the hypocrites would turn to a refuge if they found one (9:57); there is none from Allah except in Him (9:118); and on that Day there is none (42:47).
