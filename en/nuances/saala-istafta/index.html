To ask and to seek a ruling

سَأَلَ · اِسْتَفْتَى

What they share

Both verbs mean ‘to ask’; they differ in what is sought: an answer or a thing in general, or the resolution of an unclear question.

How to tell them apart

To ask about anything, or to ask for something (property too) → سَأَلَ; to ask for the answer to an unclear question, a ruling or an interpretation → اِسْتَفْتَى.

سَأَلَ to ask

106× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, to ask for knowledge or to ask for wealth. The most general word for asking: from questions about the new moons to asking about Allah Himself.

اِسْتَفْتَى to seek a ruling, to consult

6× in the Quran · Verb page →

To ask for the answer to an unclear question — a ruling, a dream's interpretation, or an opinion. According to ar-Rāġib, a fatwā is the answer to what is unclear in rulings.
