Nuances

Glorifying and hallowing

سَبَّحَ · قَدَّسَ

What they share

Both words speak of the creatures' response to Allah's exaltedness and purity — as befits Him. In the Quran they stand together in the angels' words (2:30).

How to tell them apart

Exalting Allah above all that does not befit Him — by word, deed and intention, and all creation glorifies Him → سَبَّحَ; divine purification — ar-Rāġib reads ‘nuqaddisu laka’ as purifying things in obedience to Him → قَدَّسَ.

سَبَّحَ to glorify

42× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, tasbīḥ is exalting Allah above all that does not befit Him; at root it is swift passage in worship, and it covers worship in word, deed and intention. He says all things glorify Allah, some by compulsion and some by choice, and that 17:44 means a real glorifying of all in the heavens and the earth, in a way we do not understand.

قَدَّسَ to hallow, to sanctify

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, taqdīs is divine purification, not the removal of visible impurity. ‘Nuqaddisu laka’ (2:30) he reads: we purify things in obedience to You; he also gives another reading (‘it has been said’): we describe You with holiness. The verb itself occurs in the Quran only here (the participle muqaddas is in ‘the holy land’, 5:21).
