Nuances

Strength, might, assault

قُوَّة · بَأْس · بَطْش

What they share

All three words speak of force. They differ in whether it is a power one has, a severity that inflicts harm, or a violent grip by which something is seized.

How to tell them apart

Power, capacity — in the body, in the heart, in helpers, and Allah's power too → قُوَّة; severity and hardship, mostly in inflicting harm and in battle → بَأْس; seizing something with violence, an assault → بَطْش.

قُوَّة strength, power

30× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, quwwa is power, capacity: in the body, in the heart, in helpers from outside, and also Allah's power. John was told to take the Scripture with strength (19:12) — ar-Rāġib: with strength of heart.

بَأْس might, severity

25× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, baʾs is hardship and what is hateful, but unlike buʾs (said more of poverty and war) it is said more of inflicting harm (nikāya). In the Quran it is often Allah's punishment that comes upon a people, and battle itself.

بَطْش assault, violent seizing

5× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, baṭš is seizing something with an assault (ṣawla), as in ‘a striking hand’. In our reading it is the act of seizing, not a power one merely has. Said of Allah, it is His seizing, as befits Him.
