Nuances

To forbid

نَهَى · حَرَّمَ

What they share

Both verbs mean ‘to forbid’; they differ in whether the act of restraining or the declaring of a thing unlawful is in view.

How to tell them apart

To forbid, to hold someone back from a deed — to tell him ‘do not’ → نَهَى; to make a thing ḥarām, unlawful, or to withhold it → حَرَّمَ.

نَهَى to forbid, to prohibit

32× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, restraining from a thing. نَهَى is addressed to a person: ‘do not’. Allah forbids, the Messenger forbids, and people forbid too — good and evil alike.

حَرَّمَ to make unlawful

39× in the Quran · Verb page →

To declare a thing ḥarām, unlawful — a standing ruling, not only a prohibition addressed to a person. According to ar-Rāġib, withholding may also be by Allah's decree in creation: the infant Moses would take no wet-nurse.
