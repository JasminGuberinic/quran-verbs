Nuances

Veil, cover, sheaths and overlay

حِجَاب · غِطَاء · أَكِنَّة · غِشَاوَة

What they share

All four words mean something that screens. They differ in how it screens and what: a barrier between two, a cover laid on top, a sheath in which a thing is enclosed, or an overlay on the eyes.

How to tell them apart

What keeps one from reaching a thing, a barrier between two → حِجَاب; a cover laid over, like a lid — and the heedlessness that is lifted → غِطَاء; sheaths in which a thing is enclosed — always on hearts → أَكِنَّة; an overlay on the eyes and the sight → غِشَاوَة.

حِجَاب veil, barrier

7× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, ḥiǧāb is what keeps one from reaching a thing. On 7:46 he says it is not what blocks the sight, but what keeps the delight of the people of the Garden from reaching the people of the Fire, and their torment from reaching the people of the Garden. And ‘from behind a veil’ (42:51) means: so that the one spoken to does not see Him.

غِطَاء cover

2× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ġiṭāʾ is what is put over a thing, a lid and the like — as ġišāʾ is what is put over a thing like clothing; it is also carried over to ignorance, and he cites 50:22. In the Quran (2 places) both times of the eyes and the sight.

أَكِنَّة sheaths

4× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, kinn is that in which a thing is kept, and kinān is the cover in which a thing is enclosed, plural akinna. In the Quran (all 4 places) the akinna are always upon hearts, or the hearts are in them.

غِشَاوَة overlay

2× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, ġišāwa is that with which a thing is covered. In the Quran (both places) it is on the eyes and the sight, beside the seal on hearing and heart (2:7, 45:23).
