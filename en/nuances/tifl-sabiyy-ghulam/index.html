Nuances

Child, youngster, boy

طِفْل · صَبِيّ · غُلَام

What they share

All three words speak of a young person before maturity. They differ in what they stress: the tenderness of a small child, the age before puberty, or a boy whose moustache has begun to show.

How to tell them apart

A child while still tender, for one or many → طِفْل; one who has not yet reached puberty → صَبِيّ; a boy whose moustache has sprouted, in the Quran most often a son whose coming is announced → غُلَام.

طِفْل child

4× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ṭifl is a child while it is still tender; the word can also stand for several children. In the Quran it comes where Allah describes bringing man out of the womb as a child, and in the ruling on what children must do once they reach puberty.

صَبِيّ youngster (not yet of age)

2× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ṣabiyy is one who has not yet reached puberty. The word stresses age, not tenderness. In the Quran it comes twice, both times of a youngster given by Allah what is not expected at that age: ḥukm to John, and Jesus spoke from the cradle.

غُلَام boy

13× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ġulām is a boy whose moustache has sprouted. In the Quran, though, it is most often said of a son whose coming is announced as good news — to Abraham, Zechariah and Mary — so even before birth.
