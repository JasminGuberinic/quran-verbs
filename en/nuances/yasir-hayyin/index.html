Easy and light

يَسِير · هَيِّن

What they share

Both words mean that something is not hard. They differ in the image they carry: ease as the opposite of hardship (and also something small), or something light, without weight — even what people take lightly.

How to tell them apart

Easy, the opposite of hard; also little, slight — ‘that is easy for Allah’ → يَسِير; light, without weight; also what is counted a trifle — ‘it is light for Me’ → هَيِّن.

يَسِير easy; slight

15× in the Quran · Adjective page →

According to ar-Rāġib, yusr is the opposite of hardship (ʿusr), and yasīr is what is easy; it is also said of something small. In the Quran mostly: ‘that is easy for Allah’ — as befits Him; but also an easy reckoning, and a short while.

هَيِّن light, of no weight

4× in the Quran · Adjective page →

Ar-Rāġib gives this sense (‘the matter was easy for him’) in his entry on humility and humiliation (hawān), and cites 19:9, 30:27 and 24:15 for it. In the Quran twice: ‘it is light for Me’ — Allah's words on the birth of John and of Jesus, as befits Him; and once what people counted a trifle, though with Allah it is great.

Sources

  • ar-Rāġib al-Iṣfahānī, Mufradāt alfāẓ al-Qurʾān — entries يسر, هان
