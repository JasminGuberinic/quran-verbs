Waiting and awaiting

اِنْتَظَرَ · تَرَبَّصَ

What they share

Both words mean waiting for what has not yet come. They differ in whether it is plain expectation, or waiting for something specific to happen, often to someone else.

How to tell them apart

To wait for, to expect what is coming — the general word → اِنْتَظَرَ; to wait for a definite thing to arrive or pass, to lie in wait for something to strike someone → تَرَبَّصَ.

اِنْتَظَرَ to wait, to expect

8× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, intiẓār is waiting, putting off till later. In the Quran it is most often the command to the deniers: ‘Wait, we too are waiting’; the believers of 33:23 also wait — many mufassirun say, for martyrdom in Allah's way.

تَرَبَّصَ to await, to lie in wait

13× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, tarabbuṣ is waiting with regard to a thing: goods, for their price to rise or fall, or a matter, for it to arrive or pass. So divorced women wait out their term, and the deniers and hypocrites wait for something to strike the believers.

Sources

  • ar-Rāġib al-Iṣfahānī, Mufradāt alfāẓ al-Qurʾān — entries نظر, ربص
