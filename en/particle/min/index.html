Particles · preposition

مِنْfrom; of, some of

3226× in the Quran

What it does

A preposition: the noun after it is in the genitive (a kasra at the end: ٱلْأَرْضِ, أَرْضٍ). A pronoun is joined to it as a suffix (مِنْهُ ‘from him’). Before the article it is said مِنَ (مِنَ ٱلنَّاسِ).

See a noun's cases, the genitive after a preposition among them, on: بِعَبْدِهِۦ →

Uses

  1. ‘from’: the starting point
  2. ‘some of’: a part of a whole
  3. ‘of (the kind)’: explaining
  4. Emphasis after a negation

‘from’: the starting point

preposition

The start in space or time (ibtidāʾ al-ġāya), often paired with إِلَى ‘to’.

The noun after it is genitive.

‘some of’: a part of a whole

preposition

It takes a part of a whole (at-tabʿīḍ): ‘some of the people’.

The noun after it is genitive.

‘of (the kind)’: explaining

preposition

It says what something consists of or what kind it is (bayān al-jins): ‘the filth, namely the idols’.

The noun after it is genitive.

Emphasis after a negation

preposition

After a negation, before an indefinite noun, مِنْ strengthens it: ‘no god at all’. Ibn Hišām calls it additional (zāʾida): the sentence would make sense without it, but it makes it total, with no exception.

The noun after it is genitive.
