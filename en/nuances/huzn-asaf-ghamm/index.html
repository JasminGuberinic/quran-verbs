Nuances

Grief

حُزْن · أَسَف · غَمّ

What they share

All three words name pain in the soul; they differ in what is in view.

How to tell them apart

Heavy grief, the opposite of joy → حُزْن; grief mixed with anger → أَسَف; distress that ‘covers’ the heart → غَمّ.

حُزْن grief, sorrow

5× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the root sense is roughness of the ground, and in the soul the roughness grief brings; its opposite is joy (فَرَح).

أَسَف sorrow (with anger)

2× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, grief and anger together, though said of either alone: towards one below it becomes anger, towards one above, grief. He glosses 43:55 فَلَمَّا آسَفُونَا as “when they angered Us” and quotes the saying that Allah does not grieve as we do — as befits Him.

غَمّ distress

6× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the root sense is covering — hence غَمَام, clouds that veil the sun; he glosses ghamm as karb, distress that closes in on one.
