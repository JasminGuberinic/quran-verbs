Making ready and setting right

أَعَدَّ · هَيَّأَ

What they share

Both words mean setting something up for someone beforehand. They differ in what is prepared: a thing kept ready to be at hand when needed, or a state of affairs arranged to be as it should.

How to tell them apart

Making ready, keeping at hand — strength for defence, equipment, and most often the Garden or the punishment Allah has prepared → أَعَدَّ; setting right, letting an affair turn out well — in the Quran only the prayer and hope of the youths of the Cave → هَيَّأَ.

أَعَدَّ to make ready, to prepare

20× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, iʿdād comes from counting: ‘I made this ready for you’ means I made it so that you can count on it and take it when you need it. In the Quran people make ready strength and equipment, and most often it is Allah Who prepares — the Garden or the punishment — as befits Him.

هَيَّأَ to set right, to shape

2× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, hayʾa is the state a thing is in, and muhāyaʾa what people make ready for and agree on; he cites both Quranic places. The word speaks not of a thing kept ready but of a state set right: that someone's affair comes together as it should.
