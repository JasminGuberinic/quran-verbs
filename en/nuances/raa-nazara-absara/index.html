Nuances

To see and to look

رَأَى · نَظَرَ · أَبْصَرَ

What they share

All three words are about sight, but one is the act of looking, one is seeing itself, and one is clear perception — by the eyes and by insight.

How to tell them apart

نَظَرَ is directing the gaze (and the mind) and may stop short of seeing; رَأَى is seeing itself — with the eyes, in a dream or with the mind; أَبْصَرَ is clear perception, often grasping the truth. 7:198 holds the difference in one place: they look (يَنْظُرُونَ) but do not see (لَا يُبْصِرُونَ).

رَأَى to see

267× in the Quran · Verb page →

Seeing as perception. Ar-Rāġib lists seeing by the senses, by supposition and imagination, by reflection and by the intellect — so رَأَى means both ‘to see with the eyes’ and, with two objects, ‘to know’.

نَظَرَ to look; to consider

87× in the Quran · Verb page →

Ar-Rāġib: naẓar is turning the eye and the mind's insight (بَصِيرَة) toward something in order to perceive it, while ruʾya is the seeing itself — so one may look and not see. It also means considering and examining with the mind.

أَبْصَرَ to see clearly, to perceive

29× in the Quran · Verb page →

From baṣar — sight as a power of perception; ar-Rāġib says the same word is used for the eye and for the insight of the heart (baṣīra). In the Quran أَبْصَرَ often points to insight — grasping the truth.
