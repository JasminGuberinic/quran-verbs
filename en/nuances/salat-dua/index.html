Prayer and supplication

صَلَاة · دُعَاء

What they share

Both words speak of turning to Allah. ar-Rāġib reports that many lexicographers take the basic sense of ṣalāh to be supplication itself; in the Sharia it became the name of the prescribed prayer.

How to tell them apart

The prescribed prayer, with its set form and times (and, according to many lexicographers, at root a supplication too) → صَلَاة; supplication, a plea to Allah at any time and in one's own words → دُعَاء.

صَلَاة prayer

83× in the Quran · Noun page →

The prescribed prayer, most often with the verb ‘to establish’ (أَقَامَ) and often beside zakat. In places the Quran keeps its root sense of supplication and blessing.

دُعَاء supplication

22× in the Quran · Noun page →

A plea addressed to Allah (or, by idolaters, to others), in one's own words and at any time. In the Quran the prophets call their own pleas دُعَاء.
