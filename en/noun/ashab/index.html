What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الْأَصْحَابُ

    the companions (drugovi) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الْأَصْحَابَ

    the companions (drugove) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ الْأَصْحَابِ

    from the companions (od drugova) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي الْأَصْحَابِ

    in the companions (u drugovima) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    بَيْتُ الْأَصْحَابِ

    the house of the companions (kuća drugova) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    أَصْحَٰبِهِمْ from the Quran

    of their companions (njihovih drugova) · Bosnian genitive
