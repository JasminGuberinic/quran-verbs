Nuances

Envy and resentment

حَسَدَ · غِلّ

What they share

Both words speak of an ill feeling towards another that one carries within. They differ in their object: envy is tied to a blessing someone else has; resentment is a hidden rancour.

How to tell them apart

To wish that another lose a blessing, and sometimes to work towards it → حَسَدَ; enmity and hidden rancour that has entered the breast → غِلّ.

حَسَدَ to envy

3× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, envy is wishing that another, who deserves it, lose a blessing — and sometimes with that striving to make him lose it. So in the Quran people are envied “because of that which Allah of His bounty hath bestowed upon them” (4:54).

غِلّ resentment, rancour

9× in the Quran

Ar-Rāġib says ġill is enmity, and that one who has it is said to have become a man of ḍiġn, hidden rancour; the root means wrapping oneself in a thing (as in armour) and being in its midst. In the Quran ġill (as resentment) is always in breasts or hearts, and it is what is removed from the people of Paradise.
