Nuances

Time: a while, an age and a span

حِين · دَهْر · أَمَد

What they share

All three words mean a stretch of time. They differ in length and in what they stress: an indefinite time fixed by what it stands with, a long age — at root the age of the world — or a span seen with regard to its end.

How to tell them apart

A while, indefinite — ‘for a while’, ‘at the time when’ → حِين; a long age — at root the duration of the world → دَهْر; a span with a limit, seen with regard to its end → أَمَد.

حِين a while, a time

35× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ḥīn is the time when a thing comes about; its meaning is vague and is fixed by what it is joined to. It comes as a term, a year, an hour or time at large — and, he says, it was explained by what it stood with. In the Quran mostly ‘for a while’ and ‘at the time when …’.

دَهْر age

2× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, dahr is at root the name for the duration of the world from its beginning to its end, and then for any long span — unlike zamān, which is said of short and long alike. In the Quran only twice (76:1, 45:24).

أَمَد span

4× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, amad and abad are close, but abad is time without a bound, while amad is a span whose bound is unknown when it is said alone, and it can also be bounded; amad is said with regard to the end (ġāya). In the Quran 4 times.
