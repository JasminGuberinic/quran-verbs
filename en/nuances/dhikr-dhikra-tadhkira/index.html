Nuances

Remembrance, memorial, admonishment

ذِكْر · ذِكْرَى · تَذْكِرَة

What they share

All three words come from the root ‘to remember, to mention’. They differ in what they stress: remembering and mentioning itself (and the Quran as the Reminder), a stronger, abundant reminding, or that by which a thing is called back to mind.

How to tell them apart

Remembrance — remembering with the heart and mentioning with the tongue; also a name of the Quran → ذِكْر; memorial — ar-Rāġib: an abundance of remembrance, more emphatic than ḏikr → ذِكْرَى; admonishment — that by which a thing is called back to mind, wider than a sign; ar-Rāġib: in 80:11 the Quran → تَذْكِرَة.

ذِكْر remembrance, mention; the Reminder

76× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ḏikr is the soul's capacity to keep the knowledge it gains — like memory (ḥifẓ), except that ḥifẓ is said with regard to keeping it and ḏikr with regard to calling it up; and the presence of a thing in the heart or on the tongue. The Quran is called ‘aḏ-Ḏikr’, and in 43:44 ar-Rāġib explains the word as honour.

ذِكْرَى a memorial, abundant reminding

23× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ḏikrā is an abundance of ḏikr and more emphatic than it. In the Quran it is often ‘a memorial for’ certain people — for those of understanding, for believers — and a few times in the question ‘what good will the reminding be to them’ when it is too late.

تَذْكِرَة an admonishment

9× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, taḏkira is that by which a thing is called back to mind, and it is wider than a proof or a sign. He says that in 80:11 it is the Quran. In the Quran it is mostly the Quran itself or revelation, and also things made into a reminder — fire (56:73), the ark (69:12).
