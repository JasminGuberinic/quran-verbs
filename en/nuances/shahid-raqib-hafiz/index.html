Witness, watcher and keeper

شَهِيد · رَقِيب · حَفِيظ

What they share

All three words speak of one who is over something and knows what happens — of Allah, as befits Him, and of people and angels. They differ in what they stress: presence and testimony, watching and observing, or keeping and taking care.

How to tell them apart

One present who sees, by eye or insight, and so testifies; also the martyr → شَهِيد; one who keeps watch over someone → رَقِيب; a keeper who guards and is answerable for something — ‘I am not a keeper over you’ → حَفِيظ.

شَهِيد witness

56× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, šuhūd is being present with seeing, by eye or by insight, and šahāda is a statement that comes from such knowledge. When Allah is said to be witness over everything, it means, he says, that nothing of them is hidden from Him — as befits Him. The martyr is called šahīd, he says, because the angels are present with him, or because he sees what is prepared for him, or because their souls are present with Allah.

رَقِيب watcher, observer

5× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, raqīb is a guardian who keeps watch; the word is from raqaba, the neck: either because he watches over the neck of the one he guards, or because he raises his own neck to look. In the Quran it is Allah, as befits Him, the angel who records speech, and Shuʿayb waiting with his people for the outcome.

حَفِيظ keeper, guardian

12× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, ḥifẓ is first retaining in memory, then any looking after, tending and keeping. He likens ‘We have not sent you as a keeper over them’ (4:80) to ‘you are not a tyrant over them’ and ‘you are not a guardian (wakīl) over them’. In the Quran it is Allah, as befits Him, Joseph over the storehouses of the land, and the Book that keeps the deeds.
