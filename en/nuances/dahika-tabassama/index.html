Nuances

Laughing and smiling

ضَحِكَ · تَبَسَّمَ

What they share

Both words speak of a face that brightens. The Quran uses تَبَسَّمَ only once, of Solomon, and right beside it ضَاحِكًا, ‘laughing’.

How to tell them apart

To laugh — from joy, from wonder, but also in mockery → ضَحِكَ; to smile, the gentler beginning of laughter → تَبَسَّمَ.

ضَحِكَ to laugh

7× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the face spreads and the teeth show from the soul's joy. The word is used for plain joy, for wonder (the laughter of Abraham's wife, 11:71, which he reads as wonder) and for mockery — in the Quran, the disbelievers' laughter is most often the last.

تَبَسَّمَ to smile

1× in the Quran

A smile. Only once in the Quran: Solomon smiles at the ant's words and at once thanks Allah for His favour. Some mufassirun note that the laughter of the prophets was mostly smiling, without loudness.
