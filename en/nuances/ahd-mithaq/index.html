Nuances

Pledge and covenant

عَهْد · مِيثَاق

What they share

Both words speak of a bond that must be kept. According to ar-Rāġib, عَهْد is guarding and attending to a thing again and again, while مِيثَاق is a pact made firm by oath and pledge.

How to tell them apart

A pledge, a bond to be kept — Allah's and people's → عَهْد; a firm covenant, sealed by oath (with the prophets, the Children of Israel, in marriage) → مِيثَاق.

عَهْد pledge, covenant

29× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, keeping and attending to a thing time after time; so a pledge that must be watched over is called عَهْد. Allah's pledge is what He set in the mind, what He commanded by the Book and the messengers, and what people take upon themselves, like a vow.

مِيثَاق firm covenant, pact

25× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, a pact confirmed by oath and pledge. The Quran uses it for Allah's covenant with the prophets, with the Children of Israel, and for marriage — “a strong pledge” (4:21).
