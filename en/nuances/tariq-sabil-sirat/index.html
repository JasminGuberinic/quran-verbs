Nuances

Path

طَرِيق · سَبِيل · صِرَاط

What they share

All three words mean ‘path’, literally and figuratively; they differ in what is in view.

How to tell them apart

A way beaten by feet, a literal road too → طَرِيق; a passable way, and the way as a means and cause (‘in the way of Allah’) → سَبِيل; the one straight path, in the Quran always singular → صِرَاط.

طَرِيق road, way

4× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, a way beaten by feet (from ṭaraqa — to strike). In the Quran both literal and figurative.

سَبِيل way, path

176× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, a way with ease in it. The Quran's most frequent word for a way; often figurative: the way as a means, cause or possibility (فِي سَبِيلِ اللَّهِ — in the way of Allah).

صِرَاط path

45× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the straight way. In the Quran always singular, mostly “the straight path” (1:6) or Allah's path.
