Nuances

The jinn: jinn, jānn, jinna

جِنّ · جَانّ · جِنَّة

What they share

All three words come from a root meaning ‘to hide from the senses’, and all three refer to the jinn. They differ in form and use: the general name of the kind set against humankind, a form said of their creation from fire, and a collective form that also means ‘madness’.

How to tell them apart

The jinn as a kind, beside humankind (al-ins) — created to worship Allah, they listened to the Quran → جِنّ; jānn — of creation from fire (15:27, 55:15), beside ins in surah 55; ar-Rāġib: a kind of jinn; in 27:10 and 28:31 a snake → جَانّ; the company of the jinn, beside an-nās (114:6, 11:119, 32:13) — the same word also means madness → جِنَّة.

جِنّ the jinn

22× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, the root sense is hiding from the senses: the jinn are beings hidden from our senses. He gives two views: in the wide sense all hidden spiritual beings as against humankind; and by another opinion (‘it has been said’) a particular kind among whom there are both good and evil, as in surah 72. In the Quran the jinn most often stand beside humankind (al-ins).

جَانّ the jānn

7× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, al-ǧānn in 15:27 is ‘a kind of the jinn’. In the Quran this form appears where their creation from fire is spoken of, and beside ins in surah 55. In 27:10 and 28:31, where Moses' staff moves ‘as if it were a jānn’, ar-Rāġib reports that it is said to be a kind of snake.

جِنَّة the company of the jinn; madness

10× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, al-ǧinna is the company of the jinn (114:6, 37:158), and the same word also means madness (34:46) — what comes between the soul and the intellect. In the Quran it stands both of the jinn — beside ‘mankind’ (an-nās) or in what the idolaters claim of them — and of the charge of madness, which the Quran rejects.
