What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    ٱلرِّجَالُ

    the men (muškarci) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    ٱلرِّجَالَ

    the men (muškarce) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ ٱلرِّجَالِ

    from the men (od muškaraca) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي ٱلرِّجَالِ

    in the men (u muškarcima) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    بُيُوتُ ٱلرِّجَالِ

    the houses of the men (kuće muškaraca) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    رِجَالُهُ

    his/its men (njegovi muškarci) · Bosnian nominative

Practice sentences

    شَكَرَ الْمُعَلِّمُ الرِّجَالَ

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    The director spoke with the men.

  • possessor (genitive)

    بُيُوتُ الرِّجَالِ قَرِيبَةٌ

    The men's houses are near.
