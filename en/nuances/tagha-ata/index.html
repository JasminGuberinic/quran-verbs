Nuances

Transgressing and defying

طَغَى · عَتَا

What they share

Both words speak of disobedience that goes too far. They differ in the image: overflowing a bound, or shying away from obedience, refusing it.

How to tell them apart

Going beyond the bound in disobedience — also water that rises past its bound → طَغَى; refusing obedience, shying away from it, mostly ‘from the command of their Lord’ → عَتَا.

طَغَى to transgress, to exceed the bounds

12× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ṭuġyān is exceeding the bound in disobedience; figuratively it is also said of water that passes its bound (69:11). In the Quran it is said of Pharaoh, of man in general, of peoples such as Thamud (91:11) and of the waters of the flood, and transgressing is forbidden in the balance (55:8) and in food (20:81).

عَتَا to defy, to turn away from obedience

5× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ʿutuww is shying away from obedience. In the Quran (all 5 places) it is said of peoples, and in four of the five ‘from’ (ʿan) follows: from the command of their Lord, from what they were forbidden.
