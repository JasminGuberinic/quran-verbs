What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الْوَالِدُ

    the parent (roditelj) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الْوَالِدَ

    the parent (roditelja) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي الْوَالِدِ

    in the parent (u roditelju) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ الْوَالِدِ

    from the parent (od roditelja) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    بَيْتُ الْوَالِدِ

    the house of the parent (kuća roditelja) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    وَالِدُهُ

    his/its parent (njegov roditelj) · Bosnian nominative
