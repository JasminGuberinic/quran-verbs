Nuances

Party, group, faction, following

حِزْب · فَرِيق · طَائِفَة · شِيعَة

What they share

All four words mean a group of people. According to ar-Rāġib, each sees it from another side: its toughness, its separation, its being a part of a whole, and those who strengthen someone and spread out from him.

How to tell them apart

A group with toughness, a camp (the party of Allah, of Satan, the confederates) → حِزْب; a group set apart from others → فَرِيق; a portion of a whole, of people or of things → طَائِفَة; followers who strengthen someone and keep to his way → شِيعَة.

حِزْب party, camp

20× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, a group with toughness (غِلَظ) in it. The plural الْأَحْزَاب are the confederates gathered against the Prophet (33:22); “the party of Allah” (5:56) are, he says, Allah's helpers, and the Quran also speaks of “the devil's party” (58:19).

فَرِيق group (set apart)

33× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, a group separated from others — from the root of parting. So it often comes in pairs and divisions: some this way, others that.

طَائِفَة faction, portion

24× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, of people a group of them, and of a thing a piece of it — a portion of a whole. He reports that some say the word can apply to one person and more.

شِيعَة following, adherents

11× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, the root means spreading and strengthening; شِيعَة are those by whom a man is strengthened and who spread out from him. Hence the adherents of a way, and in the plural (شِيَع) communities split into sects.
