One

A collective noun names a group as a whole: it has no singular, dual or plural of its own. Verbs, adjectives and pronouns agree with it as stated above. For one of them Arabic has words of their own:

What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الرُّمَّانُ

    the pomegranates (narovi) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    ٱلرُّمَّانَ

    the pomegranates (narove) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي الرُّمَّانِ

    in the pomegranates (na narovima) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ الرُّمَّانِ

    from the pomegranates (od narova) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    طَعْمُ الرُّمَّانِ

    the taste of the pomegranates (okus narova) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    رُمَّانُهُ

    his/its pomegranates (njegovi narovi) · Bosnian nominative

Practice sentences

  • subject (nominative)

    الرُّمَّانُ أَحْمَرُ

    Pomegranates are red.

  • object (accusative)

    قَشَّرَتِ الْأُمُّ الرُّمَّانَ

    The mother peeled the pomegranates.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    صَنَعْتُ عَصِيرًا مِنَ الرُّمَّانِ

    I made juice from the pomegranates.

  • possessor (genitive)

    طَعْمُ الرُّمَّانِ حَامِضٌ

    The taste of pomegranates is sour.
