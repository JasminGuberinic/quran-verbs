Nuances

All-Powerful, able, Fully Powerful

قَدِير · قَادِر · مُقْتَدِر

What they share

All three words come from the root of power (qudra). They differ in of whom they are said and how far: a form ar-Rāġib keeps for Allah alone, the participle ‘able to do something’ said also of people, and a form that of Allah means the same as the first, and of people ‘one who strives to gain power’.

How to tell them apart

All-Powerful — the one who does what He wills in the measure of wisdom; ar-Rāġib: only Allah; in the Quran always of Him → قَدِير; able (to do something) — mostly Allah, but also people (10:24) → قَادِر; Fully Powerful — of Allah like qadīr; of people ‘one who acquires power’ → مُقْتَدِر.

قَدِير All-Powerful

45× in the Quran · Adjective page →

According to ar-Rāġib, qadīr is the one who does what He wills exactly in the measure that wisdom requires, no more and no less — and so only Allah may be described so. In the Quran it is indeed always of Allah, most often in ‘He has power over all things’ — as befits Him.

قَادِر able

14× in the Quran · Adjective page →

According to ar-Rāġib, of anyone but Allah one should say ‘able to such-and-such’: human power is always restricted. In the Quran the participle mostly stands with what Allah is able to do — to bring the dead to life, to create the like of them — and of people only in 10:24, where their supposed mastery proves empty (in 68:25 ar-Rāġib takes the word as ‘intending’).

مُقْتَدِر Fully Powerful

4× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, muqtadir is close to qadīr and of Allah means the same — as befits Him; of people it would mean ‘one who takes pains to gain power’. In the Quran it is in all four places of Allah, twice with the punishment of those who deny.
