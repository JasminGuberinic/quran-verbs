Nuances

Ignorance and foolishness

جَاهِل · سَفِيه

What they share

Both words speak of a lack of sound judgement. According to ar-Rāġib, جَهْل is not knowing, wrong belief or acting as one should not, while سَفَه is a lightness of soul from lack of reason.

How to tell them apart

One who does not know, or behaves as one should not (mostly as blame) → جَاهِل; light-minded, without sound judgement — in wealth or in religion → سَفِيه.

جَاهِل ignorant

10× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ignorance is of three kinds: not knowing, believing wrongly, and acting as one should not — so Moses calls mockery ignorance. The word mostly blames, but not always: in 2:273 the ‘ignorant’ is simply one who does not know the poor's state.

سَفِيه foolish, weak-minded

7× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, at root lightness (even of the body), then lightness of soul from lack of reason — in worldly matters (those not to be given wealth, 4:5) and in religion. The hypocrites called the believers سُفَهَاء, and the Quran says they themselves are.
