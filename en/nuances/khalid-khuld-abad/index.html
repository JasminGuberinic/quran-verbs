Nuances

Abiding, immortality and for ever

خَالِد · خُلْد · أَبَدًا

What they share

All three words speak of a staying that does not cease. They differ in what they stress: remaining in the same state, without change or decay; lastingness as the name of what remains; or time without a bound.

How to tell them apart

The one who abides — ‘they will abide therein’ → خَالِد; lastingness as a noun — ‘the Garden of Immortality’, given to no human in this world → خُلْد; ‘for ever / never’ — time without a bound, stressing that the stay has no end → أَبَدًا.

خَالِد abiding

74× in the Quran · Adjective page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ḫulūd is a thing's being spared decay and remaining in the state it is in; the Arabs also called ḫālid what change is slow to reach — for its long stay, not for lasting for ever. Ḫulūd in the Garden, he says, is things remaining as they are, untouched by decay. In the Quran it is almost always ‘they will abide therein’.

خُلْد immortality

6× in the Quran

A noun of the same root. In the Quran (6 places) mostly in a construct naming what lasts: ‘the Garden of Immortality’ (25:15), the ‘abode’ and the ‘punishment’ of immortality in the hereafter (41:28, 10:52, 32:14) — and the lasting life given to no human in this world (21:34). Ar-Rāġib's example for ḫuld (the part of a man that does not change while he lives) is not a Quranic use, so we do not rest on it.

أَبَدًا for ever; never

28× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, abad expresses an extended span of time that is not divided as time is divided: one says ‘the time of such-and-such’, but not ‘the abad of such-and-such’. In the Quran it follows ‘abiding therein’ in 11 places — 8 of the Garden, 3 of the Fire — and so says that the stay has no end; with a negation it means ‘never’ (5:24, 18:35).
