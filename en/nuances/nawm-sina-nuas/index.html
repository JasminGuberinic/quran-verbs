Nuances

Sleep, slumber and drowsiness

نَوْم · سِنَة · نُعَاس · رُقُود

What they share

All four words speak of sleep, from the first drowsiness to deep sleep. ar-Rāġib explains نُعَاس and رُقُود as kinds of نَوْم, and سِنَة as drowsing (غَفْوَة).

How to tell them apart

Sleep, full sleep → نَوْم; drowsiness, the heaviness before sleep → سِنَة; short, light sleep, dozing → نُعَاس; pleasant, short sleep (of the people of the cave, ‘asleep’) → رُقُود.

نَوْم sleep

3× in the Quran · Noun page →

Full sleep. According to ar-Rāġib, sleep is explained in several ways, each right from its own side: as a slackening of the brain's nerves, as Allah taking the soul without death (39:42), and as a ‘light death’. The Quran names it among Allah's signs and as rest.

سِنَة drowsiness

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, the same as وَسَن: drowsing, a lapse of awareness (غَفْلَة). Many mufassirun explain it in 2:255 as the heaviness that comes before sleep — not even that overtakes Allah, let alone sleep.

نُعَاس dozing, light sleep

2× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, a little sleep. Both times in the Quran Allah sends drowsiness on the believers in battle as security and calm (some, he reports, take it here as calm itself).

رُقُود asleep

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, رُقَاد is pleasant, short sleep. The Quran calls the people of the cave رُقُود though they slept long — ar-Rāġib says: measured against death, for they were thought dead, that sleep was short.
