فَـso, then (at once); therefore

3001× in the Quran

What it does

It is written joined to the word after it. As a conjunction it joins with order and immediacy: first one, then at once the other (unlike ثُمَّ, with a gap). It also introduces the answer of a conditional sentence, and when it gives a consequence after a command or a prohibition, the verb after it is subjunctive.

Uses

  1. ‘and then’: order without delay
  2. Carrying the account on
  3. The answer of a condition: ‘then’
  4. ‘so that’: consequence, the verb subjunctive
  5. أَفَ: after the question hamza

‘and then’: order without delay

conjunction · corpus: 517×

The second follows the first at once (at-tartīb wa-t-taʿqīb, Ibn Hišām).

Carrying the account on

resuming conjunction · corpus: 1891×

The corpus marks this way a فَ that opens a new sentence in a run of events, or a conclusion from what was said.

The answer of a condition: ‘then’

answer of a condition · corpus: 350×

It links a condition to its answer when the answer cannot simply follow it (a command, a nominal sentence, سَ/سَوْفَ…).

‘so that’: consequence, the verb subjunctive

conjunction of consequence · corpus: 88×

After a command, a prohibition, a question, a negation or a hope (لَعَلَّ), فَ says what would follow, and the verb after it is subjunctive (fāʾ as-sababiyya). According to Ibn Hišām an implied أَنْ makes it so, not فَ itself as some Kufan grammarians held.

The verb after it is subjunctive.

أَفَ: after the question hamza

conjunction · corpus: 155×

The corpus files so (SUP) a فَ that most often stands after the question hamza: أَفَلَا تَعْقِلُونَ ‘will you not then reason?’. As with أَوَ, according to Sībawayh and most grammarians the conjunction belongs to the sentence after it, and the hamza stands first because a question goes first.
