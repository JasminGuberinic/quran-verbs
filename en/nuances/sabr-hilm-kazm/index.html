Nuances

Patience, forbearance, restraining anger

صَبْر · حَلِيم · كَاظِم

What they share

All three words speak of a person holding himself in check. They differ in what is held and when: the soul kept to what reason and the Law require, a nature that does not let anger stir, or anger already risen that is held in.

How to tell them apart

Keeping the soul to what reason and the Law require, the general word → صَبْر; a nature that restrains the soul so that anger does not stir → حَلِيم; holding in an anger or distress already risen, as breath is held → كَاظِم.

صَبْر patience

15× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, keeping the soul to what reason and the Law require, or away from what they require it to be kept from. It is a general word: in calamity it is patience, in battle courage, in speech keeping a secret — and Allah called all of it patience.

حَلِيم forbearing

15× in the Quran · Adjective page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ḥilm is the soul and nature mastering the stirring of anger; of the promised boy he says the strength of ḥilm was found in him. This suggests a trait rather than a moment's effort (our observation). The Quran calls Abraham and the boy promised to him forbearing, and most often Allah, as befits Him: the mufassirun explain it as His not hastening punishment.

كَاظِم who holds in (anger, distress)

2× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, kaẓm is at root the outlet of breath, and kuẓūm the holding of breath; so kaẓm al-ġayẓ is to hold anger in, not letting it out. The anger is already there — what is held back is its breaking out.
