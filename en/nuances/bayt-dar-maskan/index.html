Nuances

House and home

بَيْت · دَار · مَسْكَن

What they share

All three words name a place one lives in; they differ in the image each root carries.

How to tell them apart

A house, a shelter — originally for the night → بَيْت; a space ringed by a wall, and so a town, a land, this world and the next → دَار; a place of rest, a dwelling → مَسْكَن.

بَيْت house

65× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, at root a person's shelter for the night (from bāta — to spend the night), then any house. In the Quran also the Kaʿba — ‘the House’.

دَار abode, home

48× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, a house named from the wall that encircles it (from dāra — to go round); then a town, a region, this world and the next (الدَّارُ الْآخِرَةُ).

مَسْكَن dwelling

12× in the Quran · Noun page →

From the root s-k-n; according to ar-Rāġib, sukūn is stillness after motion, also used of dwelling: maskan is the place one lives and rests in.
