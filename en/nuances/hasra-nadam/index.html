Nuances

Anguish and regret

حَسْرَة · نَادِم

What they share

Both words mean pain over what is past. According to ar-Rāġib, nadam is grief at a changed opinion about a past matter, and ḥasra is sorrow over what was missed together with regret.

How to tell them apart

Sorrow over what was missed and regret for it, often on the Last Day → حَسْرَة; regret on seeing that a decision was wrong, in the Quran always after the verb aṣbaḥa (‘to become’) → نَادِم.

حَسْرَة anguish, sorrow over what is lost

9× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ḥasra is grief over what one has missed, with remorse for it, as though the ignorance that drove him to the deed were stripped off him, or his strength had failed from grief, or he were powerless to make up what he neglected. The word is from the root of uncovering, laying bare.

نَادِم regretful

5× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, nadam is grief at a changed opinion about something past: one sees that one decided wrongly. It comes five times in the Quran, each time with the verb ‘to wake up as, to become’ (أَصْبَحَ) — regret that comes after the deed.
