Nuances

Idols, stone idols, images

أَصْنَام · أَوْثَان · تَمَاثِيل

What they share

All three words name objects that people shaped or set up, some of which they worshipped besides Allah. They differ in material and in whether the word itself speaks of worship or only of shape.

How to tell them apart

A made figure — of silver, copper or wood — that is worshipped → أَصْنَام; stones that were worshipped → أَوْثَان; what is shaped after the likeness of something, a word that does not itself speak of worship → تَمَاثِيل.

أَصْنَام idols

5× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, صَنَم is a body made of silver, copper or wood which they worshipped, thinking it brought them nearer to Allah. He also reports some sages as saying that anything worshipped besides Allah, even anything that turns one away from Allah, may be called an ‘idol’.

أَوْثَان stone idols (worshipped stones)

3× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, وَثَن is stones that were worshipped. In the Quran it comes three times: twice in Abraham's words to his people (29:17, 29:25), and once in the command to shun the filth of idols (22:30).

تَمَاثِيل images, shaped figures

2× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the verbal noun مُثُول basically means standing upright, and تِمْثَال is a shaped thing; a related form of the root, he says, denotes what is shaped after the likeness of something else. The word itself does not speak of worship: Abraham's people were devoted to images (21:52), while the jinn made images for Solomon (34:13), which are not named there as objects of worship.
