Nuances

Transgressor, wicked, disobedient

فَاسِق · فَاجِر · عَصَى

What they share

All three words mean going out of what Allah requires. Ar-Rāġib derives each from another image: leaving a bound, tearing a veil, resisting with a staff.

How to tell them apart

To go out of the bounds of the Sharia, as a ripe date out of its skin — by small or great sins, though customarily of many → فَاسِق; to tear the veil of religion, open sin → فَاجِر; to disobey, to leave obedience → عَصَى.

فَاسِق transgressor

37× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, one who goes out of the bounds of the Sharia, as a ripe date out of its skin; wider than unbelief. It can be by few sins, but is customarily said when they are many, and mostly of one who accepted the religious rulings and then breaks them.

فَاجِر wicked one

5× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, from fajr, to split widely; fujūr is tearing the veil of religion. The liar is called فَاجِر because lying is part of it. In the Quran the فُجَّار are set against the righteous (أَبْرَار) and the God-fearing.

عَصَى to disobey

27× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, to go out of obedience; at root, to resist with one's staff (عَصًا).
