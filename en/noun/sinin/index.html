What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    السِّنُونَ

    the years (godine) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    السِّنِينَ

    the years (godine) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    بِٱلسِّنِينَ

    with the years (godinama) · Bosnian instrumental

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي ٱلسِّنِينَ

    in the years (u godinama) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    مُرُورُ ٱلسِّنِينَ

    the passing of the years (prolazak godina) · Bosnian genitive

Practice sentences

  • subject (nominative)

    مَرَّتِ السِّنُونَ سَرِيعًا

    The years passed quickly.

  • object (accusative)

    تَذَكَّرَ الْجَدُّ السِّنِينَ الْمَاضِيَةَ

    The grandfather remembered the past years.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    تَغَيَّرَتِ الْمَدِينَةُ فِي السِّنِينَ الْأَخِيرَةِ

    The city changed in the last years.

  • possessor (genitive)

    مُرُورُ السِّنِينَ سَرِيعٌ

    The passing of the years is quick.
