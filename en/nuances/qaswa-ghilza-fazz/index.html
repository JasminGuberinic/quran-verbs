Nuances

Hardness of heart, harshness, rudeness

قَسَا · غَلِيظ · فَظّ

What they share

All three words speak of a lack of softness. They differ in where that hardness lies: in a heart that does not take a reminder, in coarseness and weight, or in an unpleasant nature in dealing with people.

How to tell them apart

A heart that hardens like stone → قَسَا; harshness, the opposite of delicacy — at root of bodies, then of conduct, punishment and covenant → غَلِيظ; an unpleasant, repellent nature → فَظّ.

قَسَا to harden (of the heart)

3× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, قَسْوَة is ‘thickness of the heart’, from the expression for a hard stone. In the Quran the verb is said only of hearts — all three times — hearts that do not take a reminder.

غَلِيظ harsh, heavy, firm

10× in the Quran · Adjective page →

According to ar-Rāġib, غِلْظَة is the opposite of delicacy (رِقَّة); at root it is said of bodies, and figuratively of meanings, like ‘big’ and ‘many’. So in the Quran it goes with punishment (a heavy punishment), with a covenant (a firm covenant) and with conduct towards the enemy in battle (9:123), where, he says, it is harshness.

فَظّ rude, ill-natured

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, فَظّ is a person of disagreeable character; the word is borrowed from the name for the water of the rumen, which is drunk unwillingly, only in the direst need. In the Quran only once, in a supposition that praises the Prophet's gentleness: had he been rude, people would have dispersed from him (3:159).
