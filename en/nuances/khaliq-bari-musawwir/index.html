Nuances

the Creator, the Maker, the Shaper

خَالِق · بَارِئ · مُصَوِّر

What they share

All three names speak of Allah's creating, as befits Him, and all three stand together in 59:24. They differ in what they stress: measuring and creating (the kind that is Allah's alone), bringing creatures into being, and giving each creature the form by which it is told apart.

How to tell them apart

The Creator — creating without a model, which is Allah's alone; the word is also said in ‘the best of creators’ (23:14) → خَالِق; the Maker — ar-Rāġib: a name that belongs to Allah alone; in the Quran 2:54 and 59:24 → بَارِئ; the Shaper — the One who gives the form (ṣūra) by which a creature is told apart; only 59:24 → مُصَوِّر.

خَالِق the Creator

12× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, the root of ḫalq is right measuring, and the word is said of creating without a model and of bringing a thing out of a thing. Creating in the sense of originating without a model belongs to Allah alone — as befits Him. ‘The best of creators’ (23:14) he explains as ‘the best of those who measure’, or as said on the terms of what people claimed.

بَارِئ the Maker

3× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, al-Bāriʾ is a name that belongs only to the description of Allah — as befits Him; he also treats here al-barīya, ‘creation, creatures’, reporting views that it may come from another base. He does not tie the root's basic sense (to get clear of, to recover) explicitly to this name. In the Quran it is in 2:54, in Moses' words, and in 59:24.

مُصَوِّر the Shaper

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, ṣūra is that by which things are told apart from one another: the visible form of a man or an animal, and also what is grasped by the mind — the reason and reflection by which man is singled out. In the Quran the name is only in 59:24, and the verb in ‘He shaped you and made your shapes good’ — as befits Him.
