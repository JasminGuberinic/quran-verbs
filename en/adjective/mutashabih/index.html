How the adjective agrees with its noun

The rules, then this adjective with our nouns:

  • An attribute comes after its noun and agrees with it in everything: gender, number, case and definiteness — بَيْتٌ كَبِيرٌ “a big house”, الْبَيْتُ الْكَبِيرُ “the big house”.
  • A predicate is indefinite and nominative (accusative after كَانَ): الْبَيْتُ كَبِيرٌ “the house is big” — the article alone tells “the house is big” from “the big house”.
  • A plural of people takes the adjective's plural of the same gender: رِجَالٌ كِبَارٌ. A plural of things and animals takes the feminine singular: بُيُوتٌ كَبِيرَةٌ “big houses”.
  • A dual takes the dual of its gender: بَيْتَانِ كَبِيرَانِ “two big houses”.
