Nuances

To swear an oath

أَقْسَمَ · حَلَفَ

What they share

Both verbs mean to swear an oath. What tells them apart (our observation) is who swears in the Quran and what the oath is for.

How to tell them apart

Oath in general — some of Allah's oaths (لَا أُقْسِمُ…) and people's → أَقْسَمَ; an oath that in the Quran almost always goes with the hypocrites' lie or excuse → حَلَفَ.

أَقْسَمَ to swear

20× in the Quran · Verb page →

The general word for an oath. Some of Allah's oaths in the Quran are spoken with it (most use the particle wa-, e.g. وَالْفَجْرِ), and so are people's — believers', witnesses' in the rulings, and disbelievers' ‘strongest oaths’.

حَلَفَ to swear

12× in the Quran · Verb page →

Also ‘to swear’, but in the Quran (our observation) almost always of hypocrites and those who swear to cover a lie or excuse themselves; it is neutral only in the ruling on expiating an oath.
