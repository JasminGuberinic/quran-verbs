Nuances

Evening and late day

عَشِيّ · أَصِيل

What they share

Both words name the latter part of the day, as it goes towards night. In the Quran they almost always stand in a pair with a word for morning: so one says ‘morning and evening’, that is, constantly.

How to tell them apart

The evening as a wider part of the day, for worship and for other events too (horses presented, people exposed to the Fire, provision in Paradise) → عَشِيّ; a word the Quran uses only in the pair ‘early morning and late day’ or ‘mornings and late days’ → أَصِيل.

عَشِيّ evening

11× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, ʿašiyy is the time from the sun's passing noon onwards — in his wording, on until the morning: a wide span of time. In the Quran it goes with glorifying Allah, but also with other events: in the evening horses were presented to Solomon, in the evening Pharaoh's people are exposed to the Fire.

أَصِيل late day

7× in the Quran

Ar-Rāġib explains الآصَال as evenings and says that ʿašiyya is also called aṣīl — he does not set it apart from the evening in time. The difference is in Quranic usage: aṣīl never stands alone, but always in a pair with the early morning (بُكْرَة) or with the mornings (الغُدُوّ).
