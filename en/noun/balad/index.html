What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    ٱلْبَلَدُ

    the country (zemlja) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    ٱلْبَلَدَ

    the country (zemlju) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي ٱلْبَلَدِ

    in the country (u zemlji) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    إِلَى ٱلْبَلَدِ

    to the country (do zemlje) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    أَهْلُ ٱلْبَلَدِ

    the people of the country (stanovnici zemlje) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    بَلَدُهُ

    his/its country (njegova zemlja) · Bosnian nominative
