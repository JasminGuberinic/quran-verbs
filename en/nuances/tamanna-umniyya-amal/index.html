Nuances

Wishing, vain wishes and hope

تَمَنَّى · أُمْنِيَّة · أَمَل

What they share

All three words speak of what a person pictures inside himself and turns towards. They differ in what they are: a wish the soul paints for itself, the picture of such a wish with no reality behind it, or an expectation of good that a person keeps.

How to tell them apart

Wishing, picturing within — ar-Rāġib: mostly from guesswork → تَمَنَّى; vain wish, a picture in the soul with no reality — in the Quran mostly of others' claims → أُمْنِيَّة; hope, expectation — both the kind that distracts (15:3) and the better kind (18:46) → أَمَل.

تَمَنَّى to wish for, to long for

6× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, tamannī is picturing and shaping a thing in the soul; it may come from reflection, but most often from guesswork, so it is mostly the picture of what is not. In the Quran it is heard as a challenge (long for death, if you are truthful) and as a ban on envy (4:32).

أُمْنِيَّة vain wish, fancy

6× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, umniyya is the image formed in the soul from wishing; since a lie is ‘picturing what is not and voicing it’, wishing is like the start of a lie. In the Quran (plural amānī) these are mostly claims without proof that the ayah rejects (2:111, 4:123); for 2:78 ar-Rāġib reports Mujāhid's ‘nothing but lies’ and another view, ‘mere recitation without understanding’.

أَمَل hope, expectation

2× in the Quran · Noun page →

Ar-Rāġib has no entry for this word; this is our observation of Quranic usage. The word is in two places only: in 15:3 it is a hope that distracts and diverts, and in 18:46 good deeds are ‘better in respect of hope’ — so hope itself is not blamed, but what it is set on.
