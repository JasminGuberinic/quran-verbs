Nuances

Early morning and morning

بُكْرَة · غُدُوّ

What they share

Both words name the beginning of the day and in the Quran most often stand in a pair with a word for evening.

How to tell them apart

The first part of the day, from which the sense of hurrying and setting out early is derived → بُكْرَة; the morning and the morning's going-out — a word of the same root as ‘to set out early’ and ‘tomorrow’ → غُدُوّ.

بُكْرَة early morning

7× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, bukra is the first part of the day; since it comes before the rest of the day, the sense of hurrying was derived from it — anyone who hastens in a matter is said to bakara. In the Quran it is mostly paired with the late day or the evening, and once it stands alone: the punishment came “early in the morning” (54:38).

غُدُوّ morning

5× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, ġudwa and ġadāt are from the beginning of the day; the Quran sets ġuduww against the late days (الآصَال). The same root gives the verb ‘to set out early’ and the word ‘tomorrow’. In the Quran ġuduww also names the morning's journey: the wind covered a month's travel for Solomon in one morning.
