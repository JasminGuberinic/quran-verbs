Nuances

Falling: to fall down, to plunge, to drop

خَرَّ · هَوَى · سَقَطَ

What they share

All three words mean falling from a height. They differ in what they stress: a fall that is heard — and falling prostrate, plunging from a height into the depth, or dropping from where a thing was.

How to tell them apart

To fall so that it is heard, and to fall prostrate → خَرَّ; to plunge from a height downward — and to be ruined → هَوَى; to drop from a place — a leaf from a tree, and ‘to fall into temptation’ → سَقَطَ.

خَرَّ to fall down

12× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, خَرَّ is to fall so that a sound (ḫarīr) is heard — like the sound of water, of wind, and of whatever falls from a height. When they ‘fall down prostrate’, he says, the word points to two things together: the falling and the sound of their glorifying — and ‘and hymn the praise of their Lord’ (32:15) shows that this sound was glorifying Allah. In the Quran six of the twelve places are falling in prostration or in tears.

هَوَى to plunge

8× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, huwiyy is falling from a height to what is below (beside hawā — desire — from the same root). In the Quran the verb in the sense of falling is in 20:81 and 22:31 (53:1 is read in different ways), and ahwā ‘He overthrew’ in 53:53 — ar-Rāġib: He lifted it into the air and dropped it.

سَقَطَ to drop

3× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, suqūṭ is a thing's being thrown down from a high place to a lower one, as a man falls from a roof; saqaṭ is also what is of little worth. The phrase ‘it fell into their hands’ (7:149) he explains as regret. In the Quran the basic form is only three times (6:59, 7:149, 9:49).
