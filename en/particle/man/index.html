مَنْwho; the one who; whoever

871× in the Quran

What it does

The word for persons (beings with reason), as مَا is for things. The corpus files it as a rootless noun. It has three roles: relative (‘the one who’), conditional (‘whoever’, putting the verbs in the jussive) and interrogative (‘who?’). It does not change for case itself.

Uses

  1. ‘The one who…’
  2. ‘Whoever…’: condition
  3. ‘Who?’: asking

‘The one who…’

relative pronoun · corpus: 650×

It introduces a clause describing a person; the verb after it keeps its own mood.

‘Whoever…’: condition

condition · corpus: 184×

As a condition it puts the present-tense verbs of the condition and of the answer in the jussive.

The verb after it is jussive.

‘Who?’: asking

question word · corpus: 37×

It asks about a person. A question is often a denial: مَن ذَا ٱلَّذِى يَشْفَعُ “Who is he that intercedeth…?” (2:255) – no one, except by His permission.
