لَـindeed, surely

1001× in the Quran

What it does

لَـ with a fatḥa (lām al-ibtidāʾ and the lām answering an oath) is a different word from لِـ with a kasra: it changes no case or mood, it stresses. It mostly stands on the predicate after إِنَّ (إِنَّ رَبَّكَ لَبِٱلْمِرْصَادِ), in the answer to an oath, in the answer to لَوْ and after the lightened إِنْ.

Uses

  1. With إِنَّ: a double emphasis
  2. Answering an oath: ‘you will surely…’
  3. The answer to لَوْ and لَوْلَا
  4. At the head of a sentence
  5. After the lightened إِنْ

With إِنَّ: a double emphasis

emphasis

The lām of emphasis belongs at the head of the sentence, but when إِنَّ opens it the two words of emphasis are not put side by side, so the lām moves to the predicate, or to the subject when that comes late (al-lām al-muzaḥlaqa). The lām changes no case: the word keeps the case it has in the sentence.

Answering an oath: ‘you will surely…’

emphasis

When Allah swears, or an oath is understood, the verb of the answer takes لَـ, and in the present tense most often also the ـنَّ of emphasis (لَتَرَوُنَّ). The same lām stands in لَقَدْ.

The answer to لَوْ and لَوْلَا

emphasis

A past-tense verb answering لَوْ or لَوْلَا often takes لَـ; sometimes it does not (لَوْ نَشَآءُ جَعَلْنَٰهُ أُجَاجًا, 56:70).

At the head of a sentence

emphasis

Without إِنَّ the lām of emphasis heads a nominal sentence (lām al-ibtidāʾ): ‘surely…’. The subject after it stays nominative.

After the lightened إِنْ

emphasis

After إِنْ lightened from إِنَّ comes لَـ. According to Sībawayh and most grammarians it is the lām of emphasis, which also tells the affirming إِنْ apart from the negating one; al-Fārisī, Ibn Ǧinnī and others take it as a separate lām of distinction (al-lām al-fāriqa); the Kufans say إِنْ is a negation there and لَـ means إِلَّا. See إِنْ.
