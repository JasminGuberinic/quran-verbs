To clench and to hold

قَبَضَ · أَمْسَكَ

What they share

Both verbs mean to take a thing in hand or not let it go, and both are said of withholding. They differ in the movement: one is closing the hand, the other is holding and keeping what is held.

How to tell them apart

To grasp with the whole hand, to draw in, to withhold — and of Allah: He withholds and grants, as befits Him → قَبَضَ; to hold on to a thing and keep it, hold it up so it does not fall, keep back → أَمْسَكَ.

قَبَضَ to clench, to draw in, to withhold

5× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, to grasp with the whole palm; to close the hand on a thing after taking it, or before taking it — which is refraining from it, so ‘to clench the hands’ means not to give. With Allah as subject (2:245) he gives several readings, among them that He takes away at one time and gives at another, as befits Him.

أَمْسَكَ to hold, to keep back

18× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, to hold on to a thing and keep it; so Allah ‘holds’ the sky lest it fall, that is, preserves it, as befits Him. It is said of keeping a wife in marriage, and ‘holding back’ is for him also an image of stinginess.
