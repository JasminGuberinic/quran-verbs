Harm and hurt

ضُرّ · أَذًى

What they share

Both words speak of harm. According to ar-Rāġib, ضُرّ is a bad state of a person — in soul, body or means — while أَذًى is harm that reaches a living being; in 3:111 أَذًى is the little that will reach them, not real harm.

How to tell them apart

Adversity, a bad state — illness, poverty, loss (what Allah removes) → ضُرّ; hurt, annoyance, injury — often from others' words or deeds → أَذًى.

ضُرّ harm, adversity

19× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, a bad state — in the soul (little knowledge and virtue), in the body (a lost limb, a defect) or in one's circumstances (poverty, low standing). In the Quran it is often adversity that ‘touches’ a person, and which Allah removes.

أَذًى hurt, harm, injury

9× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, harm that reaches a living being — in soul, body or consequences, in this world or the next. Often it comes from others: reproach that follows charity, insults aimed at the prophets.
