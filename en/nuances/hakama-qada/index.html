Nuances

Judging and decreeing

حَكَمَ · قَضَى

What they share

Both words mean deciding a matter. According to ar-Rāġib, حُكْم is judging that a thing is so or is not, while قَضَاء is settling a matter, cutting it off, by word or by deed — so قَضَى also means commanding, creating and carrying out.

How to tell them apart

To judge between people, to decide what is right → حَكَمَ; to settle a matter by word or deed: to decree, command, create, carry out → قَضَى.

حَكَمَ to judge

45× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the root means restraining in order to set right (a horse's bridle is حَكَمَة), and judging is deciding that a thing is so or is not. In the Quran it is mostly judging between people, and Allah's judging between them on the Day of Resurrection.

قَضَى to decree; to settle, to complete

59× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, settling a matter, cutting it off — by word or by deed: Allah's command (17:23), Allah's creating and completing the heavens (41:12), people's carrying out their rites (2:200), even the end of a life. Allah's قَضَاء, he says, is narrower than qadar: qadar is the measuring, قَضَاء the final decision.
