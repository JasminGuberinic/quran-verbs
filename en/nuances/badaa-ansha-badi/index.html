Nuances

Beginning, bringing forth, originating

بَدَأَ · أَنْشَأَ · بَدِيع

What they share

All three words speak of coming to be. They differ in what they stress: a beginning that comes before what follows, a coming to be that grows and develops, or creation without any model.

How to tell them apart

Making something first, beginning — Allah begins creation, then repeats it → بَدَأَ; bringing into being and raising, mostly of living things → أَنْشَأَ; the Originator, without model, tool, matter, time or place — Allah alone → بَدِيع.

بَدَأَ to begin

12× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, badʾ is putting a thing before another, making it first; Allah is al-Mubdiʾ al-Muʿīd, the cause of the beginning and of the end. In the Quran it is mostly Allah's beginning of creation, paired with repeating it (يُعِيدُهُ) — as befits Him; but also of people: in 12:76 the search began with the brothers' bags, before the bag of Joseph's brother.

أَنْشَأَ to bring forth, to bring into being and raise

20× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, inšāʾ is bringing a thing into being and raising it, and it is said mostly of living beings; Allah's bringing forth of people and generations he sees as creation proper to Allah alone — as befits Him. So too clouds form in the air and grow bit by bit (13:12).

بَدِيع Originator (without a model)

2× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, ibdāʿ is making a thing without following any model; said of Allah, it is creation without tool, matter, time or place — and that is Allah's alone. In the Quran it comes only twice, both times as بَدِيعُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضِ.
