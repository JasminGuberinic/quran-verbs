Nuances

Creature, livestock, beast

دَابَّة · أَنْعَام · بَهِيمَة

What they share

All three words speak of animals. They differ in how wide the group is: everything that moves on the earth, domestic livestock, or the animal without speech.

How to tell them apart

Every creature that moves on the earth, the widest word → دَابَّة; camels, cattle, sheep and goats — livestock that serves man → أَنْعَام; the animal without speech, in the Quran always in ‘the beasts of livestock’ → بَهِيمَة.

دَابَّة creature (that moves)

18× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the word comes from dabīb, light walking, and is said of every animal, though everyday speech narrows it to the horse. In the Quran it is the widest word: every creature Allah provides for and has created from water.

أَنْعَام livestock

33× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, naʿam is said specifically of camels — so called because camels were the Arabs' greatest blessing (niʿma) — and anʿām is said of camels, cattle, sheep and goats, but only when camels are among them. In the Quran it is the livestock Allah created for man: for food, milk, warmth and riding.

بَهِيمَة beast

3× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, bahīma is any animal without speech — so named for the vagueness (ibhām) of its voice — and in everyday speech it is said of all but beasts of prey and birds. In the Quran (3×) it comes only in bahīmat al-anʿām: livestock as lawful food and sacrifice.
