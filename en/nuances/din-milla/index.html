Nuances

Religion, creed, law

دِين · مِلَّة · شِرْعَة

What they share

All three words speak of the way Allah has prescribed for people. Following ar-Rāġib, they differ in where that way is seen from: from the one who keeps to it in obedience, from what Allah laid down through a prophet, or as the clear track of its laws.

How to tell them apart

Religion as obedience and submission, and that for which one is repaid (the Day of Judgement) → دِين; the creed ascribed to a prophet (Abraham's) or to a community, never ‘Allah's’ → مِلَّة; the law, the clear track of rulings that differs from one community to another → شِرْعَة.

دِين religion; requital

92× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, dīn is said for obedience and for requital, and by extension for the revealed law; it is like milla, but said with regard to obedience and submission to the law. So dīn is both Allah's (‘the religion of Allah’) and a person's.

مِلَّة creed

15× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, milla is like dīn: what Allah prescribed through the prophets; it is ascribed to the prophet on whom it rests, and is hardly ever found ascribed to Allah or to an individual — one does not say ‘Allah's milla’. In the Quran it is mostly ‘the milla of Abraham’; it is also used of a community's creed (14:13) — never of Allah's.

شِرْعَة law

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, šarʿ is the clear, beaten track, transferred to the divine way. In 5:48, he says, the phrase points, among other things, to the part of religion in which laws differ and abrogation enters, while the principles all prophets share stay the same (42:13). He also reports from Ibn ʿAbbās: šir'a is what the Quran brought, and minhāj what the Sunna brought.
