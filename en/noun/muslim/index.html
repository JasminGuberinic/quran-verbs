What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الْمُسْلِمُ

    the Muslim (musliman) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الْمُسْلِمَ

    the Muslim (muslimana) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ الْمُسْلِمِ

    from the Muslim (od muslimana) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي الْمُسْلِمِ

    in the Muslim (u muslimanu) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    بَيْتُ الْمُسْلِمِ

    the house of the Muslim (kuća muslimana) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    مُسْلِمُهُ

    his/its Muslim (njegov musliman) · Bosnian nominative
