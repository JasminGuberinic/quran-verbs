Nuances

Splitting: to split, to be cleft, to part

اِنْشَقَّ · اِنْفَطَرَ · اِنْفَلَقَ

What they share

All three words mean that something splits. They differ in the image: a breach that opens in a thing, a cleft along its length, or a splitting by which the parts move apart from one another.

How to tell them apart

A breach in a thing, splitting in two — the sky, the moon, the earth → اِنْشَقَّ; a cleft along the length — the sky on the Last Day (82:1) → اِنْفَطَرَ; splitting so that the parts stand apart — the sea before Moses → اِنْفَلَقَ.

اِنْشَقَّ to split

5× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, šaqq is the breach that comes about in a thing — ‘I split it in two halves’. In the Quran the sky splits (55:37, 69:16, 84:1), the moon (54:1) and the earth (19:90).

اِنْفَطَرَ to be cleft

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, the root sense of faṭr is splitting lengthwise; fuṭūr (67:3) is a flaw and weakness. Allah's originating of creation (faṭara) is from the same root — but that is not this sense. In the Quran the verb in this form is only in 82:1 (the participle munfaṭir is in 73:18).

اِنْفَلَقَ to part

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, falq is splitting a thing and separating its parts from one another. In the Quran this form is only in 26:63 — the sea parted before Moses.
