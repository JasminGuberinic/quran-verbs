Nuances

Arrogance

اسْتَكْبَرَ · تَكَبَّرَ · مُتَكَبِّر

What they share

All three words are of the root of greatness (k-b-r) and speak of raising oneself up. They differ in whether the greatness is real or assumed, and to whom it is ascribed.

How to tell them apart

To disdain, to grow arrogant before the truth and worship → اسْتَكْبَرَ; to behave arrogantly, to set oneself above others in the land → تَكَبَّرَ; arrogant — blame for people, and as Allah's name: the Supreme → مُتَكَبِّر.

اسْتَكْبَرَ to be arrogant, to disdain

40× in the Quran · Verb page →

To grow so arrogant as to refuse the truth, a messenger or worship. The first arrogance in the Quran is Iblis's.

تَكَبَّرَ to behave arrogantly

2× in the Quran

To set oneself above others, to show a greatness one does not have. Only twice in the Quran, both times in blame.

مُتَكَبِّر arrogant; the Supreme

7× in the Quran · Adjective page →

Of people: arrogant, one who makes himself great. As Allah's name (59:23): the One to whom alone true greatness belongs — according to ar-Rāġib, a greatness that is real, not assumed.
