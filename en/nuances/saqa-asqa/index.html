Giving to drink and providing drink

سَقَى · أَسْقَى

What they share

Both verbs mean to give someone to drink. According to ar-Rāġib, Form IV is the stronger: not only to give a drink, but to make for the other a source he drinks from as he wishes.

How to tell them apart

To give someone a drink, to water (a cup, a flock, a field) → سَقَى; to make water or milk available to someone as a supply — rain, sweet water, the milk of cattle → أَسْقَى.

سَقَى to give to drink, to water

12× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, to give someone what he drinks. In the Quran it is the act of giving a drink: a cupbearer gives his master drink, Moses waters the two women's flock, a field is watered, Abraham says that Allah gives him drink, and the Lord gives the people of Paradise a pure drink.

أَسْقَى to provide with drink

8× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, isqāʾ is stronger than saqy: to make for someone a source he drinks from as he wishes, as one says ‘I gave him a river’. In the Quran it is rain and abundant water, and milk from cattle.
