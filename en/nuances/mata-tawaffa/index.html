To die and to take the soul

مَاتَ · تَوَفَّى

What they share

Both verbs speak of death; they differ in who is at the centre: the one who dies, or the One (and the angels) who takes the soul.

How to tell them apart

To die — the subject is the one who dies → مَاتَ; to take the soul in full — the subject is Allah, the angels or death, and the person is acted upon → تَوَفَّى.

مَاتَ to die

39× in the Quran · Verb page →

To die: the subject is the one who dies. The general word for death, of people and of the Prophet alike — everyone will die.

تَوَفَّى to take the soul (in death and in sleep)

24× in the Quran · Verb page →

From the root of ‘taking a thing in full’; according to ar-Rāġib, death and sleep alike are expressed by it. The subject is Allah, the angels or death; the person is the one whose soul is taken.
