Nuances

Buying, selling, sale, commerce

اشْتَرَى · شَرَى · بَيْع · تِجَارَة

What they share

All four words belong to exchange. They differ in the side of the exchange and in scope: who gives the price and who the goods, a single act of sale or the whole business of using capital for profit.

How to tell them apart

Giving the price and taking the goods, and figuratively: exchanging one thing for another → اشْتَرَى; in the Quran to sell → شَرَى; sale as an act — giving the goods, taking the price → بَيْع; using capital to seek profit → تِجَارَة.

اشْتَرَى to buy, to purchase; to exchange for

21× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the buyer gives the price and takes the goods; but širāʾ and ištirāʾ are also said of anything by which something is obtained. So in the Quran people ‘buy’ error for guidance, and Allah ‘purchases’ the believers' lives — as befits Him. Literal buying is only in 12:21.

شَرَى to sell

4× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, šaraytu more often means ‘I sold’ than ‘I bought’. In the Quran the sense is ‘sell’ in all four places: Joseph is sold, and a believer sells himself seeking Allah's pleasure.

بَيْع sale

7× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, bayʿ is giving the goods and taking the price (though he notes that bayʿ and širāʾ can each be said for the other). In the Quran sale is permitted, is left when the call to the Friday prayer is made, and on the Day of Judgement there is none.

تِجَارَة commerce, trade

9× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, tiǧāra is using capital to seek profit — so, in our reading, not a single act of sale but the whole business. The Quran also uses it for what profits in the Hereafter: ar-Rāġib says the “commerce” of 61:10 is explained by the next ayah, faith in Allah.
