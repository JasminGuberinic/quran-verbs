Nuances

Term and appointed time

أَجَل · مِيقَات · وَقْت

What they share

All three words speak of time set for something. They differ in whether it is a span, an appointment or a moment.

How to tell them apart

A term, a set span — of a life, a nation, a debt, a waiting period → أَجَل; an appointed time of meeting (and place) — Moses' appointment, the Day of Decision → مِيقَات; time, the hour — in the Quran (our observation) only of the Hour and the known day → وَقْت.

أَجَل term, appointed time

52× in the Quran · Noun page →

A term, a set span and its end: a lifetime, a nation's term, a debt's term, a woman's waiting period, even the course of sun and moon “unto an appointed term” (13:2).

مِيقَات appointed time

8× in the Quran · Noun page →

The time (and, ar-Rāġib adds, by extension the place) set for a meeting or event: Moses' appointment with his Lord, the Day of Decision, the new moons as measures of time for people and for hajj.

وَقْت time, appointed hour

3× in the Quran · Noun page →

The moment set for something. In the Quran (our observation) only three times, each time of the Hour, whose time Allah alone knows, or of “the Day of appointed time” (15:38).
