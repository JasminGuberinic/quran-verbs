Nuances

King, Owner, Sovereign

مَلِك · مَالِك · مَلِيك

What they share

All three words come from the same root, which means power and possession. They differ in what they stress: rule over people by command and prohibition, owning and disposing of something, or a sovereign's title that appears in the Quran only once.

How to tell them apart

King — one who rules people by command and prohibition; of human kings and of Allah → مَلِك; owner, master of something — of Allah (of sovereignty, of the Day of Judgement) and of people (owners of cattle) → مَالِك; the Sovereign — only 54:55, of Allah → مَلِيك.

مَلِك king

15× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the malik is the one who governs a community by command and prohibition, and this concerns ruling rational beings: one says ‘king of people’, not ‘king of things’. In the Quran these are human kings, and also Allah — the True King, as befits Him.

مَالِك owner, master

4× in the Quran · Noun page →

The participle ‘one who owns, who disposes’. It is of Allah in 1:4 and 3:26 — Master of the Day of Judgement and Owner of sovereignty, as befits Him — and in 36:71 of people as owners of cattle. Ar-Rāġib ties 1:4 to rule on the Day of Judgement (after 40:16); 1:4 is read both māliki and maliki in the canonical readings.

مَلِيك the Sovereign

1× in the Quran

An intensive form of the same base. It is in the Quran only once, of Allah, beside His name al-Muqtadir: the God-fearing are in the Garden, in a seat of truth with the Sovereign, Fully Powerful, as befits Him. Ar-Rāġib has no separate word on this form.
