A collective noun names a group as a whole: it has no singular, dual or plural of its own. Verbs, adjectives and pronouns agree with it as stated above. For one of them Arabic has words of their own:

What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    ٱلْمَوْجُ

    the waves (valovi) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الْمَوْجَ

    the waves (valove) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي الْمَوْجِ

    in the waves (u valovima) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ الْمَوْجِ

    from the waves (iz valova) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    صَوْتُ الْمَوْجِ

    the sound of the waves (zvuk valova) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    مَوْجُهُ

    his/its waves (njegovi valovi) · Bosnian nominative
