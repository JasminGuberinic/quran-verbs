Nuances

Forsaking, emigrating, withdrawing

هَجَرَ · هَاجَرَ · اِعْتَزَلَ

What they share

All three words speak of separating from people or from something. They differ in what is cut: a bond with someone (by body, tongue or heart), a homeland left for the faith, or a nearness from which a person quietly draws back.

How to tell them apart

Breaking with someone, forsaking him — by body, tongue or heart → هَجَرَ; emigrating, leaving one's home for the faith — everywhere in the Quran → هَاجَرَ; withdrawing, keeping apart from people and what they worship → اِعْتَزَلَ.

هَجَرَ to forsake, to break with

5× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, hajr is a person's parting from another — by body, by tongue or by heart. It is a break in the bond, often with coldness or as a sanction; in 19:46 Abraham's father demands it together with a threat.

هَاجَرَ to emigrate

16× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, muhājara is originally severing ties with another and leaving him; its apparent sense is leaving the land of disbelief for the land of faith, as from Mecca to Medina, and some add the leaving of desires and sins. In the Quran it is always emigration for the faith.

اِعْتَزَلَ to withdraw, to keep apart

7× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, iʿtizāl is keeping away from something, by body or by heart. In the Quran people withdraw from others and from what they worship, from fighting, or from wives during menstruation. To the father's demand for a break (هَجَرَ, 19:46) Abraham answers with a quiet withdrawal and prayer (19:48).
