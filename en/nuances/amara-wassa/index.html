Nuances

To command and to enjoin

أَمَرَ · وَصَّى

What they share

Both verbs mean asking someone to do something; they differ in tone: a command, or a caring charge joined with counsel.

How to tell them apart

To command, to order — good or evil → أَمَرَ; to enjoin, to charge with counsel and care, something to be kept for good → وَصَّى.

أَمَرَ to command

77× in the Quran · Verb page →

To command, to require something to be done. Said of Allah's commands, but also of people and of Satan commanding evil.

وَصَّى to enjoin, to charge

11× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, directing someone to what is to be done, joined with admonition. So وَصَّى stands with what is handed on in trust: the faith a father leaves his sons, kindness to parents.
