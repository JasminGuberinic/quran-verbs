Nuances

Prescribing and ordaining

كَتَبَ · فَرَضَ

What they share

Both words say that something has become binding. They differ in the image they come from: writing that makes a thing fixed, or cutting into, a notch that decides.

How to tell them apart

To write, and so figuratively: to fix, to decree, to prescribe — writing is the end of what is willed, so it confirms it → كَتَبَ; to cut into, and so: to settle an obligation decisively → فَرَضَ.

كَتَبَ to prescribe (at root: to write)

49× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, writing expresses establishing, decreeing, obliging and resolving: a thing is first willed, then said, then written — writing is the end, so it expresses what is to be confirmed.

فَرَضَ to ordain, to make binding

9× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, farḍ is at root cutting into a hard thing. Farḍ is like obliging (īǧāb), but īǧāb is said with regard to a thing's occurring and being fixed, farḍ with regard to the decisiveness of the ruling. ‘Farada ʿalayhi’ is an obligation Allah lays on him; ‘farada lahu’ is what He did not forbid him. People too ‘settle’ things on themselves: the pilgrimage (2:197), a bridal gift (2:237).
