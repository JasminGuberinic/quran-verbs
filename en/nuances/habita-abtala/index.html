Nuances

Becoming worthless and invalidating

حَبِطَ · أَبْطَلَ

What they share

Both words speak of a deed that is no longer worth anything. They differ in form and in what is voided: the first says the deeds themselves have come to nothing, the second that something is made void.

How to tell them apart

Deeds have become worthless — through what spoils them (disbelief, a wrong intention, outweighing bad deeds); in the Quran always of deeds → حَبِطَ; to invalidate a thing, make it void — charity, deeds, magic, falsehood → أَبْطَلَ.

حَبِطَ to become worthless (of deeds)

12× in the Quran · Verb page →

At root, according to ar-Rāġib, when an animal eats so much that its belly swells — and, as the hadith he cites says, it can kill. He gives three kinds: worldly deeds that avail nothing at the Resurrection, deeds of the Hereafter not done for Allah, and good deeds outweighed by bad ones. In the Quran the subject is always deeds.

أَبْطَلَ to invalidate

4× in the Quran · Verb page →

From bāṭil, which according to ar-Rāġib is the opposite of truth (ḥaqq): what has no firmness when examined. Ibṭāl, he says, is spoiling and removing a thing, whether true or false: in the Quran charity is invalidated, but so are magic and falsehood.
