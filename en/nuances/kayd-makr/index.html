Stratagem and scheming

كَيْد · مَكْر

What they share

Both words mean a hidden design against someone. According to ar-Rāġib, كَيْد is a kind of stratagem, and مَكْر is turning another away from what he intends, by a stratagem; either can be blameworthy or praiseworthy, though كَيْد is used more often of the blameworthy.

How to tell them apart

Stratagem, ploy — of women, of a magician, of disbelievers; of Allah, as befits Him, the respite that leads to punishment (7:183) → كَيْد; scheming that turns another from his aim; of Allah, as befits Him, which mufassirun explain as requital of their scheme (27:50) → مَكْر.

كَيْد stratagem, plot

26× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, a kind of stratagem; it can be blameworthy or praiseworthy, but is said more often of the blameworthy. When ascribed to Allah (7:183), he says the correct reading is Allah's granting of respite that leads to punishment; some said it is the punishment itself.

مَكْر scheming

19× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, turning another away from what he intends, by a stratagem; praiseworthy when it seeks a fine deed, blameworthy when it seeks an ugly one. He cites 27:50 for both kinds: their scheme (the blameworthy kind) and Allah's, as befits Him (the praiseworthy kind); mufassirun often explain the latter as requital.
