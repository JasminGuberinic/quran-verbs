Nuances

To reflect and to understand

تَفَكَّرَ · تَدَبَّرَ · عَقَلَ · فَقِهَ

What they share

All four verbs speak of the work of the mind. The Quran uses them when it calls to reflection and when it blames those who do not reflect.

How to tell them apart

To reflect, to let thought run through the signs of creation → تَفَكَّرَ; to ponder the outcome, where a thing leads — in the Quran (our observation) of the Quran and its words → تَدَبَّرَ; to grasp, to comprehend by reason and be guided by it → عَقَلَ; to penetrate the meaning, to understand the hidden from the seen → فَقِهَ.

تَفَكَّرَ to reflect, to give thought

17× in the Quran · Verb page →

To reflect, to let thought turn about a thing. In the Quran most often about the creation of the heavens and earth and Allah's signs in man and nature, but also about stories (7:176) and about the Prophet (34:46).

تَدَبَّرَ to ponder, to reflect upon

4× in the Quran · Verb page →

To ponder the outcome and the end of a thing (from dubur, ‘the back, the end’). In the Quran (our observation) always with the Quran, its ayat or “the Word” (23:68) as object.

عَقَلَ to reason, to understand

49× in the Quran · Verb page →

To grasp by reason and act by it. The Quran never uses the noun ‘reason’, only the verb: reasoning is something done (our observation), often in the question “Have ye then no sense?” (2:44).

فَقِهَ to understand, to comprehend

19× in the Quran · Verb page →

To penetrate the meaning, to understand the hidden from the seen; according to ar-Rāġib narrower than knowledge (ʿilm). In the Quran (our observation) most often negated: ‘they do not understand’.
