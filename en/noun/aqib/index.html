What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    الْعَقِبُ

    the heel (peta) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    الْعَقِبَ

    the heel (petu) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    عَلَى الْعَقِبِ

    on the heel (na peti) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي الْعَقِبِ

    in the heel (u peti) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    أَلَمُ الْعَقِبِ

    the pain of the heel (bol pete) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    عَقِبُهُ

    his/its heel (njegova peta) · Bosnian nominative

Practice sentences

  • subject (nominative)

    عَقِبُ الْقَدَمِ مُتَوَرِّمٌ

    The heel of the foot is swollen.

  • object (accusative)

    جَرَحَ الْحِذَاءُ الْعَقِبَ

    The shoe wounded the heel.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    وَضَعَ الطَّبِيبُ ضِمَادَةً عَلَى الْعَقِبِ

    The doctor put a bandage on the heel.

  • possessor (genitive)

    جِلْدُ الْعَقِبِ جَافٌّ

    The skin of the heel is dry.
