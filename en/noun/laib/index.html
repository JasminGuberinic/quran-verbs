What each case does

Arabic has three cases; Bosnian seven. This is how they match, shown with this noun.

  • Nominative: the subject — who or what does or is something.

    اللَّعِبُ

    the play (igra) · Bosnian nominative

  • Accusative: the object — whom or what.

    اللَّعِبَ

    the play (igru) · Bosnian accusative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    فِي اللَّعِبِ

    in the play (u igri) · Bosnian locative

  • Genitive after a preposition — the preposition chooses the Bosnian case.

    مِنَ اللَّعِبِ

    from the play (od igre) · Bosnian genitive

  • Genitive as the possessor (iḍāfa): “the boy's book”.

    وَقْتُ اللَّعِبِ

    the time of the play (vrijeme igre) · Bosnian genitive

  • A possessive suffix: “his, her, their…”.

    لَعِبُهُ

    his/its play (njegova igra) · Bosnian nominative

Practice sentences

  • subject (nominative)

    اللَّعِبُ مُفِيدٌ لِلْأَطْفَالِ

    Play is useful for the children.

  • object (accusative)

    أَحَبَّ الطِّفْلُ اللَّعِبَ

    The child loved play.

  • after a preposition (genitive)

    تَعِبَ الْأَطْفَالُ مِنَ اللَّعِبِ

    The children were tired from play.

  • possessor (genitive)

    وَقْتُ اللَّعِبِ قَصِيرٌ

    The time of play is short.
