Nuances

Relying and committing

تَوَكَّلَ · فَوَّضَ

What they share

Both words speak of a person putting his affair into Allah's hands. They differ in the image: leaning on someone as on an agent who runs your affair, or handing your affair back to the One it belongs to.

How to tell them apart

Relying on someone as on an agent — in the Quran always on Allah, with ʿalā → تَوَكَّلَ; committing, handing one's affair back to Allah — only 40:44 → فَوَّضَ.

تَوَكَّلَ to rely, to put one's trust

40× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, the base is tawkīl: relying on another and making him your agent; ‘tawakkaltu ʿalayhi’ means ‘I relied on him’. In the Quran (all 40 places) the verb takes ʿalā and it is always reliance on Allah; so Allah is ‘al-Wakīl’ — the One to whom the running of affairs is left, as befits Him.

فَوَّضَ to commit, to hand over (one's affair)

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, ‘ufawwiḍu amrī ilā llāh’ means ‘I return my affair to Him’. It is in the Quran only once, in the words of the believer among Pharaoh's people, when he had given his counsel and stood alone: he hands the affair over to Allah, Who sees the servants.
