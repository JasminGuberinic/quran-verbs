Nuances

To gather

جَمَعَ · حَشَرَ

What they share

Both verbs mean ‘to gather’; they differ in whether mere bringing together or driving a multitude out to one place is in view.

How to tell them apart

To gather, bring together — things, wealth, people → جَمَعَ; to assemble a multitude by bringing it out and driving it to one place — the Day of Judgement too → حَشَرَ.

جَمَعَ to gather, to collect

22× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, bringing a thing together by drawing its parts close. Said of bones, of wealth, of schemes — and of gathering people on the Day of Judgement.

حَشَرَ to assemble, to muster

37× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, to bring a group out of their place and drive them on (to battle and the like), said only of a group. In the Quran most often the mustering on the Day of Judgement; it is also said of a single person (20:124–125).
