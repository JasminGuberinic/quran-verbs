Nuances

Being able, having power, bearing

اِسْتَطَاعَ · قَدَرَ · طَاقَة

What they share

All three words speak of what a person is able to do. They differ in what they stress: having all that the act requires, having power over something, or the strength for what is hard.

How to tell them apart

To be able because one has all the act requires, the means too → اِسْتَطَاعَ; to have power over something, the general word → قَدَرَ; the measure of what can be borne with hardship → طَاقَة.

اِسْتَطَاعَ to be able (to have the means)

42× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, istiṭāʿa is narrower than qudra: it is having all that makes an act feasible — for the careful scholars (al-muḥaqqiqūn), four things: a fitting constitution of the doer, a conception of the act, the material it acts on, and a tool if the act needs one. Hence the Hajj is tied to one “who can find a way thither” (3:97). It is also said of what is hard for lack of practice; then, he says, the obligation may still hold and this is no excuse.

قَدَرَ to have power

25× in the Quran · Verb page →

According to ar-Rāġib, qudra in man is a state by which he can do a certain thing, while of Allah it means no incapacity belongs to Him, as befits Him; so a man is called ‘able to do such and such’, and absolute power is Allah's alone. In the Quran, said of people, the verb often comes in denial: “They have no control of aught of that which they have gained” (2:264); yet 5:34 speaks of gaining power over wrongdoers.

طَاقَة strength (to bear)

2× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, ṭāqa is the measure of what one can do with hardship — from the image of a ring (ṭawq) encircling a thing. So the prayer in 2:286 (لَا طَاقَةَ لَنَا بِهِ) does not ask to be spared only what lies wholly beyond our ability, but what would be hard for us to bear; at times, he says, denying ṭāqa denies power itself.
