Defilement and the unclean

رِجْس · نَجَس

What they share

Both words mean uncleanness. According to ar-Rāġib, رِجْس is a filthy thing, repugnant by nature, by reason or by the Sharia (or by all three), while نَجَس is filth perceived either by the senses or by insight — and it is the second that Allah ascribes to the polytheists.

How to tell them apart

Defilement — a thing to be shunned: wine, gambling, carrion, pork, idols, and unbelief itself → رِجْس; unclean — only once in the Quran, of the polytheists; ar-Rāġib takes it as an uncleanness perceived by insight → نَجَس.

رِجْس defilement, filth

10× in the Quran · Noun page →

According to ar-Rāġib, a filthy, repugnant thing — repugnant by nature, by reason, by the Sharia, or by all three (like carrion). Wine and gambling are defilement by the Sharia, and so is pork; the Quran calls the disbelievers defilement because shirk is, to reason, the ugliest of things.

نَجَس unclean

1× in the Quran

According to ar-Rāġib, uncleanness is of two kinds: one perceived by the senses and one perceived by insight; it is with the second that Allah describes the polytheists. In the Quran only once (9:28).
